## Abstract (draft)

**Background and problem.** In dictionary-driven entity enrichment, the
schema lexicon effectively serves as the extraction model. We report a production
incident in which a category-word lexicon silently collapsed the entity
layer of a 61,137-document scholarly knowledge base: 575 type-level
entities ("method", "energy") carrying ~19.2M links while every
job-level monitor stayed green. **Method.** We diagnose the failure
across three layers (extraction mechanism, lexicon provenance,
monitoring blindness), repair it with a three-source corpus-attested
lexicon (3,702 terms), rebuild the entity layer under production load,
and derive scale-invariant quality monitors. **Results.** An isolated
fixed-code, fixed-corpus experiment that varies only the lexicon
reproduces the collapse signature alone (109±5 entities, top-10
concentration 0.513±0.016, 1.85±0.05 concepts/document) and isolates
the repair effect (692±27, 0.300±0.008, 3.89±0.07; mean±sd over ten
independent corpus resamples); the rebuilt layer carries 1,871
entities, 25.1M links, and a functioning relation layer; distinct
concepts per document rise 15.7→37.8 with document coverage 48.5%→98.5%;
top-10 link concentration falls 0.419→0.345; a 300-link stratified
audit puts link precision at 0.84 (95% CI [0.79, 0.87]; 0.95 on domain
terms, 0.58 on short ambiguous symbols; a second Qwen-series model
agrees on 96% of its parseable subset). **Limitations.** The generic tail survives repair (9% of links
sit on the single ambiguous symbol "be"); retrieval-facing gains are
not attributable to the lexicon alone (entity-channel ablation is
bitwise null; alias expansion +1.5pt, ns); audit and cross-judge are
model-assisted, not human-adjudicated; monitor thresholds are post-hoc
on 13 libraries.

## 1 Introduction

Large scholarly knowledge platforms increasingly generate their subject
schemas with LLM assistance: a batch tool drafts entity types with a
handful of aliases each, a validator checks structural well-formedness,
and the schema is bound to knowledge bases as configuration (our own
batch generator is the primary failure source in this account; LLM ontology
generation is an active research direction — Lippolis et al. 2025).
This is attractive at
scale — our deployment covers 80,855 parsed journal articles
(80,852 active, 7,229,225 text chunks) across two large knowledge bases
and several smaller ones — and it keeps humans out of a loop that would
otherwise require domain lexicographers per discipline.

This paper is about what that design decision does to the entity layer
when the enrichment pipeline extracts mentions by dictionary scan. In
our system, stage-K1 mention extraction is deliberately LLM-free: a
schema's `entity_types` main names and aliases constitute the scan
dictionary. The choice is defensible — dictionary scan is cheap enough
to run over 7.2 million chunks, deterministic, idempotent under re-ingest
— and it performed adequately on the knowledge base whose schema had
been hand-evolved over years. It failed catastrophically, and silently,
on the two knowledge bases whose schemas came out of the batch generator.

The failure signature: a 61,137-document nuclear-electronics knowledge
base (104112E6) accumulated **19.2 million entity links onto 575
canonical entities**; the entities were not domain concepts but type
words — "method", "parameter", "energy". A 13,408-document physics
knowledge base (693372D5) showed the same failure pattern at 2,018 entities.
Meanwhile every operational signal was green: enrichment jobs completed
normally, link volume read as growth, and retrieval endpoints answered.
The entity graph had degenerated into a bipartite "type-to-document"
index while presenting, to every surface-level check, as a healthy
knowledge graph.

We contribute:

1. **A failure mode and its causal confirmation.** A forensic record of
   entity collapse induced by schema-lexicon quality (Section 3),
   elevated from correlation to cause by a controlled lexicon-only
   experiment (Section 6.5): with pipeline code and corpus both fixed,
   swapping the dictionary alone reproduces the collapse signature and
   its repair across ten independent corpus resamples. The observables
   that expose the collapse are the scale-invariant concentration of
   links on the top entities and the top-entity name distribution;
   entity cardinality is a corroborating — and manipulable-in-both-
   directions — secondary signal (Section 7.1).
2. **A corpus-attested lexicon lifecycle, deployed through a full-chain
   rebuild at production scale** (Sections 4–5): 2,284 transplanted
   seed terms, 7,391 corpus-frequency candidates classified by an LLM,
   and bounded per-type expansion — yielding 3,702 real terms for
   nuclear electronics (v2.1) and 1,279 for physics (v1.1), 223 legacy
   conflicts repaired in passing — followed by vertex-and-table-scoped
   teardown and 766,501 re-enqueued jobs, with the four pipeline
   pathologies the rebuild surfaced, each with a measured fix (most
   dramatically a 137× link-throughput recovery once an O(N²) alias
   check, amplified by the richer lexicon itself, was cached).
3. **Gold-free entity-quality observability** (Sections 6–7):
   before/after evidence (cardinality 575→1,871 with a functioning
   relation layer; scoped retrieval trajectory +6.6/+7.3 cov@10 with a
   channel-level attribution bound; a 300-link stratified audit), the
   four monitors — led by top-10 link concentration — calibrated on 13
   production libraries, and a corrected incident log showing that
   dashboard-derived incident narratives themselves require row-level
   verification.

The larger claim we defend: in dictionary-driven pipelines, **the schema
lexicon is not descriptive metadata but operative extraction surface**.
Treating it as configuration-file decoration — easily batch-generated,
rarely audited — is a structural mistake that this incident renders
measurable.

## 2 Background: dictionary-driven enrichment

> 中文批注：本节交代管线设计与其正当性，为"塌缩不是设计错误、是词典
> 质量缺失"的论点铺垫。

Our enrichment pipeline (four stages per document: mentions → links →
relations → graph) extracts mentions in stage K1 by normalized
dictionary scan: every schema alias match over chunk text yields a
`raw_mention` with an entity-type hint; stage K2 links mentions to
canonical entities by normalized form + type; K3 lifts selected
mention pairs into typed relations with an LLM pass; K4 projects the
per-document entity subgraph into the graph store and search-side
entity index. Retrieval consumes all four: an entity channel seeds
personalized PageRank, a co-occurrence channel, and entity-vector
similarity all trace back to linked mentions.

Why no LLM in K1? Cost and determinism. At 7.2M chunks, a per-chunk LLM
extraction pass is a material recurring expense and a re-ingest hazard
(non-determinism breaks idempotent rebuild); a scan is O(corpus) with
zero marginal model cost. The design bets everything on one input: the
dictionary. That bet is invisible in the architecture diagram and total
in its consequences — a point Section 3 makes empirical.

The healthy control in our own deployment: a hydrogeology knowledge
base whose schema was hand-evolved to 3,742 genuine domain terms
(78 documents → 1,608 canonical entities, well-distributed). The
contrast cases: schemas produced by `corpus/gen_subject_schemas.py`,
an LLM-drafting batch tool whose output per entity type is a small set
of *category* aliases (4–8 per type) — a taxonomy of the subject, not a
terminology of it (the distinction between terminological and taxonomic
vocabulary is classical — ISO 1087 terminology work — and the adjacent
question of how to measure knowledge-graph quality is itself an
established literature, Paulheim 2017).

Neither half of our pipeline is novel in isolation. Dictionary- or
gazetteer-driven entity recognition is one of the oldest NER traditions
(surveyed in Nadeau & Sekine 2007), and corpus-statistical term
recognition with linguistic evidence goes back to C-value/NC-value
(Frantzi, Ananiadou & Mima 2000) — our corpus-candidate classification
source (Section 4) is that automatic-term-recognition program reborn
with an LLM in the classifier seat. Likewise, detecting and repairing
degraded knowledge-graph content is an established concern (Paulheim
2017), and production data-validation systems routinely gate model
inputs with schema- and statistics-level checks (Polyzotis et al.
2019). What is missing from that prior art, and what this incident
supplies, is the specific failure locus: when the dictionary *is* the
extraction model, a structurally valid but terminologically hollow
schema passes every existing gate — term-recognition research assumes
the candidate pool is real corpus vocabulary, and data-validation
systems check schema shape and distribution drift, not whether a
lexicon's entries are domain-specific. The monitors we propose
(Section 7.1) close exactly that gap.

## 3 The collapse, forensically

> 中文批注：证据链数字全部来自证据包 §1–§2，可复现 SQL 附证据包 §6。

**The observation.** During an unrelated storage investigation
(2026-10-08), a routine entity-quality sample on 104112E6 returned type
words where domain concepts were expected. The full picture:

| KB | Docs | Entities | Links | Schema provenance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 104112E6 (nuclear electronics) | 61,137 | **575** (discovery) / 503 linked (10-06 backup) | **~19.2M** (discovery) / 4.81M (10-06 backup) | batch-generated v2.0 |
| 693372D5 (physics) | 13,408 | **2,018** | 2.23M (10-06 backup, measured) | batch-generated |
| hydrogeology (reference case) | 78 | 1,608 | 719,515 (2026-10-10) | hand-evolved |

Snapshot provenance: counts marked "10-06 backup" are measured from
the pre-incident cold database backup (mid-enrichment, two days before
discovery); "discovery" figures are the incident-day counts; all are
row-level reproducible from the released evidence pack.

61,137 documents carrying ~19.2M links is ~315 links per document on
average, and ~33,000 links per entity: every document "mentions" the
type vocabulary constantly. The graph is a bipartite type--document index that is
row-level indistinguishable from a populated knowledge graph.

**The incident timeline** (compressed; full narrative in §5):

| Date | Phase | Event |
|---|---|---|
| 10-08 | discover | storage investigation surfaces type-word entities |
| 10-08 | root-cause | three-layer diagnosis; lexicon tool built, run, committed |
| 10-08 | rebuild | teardown + 766,501-event re-enqueue; B95 misattribution window |
| 10-09 | drain | four stages complete; stats recomputed; vectors reprojected |
| 10-09 | extend | two smaller libraries with the same failure pattern treated |
| 10-09/10 | audit | scoped refresh, ablation, link audit, concentration fleet-wide |
| 10-09/10 | manuscript | drafted, revised; retrospective — not pre-registered |

The compression is real and we do not smooth it over: the tooling was
written under incident pressure on day one, and the evaluation
experiments are retrospective audits of a settled production state, not
a pre-registered protocol — a limitation Section 8 keeps visible.

**Layer 1 — mechanism.** K1 scans for schema aliases. When the alias
set for type `method` contains common words ("method", "approach",
"算法", "方法"), every occurrence of those words in 7.2M chunks becomes
a mention, and K2's normalized-form+type linkage folds them onto one
canonical entity per (form, type) pair. The mechanism executed
faithfully on degenerate input.

**Layer 2 — provenance.** The dictionaries were the schemas. The batch
generator, prompted to "draft entity types with aliases for subject X",
produced exactly that: type-level category vocabulary. Nothing in its
validator checks whether aliases are *domain-specific* — structural
validity is the gate, and category words are structurally valid.

**Layer 3 — the reference case.** Hydrogeology's 3,742 hand-evolved
terms over 78 documents yield 1,608 entities. We call it a reference
case, not a controlled comparison: domain, document scale, register,
and lexicon provenance all differ, so it establishes plausibility
(lexicon nature correlates with entity-layer fate), not causation —
which is what the isolated experiment of Section 6.5 is for.

**Why nothing fired.** Job-level observability was all green (jobs
DONE, throughput nominal, error rate zero). Link volume — if anything —
read as *success* (19.2M links!). The observables that expose the
collapse are (a) the concentration of links on the top entities — the
collapsed state measures 0.419 with its top-10 entities being pure
type vocabulary, versus 0.18 for the healthy control (Section 7.1's
monitor (a) gives the measurement and its provenance) — and (b) the name distribution of top-linked entities,
which is pure type vocabulary. Entity cardinality per document — a factor of 2,192 below
the reference case (0.0094 vs 20.6), over three orders of magnitude
— corroborates, but cardinality
is a coarse signal gameable in both directions (Section 8.3's
small-library treatment shows category-word aliases inflating it), which
is why it is the secondary, not the primary, monitor. Neither
observable is provided by standard pipeline dashboards by default. This
blindness generalizes to any dictionary-driven system whose dashboards
count jobs and rows.

## 4 Corpus-Attested Lexicon Construction and Validation

> 中文批注：方法节。工具 `corpus/gen_term_lexicons.py`（提交 b44cc64），
> 幂等可重跑；三源与质量闸的每个数字见证据包 §3。

The repair requirement: produce genuine domain terminology at scale,
without per-subject lexicographers, without hallucinated terms — and
make it a repeatable tool, not a one-off patch. `gen_term_lexicons.py`
implements three sources, all gated:

1. **Seed transplant (2,284 terms).** A healthy near-domain library
   (the RADAI corpus knowledge base, same academic register) donates
   its terms with types. Transplant is the highest-precision source;
   corpus attestation below still applies.
2. **Corpus-candidate classification (7,391 candidates).** High-
   frequency n-grams from the target corpus's own text are proposed —
   concretely, the top 8,000 n-grams (unigrams and bigrams) by chunk
   frequency over a 3,000-chunk-per-KB sample, keeping candidates with
   frequency ≥ 5 after a boilerplate and stop-word filter (section
   vocabulary such as *acknowledgements/approach/using*; closed-class
   English words). An LLM classifies each surviving candidate into a
   schema entity type or rejects it. The corpus floor is a
   corpus-presence gate — attestation means the term occurs in the
   corpus, which is necessary but not sufficient for termhood (the
   "be" case of Section 6.2 is exactly this gap); it prevents
   hallucinated entries, not generic-word admissions.
3. **Per-type expansion (≤60 per type).** For each entity type the LLM
   proposes additional domain terms, given the type description and
   the transplanted seeds as in-context anchors; the same
   corpus-attestation gate applies. The bound keeps any single type
   from flooding.

Generation parameters, for reproducibility: the classifier/expander is
an AWQ 4-bit build of the Qwen3.6-35B-A3B checkpoint (community build
`cyankiwi/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-AWQ-4bit`; we retain the deployment string
verbatim since the endpoint serves it under a historical alias), served
via vLLM behind a round-robin router, temperature 0.2, ≤4,000 output
tokens per call, three retries on empty or non-JSON responses; the
entire three-source run for both schemas is a single idempotent
invocation of `gen_term_lexicons.py`.

Quality gates beyond attestation: *catch-all types* (`term`, `term_cn`,
`model_or_code`, `concept`, `question`) accept no classified noise —
they are the natural sink of classification uncertainty and the main
entry route for taxonomy garbage; a boilerplate filter removes
layout/section vocabulary; cross-type alias conflicts resolve by a
specificity ordering (the more specific type wins); and the schema
validator runs as a final structural gate before any version bump. The
same pass repaired 223 legacy cross-type conflicts in existing schemas.

Output: **nuclear_electronics v2.1 = 3,702 terms; physics v1.1 = 1,279
terms**. 104112E6 rebinds to nuclear_electronics (its corpus is
nuclear-domain journals). Both versions are idempotent re-runnable —
the tool is now part of the schema lifecycle, not an incident artifact.

An honest note on the asymmetry (physics 1,279 vs nuclear 3,702): the
physics corpus is smaller and its seed transplant thinner; v1.1 is a
floor, not a ceiling, and the runbook treats small-physics libraries as
follow-up work (Section 8).

## 5 Rebuild at scale

> 中文批注：工程节，重建即压力测试的叙事。工具
> `corpus/rebuild_graph_enrich.py`（b44cc64）；B90–B94 分别有独立 commit。

Repairing live knowledge bases requires tearing down the collapsed
entity layer without stopping ingestion or retrieval. The rebuild
driver executes per knowledge base: delete graph-store vertices with
edges; delete the four relational tables (mentions, links, relations,
graph-projection queue rows) in bounded chunks; delete the
search-side entity projection by query; then re-enqueue all documents
through the four stages — **766,501 enqueue events** across the two
affected libraries. The count is events, not distinct doc-stages
(74,545 documents × 4 stages = 298,180): the surplus is the repair
window's re-enqueue waves — the silent-empty links reset (75,831), the
post-fix relations/graph zero-effect resets (≈149k), the
watchdog-deletion graph recovery (≈122k), the small-library treatment
(5,772), and re-dispatch of partially processed waves during the gated
window — the job table holds one row per doc-stage with an attempts
counter, so every wave re-uses rows. The rebuild ran against the production fleet (five
multi-worker enrich instances, one links-dedicated consumer per KB)
while normal retrieval served.

The rebuild doubled as the system's first full-corpus stress test of
the *rich-lexicon* configuration, and four pathologies surfaced — each
now a fixed, committed, measured lesson:

- **B90 — unbounded projection join.** Graph projection originally
  joined each document against *whole-corpus* entities and the
  *whole-corpus* link table; at 80k documents and millions of links a
  single projection took 25+ minutes, and 80 concurrent consumers
  saturated a 112-core MySQL (107 cores, load 500). Scoping the join
  to the document's own mentions (an index-ordered path) is
  semantically equivalent — an entity projects through documents that
  mention it — and took projection to seconds, database to 29 cores.
- **B91/B93 — link-insertion deadlocks.** Two deadlock classes in
  link insertion: unordered INSERT-SELECT acquiring gap locks in
  interleaved order (fixed by pinning insertion order to
  `mention_id`), and per-action round-trip inserts (fixed by folding
  all actions into a single UNION-ALL derived-table INSERT-SELECT).
  Deadlock error 1213 went to zero.
- **B94 — the O(N²) the new lexicon amplified.** Alias canonicalization
  checked every acronym pair quadratic in alias count — 95 of 101
  seconds per links task, burning the GIL while 16 threads queued.
  Caching canonical forms by schema signature (lexicon version +
  alias count) took it to 0.05 s. Net link throughput: **8/min →
  1,100/min (137×)** — the collapse-era lexicon was so small it hid
  this bug for months; the *repair* exposed it in an afternoon. The
  137× is a window-level aggregate over concurrent fixes (the deadlock
  repairs land in the same window); the cache's isolated contribution
  is measured at task level — 95 of 101 seconds per links task — and
  the cache key (schema version + alias count) can in principle alias
  two same-size, same-version, different-content lexicon edits;
  version-pinned bumps (v2.0→v2.1) are the mitigation.

The last point deserves emphasis in prose, not just as a bullet: the
collapsed lexicon (575 de-facto terms) masked an O(N²) hot path because
N was tiny. Correcting the lexicon did not merely improve extraction —
it changed the computational regime of the pipeline and surfaced latent
engineering debt. Lexicon quality is load-bearing in the most literal
sense.

The rebuild also supplied its own fifth pathology, and revisiting it
with the registry's own timestamps forced a correction to our incident
log that we report in full. The original record: a single-statement
link writer shipped with a parameter-order bug (the SQL's first
placeholder expected the knowledge-base id, the call bound a term form
there), jobs completed green for hours, and the per-KB link counts read
zero growth until a row-count reconciliation caught it. What a minimal
reproduction on the production engine (MySQL 8.4, STRICT_TRANS_TABLES,
faithful DDL) now establishes is sharper than "rows were swallowed":
under `INSERT IGNORE`, the NULL produced by `UNHEX('alloy')` is
**replaced by the implicit default 0x00×16 and the row is inserted** —
into a ghost bucket that no per-KB query or monitor can see (warnings
1411 and 1048 fire, nothing fails) — and a later correct re-insert for
the same (mention, entity) pair is **silently skipped by the unique
key**, so the orphan actively poisons the repair unless deleted first.
Production carries zero 0x00 rows today, so the recovery path included
that cleanup. A second correction concerns duration: the operations log
recorded "seven hours of zero growth", but the surviving rows' own
`created_at` histogram (Figure 5) shows KB1 inserting at 1.0–2.3M
rows/hour continuously through that nominal window and KB2's entire
link layer landing in a single hour — the zero-growth reading cannot
be reconciled with the timestamps and most plausibly reflects a
monitoring view scoped to the buggy writer's partition rather than the
whole table. We keep both the original log entry and its correction in
the record: incident narratives written from job dashboards are
themselves unverified claims; row-level forensics is the only
authoritative account.

Rebuild progress at this writing (final). All four stages drained across both
knowledge bases: mentions, links, relations, graph projection. The repaired
entity layer, measured: **KB1 1,871 entities carrying 25,097,704 links
(from 575 / ~19.2M collapsed), 44,043 relations with 1.56M evidence rows
(from effectively zero); KB2 2,730 / 5,009,755 / 12,774.** Spot-checked
canonical entities (germanium detector, hpge detector, neutron,
preamplifier, scintillation detector) all project correctly into the graph
store. Retrieval on KB1 answers in 0.6–1.3 s warm with all channels active.

## 6 Before/after

**6.1 Entity layer.** The headline repair, with snapshot provenance
stated (collapsed-era link metrics come from the 2026-10-06 cold
backup, a mid-stream snapshot two days before discovery; terminal
collapsed totals are from the discovery-day count; repaired metrics are
from 2026-10-10):

| Metric (KB1) | Collapsed | Repaired |
|---|---|---|
| documents | 61,137 | 61,137 |
| canonical entities | 575 (terminal) / 503 linked (10-06) | **1,871** |
| entity links | ~19.2M (terminal) / 4.81M (10-06) | **25,097,704** |
| typed relations | ~0 effective | **44,043** + 1.56M evidence rows |
| top-10 link concentration | 0.419 (10-06 snapshot) | **0.345** |
| distinct concepts / document (mean, all-doc basis) | 15.7 (48.5% docs had zero mentions) | **37.8** (98.5% covered) |
| KB2: concepts / document | 29.5 (91% covered) | **61.4** (91% covered) |

Concepts-per-document replaces the raw entity/document ratio as the
cardinality-family metric: its numerator scales with documents, it is
readable by domain experts, and the coverage column (share of documents
with at least one mention) exposes the collapse's most user-visible
symptom — half the library had *no* entity annotations at all. The
concentration drop 0.419→0.345 is honest about its own limits: the
pre-repair figure is a mid-stream snapshot (4.81M links; the terminal
state was 19.2M on the same entities, which can only push concentration
toward the type-word head), and part of the drop comes from the entity
population growing — the name audit below remains the sharper
discriminator.

Absolute link counts *rise* after repair in both libraries (KB1
~19.2M→25.1M; KB2 2.23M→5.0M, same-snapshot backup on both sides of
the comparison): the repaired lexicons carry thousands of real terms
that match more often than the category-word dictionary did. Count was
never the quality signal — distribution (concentration) and names
(top-entity audit) are.

**6.2 Name quality, honestly.** The top-linked entities after repair are
`be` (2.26M links), `alpha`, `gamma`, `beta`, `method` — generic words and
short ambiguous symbols still dominate the link head. The corpus-
attestation gate can only guarantee that a term *occurs*; it cannot
distinguish a high-frequency common word from a domain term. The `be`
case is instructive because it is not even a ranking problem but a
normalization one: the element symbol *Be* and the English auxiliary
verb *be* are distinct in the source text, but K1's scan normalizes
case (ASCII terms match case-insensitively with word boundaries), so
every auxiliary "be" in 7.2M chunks links to the beryllium entity. A
case-sensitive allowlist for short chemical symbols plus an English
stop-word exclusion at admission would remove the bulk of this mass;
`alpha`/`gamma`/`beta` are harder — genuine nuclear-spectrum symbols
that also serve as generic Greek-letter variables — and need
context-type constraints, not stop-word lists. Genuine terminology
(hpge detector, neutron, preamplifier, …) is present and correctly
linked and projected — but the repair relocated the lexicon-quality
problem from "no terminology at all" to "terminology plus a generic
tail." We consider this the honest headline of the incident: repairing
a collapsed lexicon is one battle; lexicon hygiene is a permanent
operational discipline (Section 7.1's monitors exist precisely for
this).

**6.3 Retrieval trajectory, with a channel-level attribution bound.**
On the scoped evaluation slices (same runner, same corpus scope) the
system moved from the collapsed-era run (2026-09-24) to the rebuilt
state (2026-10-09): associative queries cov@10 0.533→**0.599** (+6.6pt,
n=48), single-intent queries 0.603→**0.675** (+7.3pt, n=39) with MRR
−2.0/+5.0pt respectively. The two-week window also carried
retrieval-configuration evolution (document-fusion re-ranking,
bilingual alias expansion, kNN search-depth tuning), so the trajectory
measures system evolution *including* the lexicon repair. To bound the
attribution we ran a single-variable channel ablation under the
identical post-rebuild configuration (in-process planner, same corpus
scope, same questions): a *no-entity* arm with every entity-dependent
channel disabled (co-occurrence, graph, entity-vector, PPR) against the
full configuration. Result: **metrics are bitwise identical on both
families** — cov@10/R@10/MRR unchanged (nea 0.599/0.938/0.735, ner
0.670/0.923/0.770; the only movement anywhere is nea cov@20 −1.0pt,
95% CI [−3.1, 0.0]) — while p50 latency falls from 1.46 s to 0.52 s.
The co-occurrence channel fired on only 6/48 association queries (its
adaptive gate suppresses it when base retrieval already serves
co-mention documents) and changed no outcome.

**Channel-liveness check (the ablation's alternative explanation,
closed).** A bitwise-identical result admits a defensible alternative
reading — that the entity channels are not wired, or that their gates
never open — so we ran a forced-trigger check on the 99-question
natural-phrasing set (template words banned), the distribution the
co-occurrence gate was designed for: there the channel fired on **54/99
queries** (versus 6/48 on the scoped template set), killing it changed
the top-10 on 26 queries, and the paired gold-hit comparison reads
**20 losses / 0 wins / 34 ties** for the off-arm — removing the channel
never helps and hurts on a fifth of the set. The channel is alive, the
gate opens exactly where designed, and the scoped zero-effect is the
gate closing because base retrieval already serves those co-mention
documents — not a dead wire.

**Run-to-run noise floor.** Two same-configuration scoped runs hours
apart (the refresh run and the ablation baseline) differ on exactly one
of 39 single-intent queries (cov@10 0.675 vs 0.670) — a measured 0.5pt
noise floor from nondeterministic channel timing that bounds every
within-window comparison in this paper.

**Alias-expansion ablation.** The one entity-adjacent mechanism that
plausibly carried the trajectory — bilingual alias expansion feeding
BM25 — was ablated on/off under the final configuration: association
cov@10 +1.5pt (CI [−3.9, +0.6], ns) and single-intent +0.1pt, at a p50
latency cost of 1.6→3.5 s. On the benchmark sets the retrieval-window
trajectory is therefore not attributable to the lexicon through this
mechanism either; where alias expansion pays is query distributions
rich in dictionary surfaces (the natural-phrasing set), which the
benchmarks under-represent.

The attribution this forces is precise and we state it against
interest: the +6.6/+7.3pt trajectory is **not** carried by entity
channels casting fusion votes. The entity layer's retrieval-facing
value on these benchmark families runs through the lexical channel's
bilingual alias expansion (entity names as BM25 terms — a mechanism
that needs a real terminology to expand, and which the collapsed
lexicon could not feed) and through the structural surfaces documented
in the companion system manuscript [7] (reranker context,
knowledge-chain evidence for generation); at the fusion layer itself, entity channels are currently
a latency cost with zero measured recall benefit on these sets,
consistent with that paper's six-arm graph-governance nulls. The
lexicon repair was necessary for every entity-consuming surface; the
scoped trajectory number alone cannot be credited to it.

**6.4 Link precision audit (LLM-assisted).** Distributional monitors
say nothing about whether individual links are *correct*. We audited a
seeded, reproducible uniform sample of 100 links from the rebuilt KB1
registry (`ORDER BY RAND(20261009)`): for each link the mention span
was programmatically verified against the chunk text (span integrity
100/100 — offsets are exact), and an LLM judge (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B,
temperature 0) received the ±250-character context with the span
marked and answered a strict two-part rubric: (i) does the span denote
the same concept as the entity's canonical name (different sense,
different concept, or non-term mention ⇒ invalid; debatable ⇒ invalid
+ borderline); (ii) is the canonical name a domain-specific technical
concept or a generic academic word. Results: **overall precision 0.85**
(Wilson 95% CI [0.77, 0.91]); the domain stratum is **25/25 = 1.00**
(CI [0.87, 1.00]) and the generic stratum 60/75 = **0.80** (CI
[0.70, 0.88]). Every adjudication, span, and reason string is released.
The reading is the §6.2 headline quantified: where the lexicon carries
real terminology the dictionary-scan link layer is essentially perfect,
and the residual error mass sits almost entirely in the generic tail.
The audit is LLM-assisted rather than human-adjudicated — a limitation
we keep visible (Section 8) — but its stratum split is exactly what
the monitor design predicts, and the programmatic span check removes
the cheapest class of audit noise.

**Cross-model check (the judge-independence concern, addressed).** The
audit judge and the lexicon classifier are the same model family — a
self-grading risk we take seriously. We re-adjudicated the identical 100
links with a second judge from a different model, checkpoint, and provider
(Qwen3.8-Flash via an external gateway; temperature 0, identical
rubric). Both judges are Qwen-series — vendor-level correlation is not
excluded, and we label this a cross-model check, not an independent
family confirmation.
The second judge failed to emit parseable verdicts on 45/100 — a
flash-tier instruction-following failure we report as-is, not hide.
The agreement figure therefore applies only to the 55-link parseable
subset and cannot be read as confirmation of the full 100; on that
subset, verdict agreement with the primary judge is
**53/55 = 96.4%** (50 both-valid, 3 both-invalid, 2 discordant — both
discordances on the borderline generic word "term"), and the domain
stratum agrees **14/14**. Where an independent model opined, it
confirmed the primary adjudication; residual circularity exposure is
confined to the unparseable subset and to the small domain stratum
(n=25 primary, 14 cross-parsed). A human-adjudicated stratified sample
remains the decisive control and is listed as such in Section 8.

**Stratified 300-link audit (third review round).** A uniform sample
reads the head; the failure modes live in the tail. We re-audited with
a stratified design: entities drawn uniformly within four strata (top-10
by link frequency; medium, ranks 11–100; tail, ranks 101+; short
symbols, canonical names of ≤4 letters), then one link uniformly per
entity — seeded, replayable, 50/100/100/50 links, same rubric and
judge. Span integrity 300/300. Results: **overall precision 0.837**
(Wilson 95% CI [0.791, 0.874]); top-10 **0.920** [0.812, 0.968];
medium **0.850** [0.767, 0.907]; tail **0.910** [0.838, 0.952];
short-symbol **0.580** [0.442, 0.706]; across the specificity axis,
domain 142/149 = 0.953 and generic 109/151 = 0.722. The error mass is
localized, not diffuse: the stratified design confirms the uniform
reading at the head and pins the failure precisely on the
short-ambiguous-symbol class — direct audit backing for the §6.5
governance pricing. Entity-uniform sampling intentionally over-weights
the tail relative to link frequency (that is the point), so the 0.837
figure is a stratum-mixed rate, not directly comparable to a
link-uniform rate without reweighting.

**6.5 The lexicon variable, isolated.** The repair bundled lexicon,
teardown, and fleet changes; this experiment isolates the lexicon alone.
Design: a semantic replica of the K1 scan-and-link semantics (CJK
substring match; ASCII word-boundary + optional plural, case-folded)
and the K2 fold (normalized form + type), with **only the dictionary
swapped**: v2.0 as committed at library creation (182 scan terms), v2.5
(the mid-evolution dictionary, 2,932), v2.1 (the repair, 3,733), plus
two v2.1 governance variants (case-sensitive matching for short ASCII
symbols; an English stop-word block at admission). Corpus frame: **ten
independent, seeded, uniformly random resamples of 2,000 chunks each**
from the library's 5.83M visible chunks (cross-sample disjoint,
deduplicated on redraw). An earlier single run had sampled an ordered
prefix — a bias we replace rather than defend; all metrics below are
deterministic, reported as mean±sd across the ten resamples.

| Arm (fixed code, fixed corpus) | Links | Entities | Top-10 conc. | Concepts/doc | Generic-head share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v2.0 (collapse-era) | 2,969±140 | 109±5 | **0.513±0.016** | 1.85±0.05 | 0.146±0.012 |
| v2.5 (mid-evolution) | 4,823±147 | 421±25 | 0.322±0.009 | 2.51±0.05 | 0.090±0.006 |
| v2.1 (repair) | 10,042±251 | 692±27 | 0.300±0.008 | **3.89±0.07** | **0.058±0.004** |
| v2.1 + short-symbol case-sensitivity | 8,758±228 | 665±28 | 0.265±0.012 | 3.54±0.07 | 0.066±0.004 |
| v2.1 + stop-word block | 9,226±251 | 691±27 | **0.252±0.011** | 3.67±0.07 | 0.063±0.004 |

Three findings. **(1) The collapse signature is reproduced by the
lexicon alone**: 109±5 entities at concentration 0.513±0.016 and
1.85±0.05 concepts per document from the v2.0 dictionary — same code,
same corpus, no other variable. **(2) The repair effect is isolated**:
v2.0→v2.1 multiplies link-bearing entities ×6.3, concepts per document
×2.1, and halves concentration and generic-head mass (0.146→0.058).
The production collapsed concentration (C=0.419 at the 10-06 backup)
sits between the v2.0 (0.513) and v2.5 (0.322) counterfactuals —
consistent with the operational
dictionary having evolved mid-enrichment after the bulk of extraction
had already run. **(3) The `be` class is treatable at known cost**:
case-sensitive short-symbol matching removes the single largest entity
and takes concentration to 0.265±0.012, at the price of a wider
case-fold recall loss (links −12.8%); a stop-word block is the gentler
instrument (−8.1% links, C 0.252±0.011). Judged precision is
near-tautological for dictionary
self-matches (the entity name *is* the surface form), so we lead with
the structural metrics and report precision as a bounded secondary —
the same caveat the §6.4 audit carries. The stratified §6.4 audit now
supplies the missing external anchor: short-ambiguous-symbol links —
the exact class this arm removes — measure 0.580 [0.44, 0.71], so the
fold-recall price buys real precision, not cosmetic concentration.

**Why 692 entities here vs 1,871 in production (Section 6.1)?** The
isolated frame counts only entities induced by scanning 2,000 chunks
(0.034% of the library) under replica folding: rare terms need corpus
mass to be attested, and the production layer additionally carries
entities from the full corpus and historical merging. The causal claim
rests on within-frame comparisons, never on this cross-frame count.

Methodological caveats: the scanner is a semantic replica, not the
service path; each sample is 2,000 chunks (ten resamples bound the
variance but not the coverage of a 5.83M-chunk library); and the arms
are committed-dictionary snapshots, not independently regenerated
component ablations (the provenance decomposition below approximates
that axis lexically).

**Provenance decomposition (component contribution, approximated
lexically).** The v2.1 file carries no per-term source tags, so we
decompose its isolated link mass by which earlier committed lexicon
first contained each matched term: v2.0-inherited terms contribute
22.0% of links (70 entities), mid-evolution terms 16.0% (232), and
terms first introduced by the three-source repair **61.9% of links
(384 of 686 entities)** — the repair's additions carry three-fifths of
the rebuilt link mass (shares stable against the retired ordered-prefix
frame, which read 21.4/15.3/63.2). A true component ablation
(independently regenerating single-source dictionaries) remains future
work; this set-algebra attribution is its lexical lower bound.

## 7 Discussion

> 7.1/7.2 现在可写（不依赖 §6 数字）；7.3 待 §6。

**7.1 Observability for lexicon quality.** Four cheap monitors would
have caught both incidents at first symptom, in the spirit of
production data-validation systems (Polyzotis et al. 2019):

- (a) **Top-10 link concentration (primary, scale-invariant).** The
  fraction of all entity links held by the ten most-linked entities.
  As a share it is comparable across corpus sizes and disciplines —
  exactly what raw cardinality is not. Measured across the thirteen
  libraries of our deployment, the metric spans 0.16–0.77: the healthy
  and repaired large libraries run at 0.16–0.19 (rebuilt physics KB2
  0.162, hydrogeology control 0.184); the rebuilt nuclear library sits
  at 0.345 with its generic tail; and the two small treated libraries
  **currently** read 0.398 (1,324 docs, 629 entities) and 0.768 (119
  docs, 153 entities) — values we report as they are, not smoothed:
  the smallest library also illustrates the metric's floor effect
  (with O(10²) entities, ten of them can hold most links under an
  ordinary Zipfian distribution alone), so the band should be read
  jointly with library size. The collapsed state itself is now
  measured, not gestured at: recovering the pre-incident cold database
  backup (2026-10-06, two days before discovery, with the collapsed
  enrichment still writing — 4.8M links on KB1 at that moment versus
  19.2M at discovery) gives **0.419** for collapsed KB1 (top-10
  entities: *time, model, method, energy, temperature, field, term,
  tau, phase, material* — 431k links on "time" alone) and **0.290**
  for collapsed KB2. The repair moves KB1 0.419→0.345 and KB2
  0.290→0.162 — direction correct, and the residual gap is the generic
  tail. One honesty note: the metric partly improves simply because the
  entity population grows (503→1,871 link-bearing entities); the name
  audit (c) remains the sharper discriminator of the collapse itself.
  We propose a band alarm (>0.35 investigation, >0.5 escalation) with
  an explicit caveat: the thresholds are calibrated post-hoc on this
  deployment's thirteen libraries, not on an external population —
  they are a starting point for adopters, not a validated norm —
  prospective validation (false-positive/false-negative rates and
  detection delay on live streams) is future work, and libraries below
  a few hundred entities warrant size-stratified bands. The
  thirteen-library calibration, summarized (the collapsed row is the
  pre-repair backup, not a live point):

| Group | Libs | Entities | C10 range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Collapsed (10-06 backup) | 1 | 503 | 0.419 |
| Hand-curated control | 1 | 1,239 | 0.184 |
| Other production, ≥500 entities | 4 | 874–2,482 | 0.259–0.287 |
| Rebuilt here, large | 2 | 1,871 / 2,726 | 0.162–0.345 |
| Rebuilt here, small | 2 | 153–629 | 0.398–0.768 |
| Other production, <500 entities | 4 | 5–424 | 0.395–1.000 |

- (b) *Entity cardinality ratio (coarse secondary).* Canonical
  entities per ingested document, alarmed against a per-domain band
  (our single hand-evolved control suggests O(10–20) for scholarly
  corpora — a one-library heuristic offered as illustration, not a
  calibrated norm; collapse ran at 0.009 — a factor 2,192 below the
  reference case, and the ratio's sublinear numerator makes it
  scale-sensitive, which is why the per-document concept counts of
  Section 6.1 are the better-behaved cardinality-family metric).
  Cardinality alone is
  gameable in the other direction — the small-KB treatment showed
  category-word aliases *inflating* the count (Section 8.3) — which is
  why it backs up (a) rather than leading.
- (c) *Top-entity name audit.* The top-K entities by link count should
  be domain concepts; a manual or LLM spot check of that list is a
  five-minute quarterly control, and the §6.4 rubric is a ready-made
  protocol for a deeper sample.
- (d) *Target-table growth reconciliation.* Completed-job counts
  reconciled against downstream row growth, mandatory wherever a bulk
  writer is guarded by INSERT IGNORE (the B95 silent-swallow incident
  of Section 5 was invisible to job metrics for seven hours and
  exposed by exactly this reconciliation).

None of the four requires ground truth. The small-library treatment
(Section 8.3) doubles as the monitors' out-of-sample validation: after
the same repair playbook, entity counts contracted (870→629, 209→153 —
the cardinality signal normalizes) while the concentration monitor
still flags both libraries — one above the investigation threshold
(0.398 > 0.35), one above the escalation threshold (0.768 > 0.5) —
correctly, since their tails remain generic — demonstrating that the two monitors
separate "cardinality repaired" from "distribution still skewed", which
cardinality alone cannot.

**7.2 LLM extraction vs. dictionary scan.** The incident is not an
argument for LLM-everywhere. It is an argument for honoring the input
contract of cheap deterministic components: if extraction is a scan,
the dictionary is the model — and it deserves the same evaluation
discipline (held-out attestation, distributional checks) as a trained
model. Batch-generating that dictionary with an LLM and shipping it on
structural validation alone is the actual failure mode.

**7.3 Schema tooling implications.** The batch schema generator produced
category taxonomy where a terminology was needed; the repair gave the
schema a real lexicon (3,702 terms) and folded lexicon generation into
the schema lifecycle as an idempotent tool. What remains open — and what
the rebuilt top-entity distribution makes plain — is that *admission*
criteria (corpus attestation) are necessary but not sufficient: a lexicon
also needs *ranking* criteria that demote high-frequency generic words
and short ambiguous symbols (IDF-style corpus contrast, bilingual alias
consistency, length/type priors). The observation that the collapsed
state and the repaired state differ by a factor of 3.25 in entity
cardinality — while both pass structural validation — is the strongest
argument we have for treating lexicon quality as first-class schema
infrastructure with its own acceptance gates, not as a one-time repair.

## 8 Limitations and honesty

1. **The generic tail survives the repair** (Section 6.2): top-linked
   entities remain short generic symbols; admission gates without
   ranking gates move the problem, they do not remove it.
2. **Retrieval attribution is bounded, not eliminated** (Section 6.3):
   the channel ablation shows the trajectory does not run through
   entity-channel fusion votes, but the full decomposition (lexicon
   old/new × retrieval configuration on/off) would require re-binding
   the collapsed schema and re-running the collapsed-era stack — a
   re-collapse of a production library we did not authorize for
   attribution's sake.
3. **Coverage**: two knowledge bases rebuilt (61,137 + 13,408 documents)
   and, after the two main libraries drained (2026-10-09), the two smaller affected libraries
   (1,324 + 119 documents) with the same playbook — where entity
   cardinality *contracted* (870→629, 209→153, with 916k/26k links and
   4.6k new typed relations): the category-word aliases had inflated the
   count, so cardinality is not the quality metric, name distribution is.
   The bilingual alias layer showed isolated anomalies (an entity whose
   mentions vanished between extraction generations) that we document
   but do not fully explain.
4. **Incident-response cost**: six secondary incidents across the
   repair window (one silent INSERT-IGNORE swallow of 7 hours, three
   half-open-connection wedges, a watchdog that mistook completion for
   failure and deleted the freshly rebuilt graph, one missing-index
   class) each extended the repair; we report them as findings
   (Section 5), not hidden costs.
5. **No human-adjudicated ground truth** for entity correctness: the
   §6.4 audit is LLM-assisted (with programmatic span verification);
   the stratified 300-link sample replaces the uniform 100 but
   entity-uniform sampling intentionally over-weights the tail relative
   to link frequency, and the cross-judge is same-vendor-family.
   A human-adjudicated stratified sample is the
   natural next evaluation step.
