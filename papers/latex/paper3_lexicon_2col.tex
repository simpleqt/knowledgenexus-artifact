\title{When Schema Lexicons Fail: Diagnosing and Repairing Entity-Layer
Collapse in a Production Knowledge Base}

\begin{abstract}
In dictionary-driven entity enrichment, the schema lexicon effectively
serves as the extraction model. We report a production incident in
which a category-word lexicon silently collapsed the entity layer of a
61{,}137-document scholarly knowledge base: 575 type-level entities
(``method'', ``energy'') carrying ${\sim}$19.2M links while every
job-level monitor stayed green. We diagnose the failure across three
layers (extraction mechanism, lexicon provenance, monitoring
blindness), repair it with a corpus-attested lexicon built by LLM
classification of corpus candidates under presence gating (deployed
as a three-source recipe) (3{,}702 terms), rebuild the entity layer under production
load, and derive gold-free monitors led by a concentration diagnostic
invariant to uniform rescaling of link counts. A controlled experiment
that fixes code and corpus and varies \emph{only the lexicon}
reproduces the collapse signature alone (109$\pm$5 entities, top-10
concentration 0.513$\pm$0.016) and isolates the repair effect
(692$\pm$27, 0.300$\pm$0.008; ten resamples); the rebuilt layer
carries 1{,}871 entities and 25.1M links; distinct concepts per
document rise 15.7$\to$37.8 with annotated-document coverage
51.5\%$\to$98.5\%; a risk-stratified 300-link audit reads 0.84 overall.
Matched spans are equally ``valid'' under old and new dictionaries
(0.94 vs.\ 0.93)---the collapse is a vocabulary-and-mass failure
invisible to per-link audits---and a composite four-leg monitor rule
reaches zero misses and zero false alarms on the degradation archive.
A three-domain replay on public corpora shows the failure form and the
repair travel: batch-schema lexicons reproduce the collapse signature
out of domain. Limitations are stated up front: the generic tail
survives repair (9\% of links sit on the symbol ``be''),
retrieval-facing gains are not attributable to the lexicon alone,
annotation is model-based with single-author human verification of a
197-term stratified sample (confirm 0.934), and alarm bands are
post-hoc candidate thresholds.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Scholarly knowledge platforms increasingly generate subject schemas with
LLM assistance: a batch tool drafts entity types with a handful of aliases
each, a validator checks structural well-formedness, and the schema is
bound to knowledge bases as configuration~\cite{llmonto}. When the
enrichment pipeline then extracts mentions by \emph{dictionary scan}, the
schema's alias list becomes the extraction surface --- the lexicon is not
descriptive metadata but operative infrastructure.

The failure we report: a 61{,}137-document nuclear-electronics knowledge
base accumulated ${\sim}$19.2M entity links onto \textbf{575 canonical
entities}, every one a bare type word (``method'', ``parameter'',
``energy''); a 13{,}408-document physics library showed the same failure
pattern at 2{,}018 entities. Every operational signal stayed green ---
jobs done, throughput nominal, link volume reading as growth. The graph
had degenerated into a bipartite type--document index that was
row-level indistinguishable from a populated knowledge graph.

We contribute three things. \textbf{(1)~A failure mode and its controlled
reproduction}: when extraction is a dictionary scan, a structurally valid
schema lexicon acts as the extraction model and can silently collapse the
entity layer --- established forensically (\S\ref{sec:collapse}) and then
reproduced by a controlled experiment that fixes code and corpus
and varies only the lexicon across ten disjoint corpus resamples, with a
frozen-sample parity check tying the replica's matching semantics to the
production scanner (\S\ref{sec:iso}). \textbf{(2)~A corpus-attested lexicon
lifecycle whose decisive mechanism is LLM classification of corpus
candidates under corpus-presence gating} (production recipe: seed
transplant, classified candidates, optional bounded expansion) that
repaired both schemas without manual lexicography and survived a full
production rebuild (\S\ref{sec:lexicon}--\S\ref{sec:rebuild}).
\textbf{(3)~Gold-free entity-quality observability}: concentration-led
monitors with candidate thresholds read from 13 production libraries,
reported with their known false-positive and false-negative classes
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}),
plus a corrected incident log demonstrating that dashboard-derived
incident narratives themselves require row-level verification.

\section{Background and Prior Art}\label{sec:related}

Dictionary- or gazetteer-driven entity recognition is one of the oldest
NER traditions~\cite{nerSurvey}; corpus-statistical term recognition
goes back to C-value/NC-value~\cite{cvalue} --- our corpus-candidate
source is that program reborn with an LLM classifier, and recent work
extracts terminology directly with LLM-plus-retrieval
pipelines~\cite{llmterm}. Automatic schema and KG construction has
meanwhile advanced to dynamic schema induction over corpora
(AutoSchemaKG~\cite{autoschemakg}), incremental schema evolution
(DIAL-KG~\cite{dialkg}), and joint evaluation of extraction quality,
schema adherence, and stability~\cite{schemakgeval}. Our subject is
complementary and upstream of a different risk: in \emph{dictionary-scan
deployment}, where the schema's alias list is the extraction surface,
we show that schema-legality and even per-link mention validity can
coexist with asset-level semantic collapse --- a failure locus those
methods do not measure, and one that our diagnostics (distribution and
name monitors, coverage accounting) target directly. We do not claim a
better schema-induction or term-extraction \emph{algorithm}; the
contribution is the reproducible failure, the separation of local
validity from coverage structure, and the repair lifecycle.
Knowledge-graph
refinement~\cite{paulheim} and production data
validation~\cite{tdd,deequ} address adjacent problems; term-extraction
evaluation is annotation-guided and domain-stratified
(TermEval/ACTER~\cite{termeval}), and vocabulary drift is measurable
even absent failure~\cite{hamilton16}. What this incident exposes is a specific failure locus: when the
dictionary effectively \emph{is} the extraction model, a structurally
valid but terminologically hollow schema passed every gate \emph{this
pipeline had in place} (schema-shape validation, ingestion checks,
job-level monitors). Distribution-statistics monitoring~\cite{tdd} is
the right tradition, but the statistic must be aimed at the lexicon's
terminological content; none of our gates asked whether a lexicon's
entries are domain-specific terms.

Our pipeline extracts mentions in stage K1 by normalized dictionary scan
(CJK substring; ASCII word-boundary with optional plural, case-folded);
K2 links mentions to canonical entities by normalized form + type; K3
adjudicates typed relations with an LLM; K4 projects to the graph store.
The healthy reference case in the same deployment --- hydrogeology, 78
documents, a hand-evolved 3{,}742-term lexicon, 1{,}608 entities ---
establishes plausibility, not causation: domain, scale, and provenance
all differ.

\textbf{Mechanism.} When the alias set for type \emph{method} contains
common words, every occurrence in 7.2M chunks becomes a mention, and K2
folds them onto one entity per (form, type). The mechanism executed
faithfully on degenerate input; the dictionaries were the schemas ---
the batch generator produced a category taxonomy where a terminology was
needed~\cite{iso1087}.

\textbf{Timeline} (compressed): 10-08 discovery $\to$ root cause,
lexicon tool built/run/committed, teardown $+$ 766{,}501-event
re-enqueue; 10-08 night: silent mis-attribution window (below); 10-09
drain completes, statistics recomputed, two smaller libraries with the
same failure pattern treated; 10-09/10: retrospective audits (scoped
refresh, channel ablation, link audit, fleet-wide concentration) and
manuscript. The compression is real; the evaluation is retrospective,
not pre-registered.

\textbf{Why nothing fired.} Job-level observability was all green; link
volume read as success. The observables that expose the collapse are the
concentration of links on the top entities (collapsed: 0.419, measured
from the pre-incident backup, versus 0.18 for the reference case) and
the name distribution of those entities (pure type vocabulary). Entity
cardinality per document --- a factor of 2{,}192 below the reference
case (0.0094 vs 20.6) --- corroborates, but is gameable in both
directions, so it is the secondary signal.

\section{Is the Lexicon the Cause? Isolating the Variable}\label{sec:iso}

The repair bundled lexicon, teardown, and fleet changes; this experiment
isolates the lexicon alone \emph{before} we present the repair method:
a semantic replica of the K1/K2 scan-and-link semantics with \emph{only
the dictionary swapped} --- v2.0 as committed at library creation (182
scan terms), v2.5 mid-evolution (2{,}932), v2.1 the repair (3{,}733 scan terms;\footnote{Three
v2.1 counts appear in this paper and refer to different objects:
3{,}702 canonical schema terms (the committed lexicon), 3{,}733 scan
terms after alias dedup (the replica frame), and 3{,}824 entries in the
production composite dictionary (schema lexicon $\cup$ operational
seed overlay, \S\ref{sec:xdomain}'s parity check).};
construction in \S\ref{sec:lexicon}), plus two v2.1 governance variants.
The replica copies the scanner's matching and folding rules from the
same source reading (CJK substring; ASCII word-boundary with optional
plural, case-folded; fold key = normalized form $+$ type); it is not
instrumented against the live service path, so absolute levels may
drift --- the experiment's force is the within-frame arm contrast. A
frozen-sample parity check bounds that drift: on 200 frozen KB1 chunks
scanned with the \emph{live} production dictionary (3{,}824 entries),
every one of the 913 production mention spans is reproduced by the
replica (span-level \emph{superset}, not output equivalence: the
per-term replica additionally reports 263 overlapping or nested
occurrences that the production merged longest-first alternation
suppresses by design, and a normalized-form bookkeeping rule differs
for aliases that themselves end in ``s''). Boundary, case, plural, and
CJK semantics are identical; the replica's link counts are an
inclusive upper bound within that frame. Because nesting prevalence
can differ per lexicon, we re-ran all cross-domain arms under
production-style overlap suppression (longest-match, non-overlapping):
links fall 10--40\% per arm but \emph{every ordering conclusion is
unchanged} (schema CS $C_{10}$ 0.859 with identical entities; the
size-matched arm $C_{10}$ 0.323 vs.\ three-source 0.366; frequency
still floods at 23+ concepts/doc).

\textbf{Design.} Ten seeded, uniformly random resamples of 2{,}000
chunks each from the library's 5.83M visible chunks --- independent
draws from a \emph{single} corpus, i.e.\ a sampling-stability check
within one library, not cross-domain evidence --- cross-sample disjoint,
deduplicated on redraw (an earlier single run had sampled an ordered
prefix, which we replace rather than defend). Fixed code, fixed corpus,
fixed scanner; the only variable is the dictionary. The sampling unit
is the chunk; in this corpus chunks map to documents almost
one-to-one (sample 0: 2{,}000 chunks $\to$ 1{,}965 distinct
documents, 1.02 chunks/doc), so ``matched documents'' below means
matched chunks and no document is sampled twice. All metrics are
deterministic; Table~\ref{tab:lexiso} reports mean$\pm$sd across the
ten resamples. Concepts-per-document is averaged over \emph{matched}
documents only ($\geq$1 scan hit under that arm's dictionary: mean 977
matched documents per sample under v2.0, 1{,}579 under v2.1), so the
arms' denominators are different sets.

\textbf{Finding 1 --- the collapse signature is reproduced by the lexicon
alone.} Under v2.0, the same code on the same corpus yields 109$\pm$5
entities at concentration 0.513$\pm$0.016 with 1.85$\pm$0.05 concepts per
\emph{matched} document: the collapse needs no pipeline bug, no data loss, no
misconfiguration --- only a category-word dictionary.

\textbf{Finding 2 --- the repair effect is isolated and monotone.}
v2.1 multiplies link-bearing entities $\times$6.3 and concepts per
matched document $\times$2.1 (matched docs per sample 977$\to$1{,}579); generic-head mass falls
0.146$\to$0.058. The production collapsed concentration ($C_{10}$=0.419)
sits between the v2.0 (0.513) and v2.5 (0.322) counterfactuals,
consistent with mid-enrichment dictionary evolution. These are
\emph{structural} signatures: entity count, concentration, and
concepts per document do not by themselves measure terminology recall
or sense fit --- the only semantic reading in this paper is the link
audit of \S\ref{sec:after}.

\textbf{Finding 3 --- the \texttt{be} class is treatable at known cost.}
Case-sensitive short symbols remove the largest entity at the price of
match volume (links $-$12.8\%; $C_{10}\to$0.265); a stop-word block is
gentler (links $-$8.1\%, $C_{10}$ 0.252). The deltas measure matching
\emph{output}, not true recall: the removed mass mixes errors with
correct terms, and the 0.580 pre-intervention judged-valid rate on this
class (\S\ref{sec:after}) motivates the treatment --- the
removed-vs-retained verification below prices exactly this cut.
\emph{Provenance}: decomposing
v2.1's link mass by which committed lexicon first contained each term
--- v2.0-inherited 22.0\%, mid-evolution 16.0\%,
\textbf{three-source-new 61.9\%} (384 of 686 entities) --- is a
decomposition, not a per-source causal ablation; the public-domain
cached-component ablation (\S\ref{sec:xdomain}) supplies the
single-source analogue, while independently regenerating single-source
lexicons for the \emph{production} library remains future work. And the arms
vary the dictionary as a whole (${\sim}$20$\times$ size gap), so term
count, selection quality, and per-source method are not separately
identified --- the equal-size frequency control and the blind
old/new matched-context judging below are the two controls that
close this gap on the same frozen samples.

\emph{Budget sweep, closing the size confound.}
A natural concern is
whether the isolated comparison (182-term collapsed vs.\ 3{,}733-term
repair dictionary) confounds size with selection. We re-ran the frozen
KB1 frame at fixed budgets $K \in \{200, 500, 1{,}000, 2{,}000,
3{,}733\}$ with three same-$K$ sources: the v2.1 pipeline in
construction order (prefix), frequency-top-$K$, and C-value-top-$K$
(candidates ranked on sample-0 text; frame overlap disclosed).
Coverage under the dual-annotated reference grows monotonically with
v2.1's own budget (R 0.004$\to$0.043 from $K$=200 to 3{,}733) while
concentration falls 0.73$\to$0.30 --- the repair's value accrues with
budget but its structure at every budget is distinct: frequency-top
recruits 4--5$\times$ the entities (2{,}036 at $K$=2{,}000) for
nominally higher R that rides on mass its own blind validity
disqualifies (0.45 at the equal-size control), and C-value-top
saturates at 554 entities with R stuck at 0.012 --- statistical
salience without attestation stalls on this corpus. Re-running the
sweep with the baselines re-typed by the unified classifier leaves R
identical at every budget (entity counts within 1.5\%)---typing-
invariant. Size explains the counts; it does not explain the validity
or the distribution.

\textbf{Verification: semantics, governance, monitors.}
Three follow-ups on the same frozen samples close the declared gaps.
(i)~\emph{Blind term-mention judging} (entity-uniform; judge
Qwen3.6-35B, temp 0, disclosed family):
old and new dictionaries are \emph{locally} indistinguishable (0.940
[0.890, 0.968] vs.\ 0.927 [0.873, 0.959] of matched spans are genuine
term mentions) --- the collapse is not per-link falsehood but a
vocabulary-and-mass failure, which is why per-link auditing alone could
not have caught it; an \emph{equal-size frequency-only control}
(3{,}733 terms, top corpus frequency, no selection) reads 0.450
[0.356, 0.548] --- size does not buy validity; selection does. (ii)~\emph{Governance verified}: short-symbol hits removed by
case-sensitivity are 0.033 [0.009, 0.114] valid vs.\ 0.700 [0.575,
0.801] for retained ones --- the $-$12.8\% link cut removes almost
purely error mass (retained still carries 30\%). (iii)~\emph{Graded
degradation} (replacement axis: drop $m$\% of v2.1, keep all category
words): the $C_{10}$ band fires at $\geq$75\% substitution (0.371) and the
full collapse reproduces (0.513, bitwise-identical arm); random
shrinkage fires count monitors first (links $-$50\% at 50\% shrinkage)
while $C_{10}$ stays flat to 50\%; moderate substitution (10--50\%) is a
blind zone for both --- no single gold-free monitor covers it; the stack
deploys as a set.

\textbf{Short-symbol ambiguity: four linking policies.} On 150
short-symbol mentions sampled from the frozen replay chunks (60 on
the \texttt{be} class; each mention judged once, blind, by judge A
and the cross-family judge, 12 disagreements), four policies on the
same dictionary: linking everything (current behavior) reads
precision \textbf{0.24}; exact-case gating (the deployed treatment)
0.725 at true-symbol recall 0.806, rejecting 73\% (rejection
precision 0.936); adding context evidence (long-term neighbors)
recalls slightly more (0.833) at lower precision (0.652); context
plus reverse-NIL trades recall (0.583) for precision (0.724). On the
\texttt{be} class specifically, \emph{zero} of 60 sampled mentions
are genuine element references --- the exact-case gate removes pure
error there. Context gating and case gating exchange precision for
recall rather than dominating each other; the recovered mass lives
in the non-\texttt{be} short symbols.

\textbf{Why 692 entities here vs 1{,}871 in production
(\S\ref{sec:after})?} The isolated frame counts only entities induced
by scanning 2{,}000 chunks (0.034\% of the library) under replica
folding: rare terms need corpus mass to be attested, and the production
layer additionally carries entities from the full corpus and historical
merging. The causal claim rests on within-frame comparisons, never on
this cross-frame count.

\section{Corpus-Attested Lexicon Construction}\label{sec:lexicon}

The repair lexicon whose causal role \S\ref{sec:iso} isolated is built
from three sources, all gated by corpus presence (a term the corpus
never attests is not admitted --- presence is necessary, not sufficient,
for termhood): \textbf{seed transplant} (2{,}284 terms from a healthy
near-domain library); \textbf{corpus-candidate classification} (top
8{,}000 unigram/bigram n-grams by chunk frequency over a
3{,}000-chunk-per-KB sample, frequency $\geq$5 after boilerplate and
stop-word filtering; an LLM classifies each into a schema type or
rejects it); \textbf{per-type expansion} ($\leq$60 per type). Catch-all
types accept no classified noise; cross-type conflicts resolve by
specificity. Classifier: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (AWQ 4-bit, community build;
served under a historical alias), temperature 0.2, single idempotent
run. Output: nuclear v2.1 = 3{,}702 canonical terms; physics v1.1 =
1{,}279.

\section{Rebuild at Scale --- and a Corrected Incident Log}\label{sec:rebuild}

Per knowledge base: delete graph vertices with edges, the four
relational tables in bounded chunks, and the search-side projection;
re-enqueue all documents through four stages. The printed
``766{,}501 jobs'' counts \emph{events}, not distinct doc-stages
(74{,}545$\times$4 = 298{,}180): the surplus is the repair window's
re-enqueue waves (silent-empty reset 75{,}831; zero-effect
relations/graph resets ${\approx}$149k; watchdog-deletion recovery
${\approx}$122k; small libraries 5{,}772).

Four pipeline pathologies surfaced and were fixed with measured effect
--- an unbounded projection join that saturated the database (25+ min
$\to$ seconds once scoped), two link-insertion deadlock classes
(deadlocks 1213 $\to$ 0), and an $O(N^2)$ alias check the richer
lexicon itself amplified (fleet link throughput 8/min $\to$
1{,}100/min, a window-level aggregate). The archival version carries
the full per-pathology accounting.

\textbf{The fifth pathology, revisited.} Our log recorded a
parameter-order bug in a single-statement link writer whose rows
\texttt{INSERT IGNORE} ``silently swallowed'' for seven hours. A minimal
reproduction on the production engine (MySQL 8.4,
\texttt{STRICT\_TRANS\_TABLES}, faithful DDL) sharpens the mechanism:
the NULL from \texttt{UNHEX('alloy')} is \textbf{replaced by the
implicit default 0x00$\times$16 and the row is inserted} --- into a
ghost bucket invisible to every per-KB query --- and a later correct
re-insert for the same (mention, entity) pair is \textbf{silently
skipped by the unique key}: the orphan poisons the repair unless
deleted first (production carries zero 0x00 rows today, so cleanup
happened). The duration claim also fails row-level forensics: the
registry's own \texttt{created\_at} histogram (Figure~\ref{fig:telemetry})
shows KB1 inserting at 1.0--2.3M rows/hour \emph{through} the nominal
silent window and KB2's entire link layer landing in one hour. We keep
the log entry and its correction side by side: incident narratives
written from job dashboards are themselves unverified claims.

\textbf{Name quality} (Figure~\ref{fig:topentities}). The repaired head
is still generic: \texttt{be} (2.26M links, 9.0\%), \texttt{alpha},
\texttt{gamma}, \texttt{beta}, \texttt{method}. The \texttt{be} case is
a normalization failure --- element symbol and auxiliary verb merge
under case-folded matching --- and \S\ref{sec:iso} prices its
treatment. The repair moved the problem from ``no terminology at all''
to ``terminology plus a generic tail.''

\textbf{Link audit: 300 risk-stratified links.} Entities drawn
uniformly within four strata (top-10 by frequency; medium, ranks 11--100;
tail, ranks 101+; short symbols, canonical names of $\leq$4 letters),
then one link uniformly per entity --- seeded and replayable. Span
integrity 300/300. The judged-valid rate is \textbf{0.837} for this
sample (Wilson 95\% CI [0.791, 0.874]; entity-cluster bootstrap over
the 187 clusters, 10{,}000 resamples: [0.782, 0.887]); by stratum: top-10
\textbf{0.920} [0.812, 0.968], medium 0.850 [0.767, 0.907], tail
0.910 [0.838, 0.952], short-symbol \textbf{0.580} [0.442, 0.706].
Three scope notes. First, the 50/100/100/50 quotas target risk, not
production link frequencies: 0.837 is a stratum-mixed rate and must not
be read as an unweighted production-wide link precision. Second, the
300 links come from 187 distinct entities (the head stratum's 50 links
from just six; the short and head strata share ``term''), so the

Table~\ref{tab:kbstats}); healthy/repaired large libraries run 0.16--0.19, the rebuilt nuclear

and 0.768 (reported as measured). The collapsed physics backup reads

alone does not catch every collapse; the name-audit and coverage legs
carry the rest. The

post-hoc} candidate thresholds read from these 13 libraries --- triage
triggers for human inspection, not classifiers, and not out-of-sample
validated. Two detection targets must be kept distinct: \emph{collapse
detection} (has the entity layer degenerated?) versus
\emph{lexicon-quality warning} (is the distribution contaminated with
generic vocabulary?). For collapse detection a genuinely concentrated
healthy library is a $C_{10}$ false positive; as a lexicon-quality
warning the same trigger is correct when the tail is in fact generic.
The composite's zero-false-alarm claim below is scoped to collapse
detection. Known blind spots (per target): \emph{false positives}
(collapse target) --- healthy libraries whose terminology is genuinely
concentrated, e.g.\ the two small treated libraries at 0.398/0.768
(real terms, small corpora) and the public-domain CS three-source arm
at 0.404 (\S\ref{sec:xdomain}); \emph{false negatives} ---
replacement-type degradation below ${\sim}$75\% substitution, where
$C_{10}$ moves less than one sd (graded degradation), and any collapse
whose head happens to be diversified. Validation need not re-collapse
production: the graded-degradation arm above injects lexicon decay
offline into a frozen slice and reports fire bands and size
sensitivity (the experiment has no time axis, so we make no
detection-latency claim). A leave-one-library-out check on the 14
rows (thresholds re-frozen at the q90/q97 of the remaining healthy
libraries each fold) yields 12 correct quiets, the same known
false-positive class (the genuinely concentrated small library),
and the same known false-negative class (the collapsed backup reads
below the LOO band --- count monitors and the name audit carry it):
the bands are self-consistent across libraries but remain
deployment-derived; unseen-deployment validation stays future work.
\emph{Composite rule, evaluated.} Because single-leg thresholds
misfire in both directions, the deployable unit is a four-leg
disjunction (fire on any leg): concentration ($C_{10}{>}0.35$, small
libraries exempt), link-count drop ($>$50\% vs.\ healthy baseline),
generic-head share ($>$0.10), and entity-cardinality drop ($>$50\%).
On the frozen graded-degradation archive this composite fires on
every should-alarm state (collapse, ${\ge}$75\% replacement,
${\ge}$50\% shrinkage) with \emph{zero} misses and zero false alarms
on the healthy baseline and mild degradations --- reported as in-sample
diagnostic verification (thresholds and classes derive from the same
libraries; frozen-threshold unseen-library validation is open); the
10--50\%
replacement band remains undetected by every leg (at 50\% the

honest structural floor of aggregate-statistics monitoring, and the
reason the name-audit leg exists. \emph{Cold start}: the two count
legs need a healthy history baseline a new library lacks; until a
first full enrichment pass seeds it, they are inert, and protection
rests on the two history-free legs---concentration and the name
audit---exactly the legs that exposed this incident.
\textbf{(b)~entity
cardinality ratio} (coarse secondary, gameable both ways).
\textbf{(c)~top-entity name audit} (five-minute quarterly control).
\textbf{(d)~target-table growth reconciliation} --- the B95
ghost-bucket mechanism makes this mandatory wherever a bulk writer is
guarded by \texttt{INSERT IGNORE}. The small-library treatment is an
illustrative within-deployment case (the candidate bands were read after
observing these 13 libraries --- not out-of-sample validation):
cardinality normalized (870$\to$629,
209$\to$153) while concentration still flags both --- the two monitors
separate ``cardinality repaired'' from ``distribution still skewed.''

The incident is not an argument for LLM-everywhere: if extraction is a
scan, the dictionary serves as the model and deserves a trained model's
evaluation discipline. Admission criteria (corpus presence) are
necessary but not sufficient; \emph{ranking} criteria (IDF-style
contrast, bilingual consistency, length/type priors) are the open
problem --- the generic tail is the standing evidence.

\section{Does It Generalize? Three Public Domains}\label{sec:xdomain}

The incident corpus is nuclear-science literature; a natural objection is
that the failure and the repair are local accidents. We therefore replay
the lexicon-only design on three public domains --- arXiv
cs.CL (10{,}000 abstracts), PubMed biomedicine (9{,}988), and arXiv
cond-mat.mtrl-sci (10{,}000) --- with seeded splits per domain:
3{,}000 abstracts for lexicon construction only, 100 frozen test
abstracts for silver annotation, and the remainder replayed as ten
disjoint ${\sim}$690-document samples for structural metrics (mean
across samples; sd archived). \emph{Mass} throughout denotes two
reported quantities --- lexicon size ($|$L$|$) and per-document
annotation volume (concepts/doc) --- plus matched-document coverage
where stated; we report all three per arm and predefine no scalar
utility over them, so no ``best'' is asserted. Five arms share one scanner; only the
lexicon varies: \emph{batch schema} (the production-failure form: each
domain's \emph{own} current repository schema lexicon, i.e.\ the batch
tool's committed output for that domain --- category-shaped alias
sets, in CS largely Chinese or uppercase-acronym forms),
\emph{frequency} top-$K$ and \emph{C-value} top-$K$ (each size-matched
to ours), \emph{LLM direct} (per-batch extraction without corpus
validation), and the \emph{three-source} recipe. One adaptation is
disclosed: source~1 transplants seeds from a healthy near-domain
library, which does not exist for these public domains --- we substitute
LLM knowledge seeds under the same corpus-presence validation; sources
2--3 (corpus-candidate classification, per-type expansion with presence
validation) are unchanged.

\textbf{Model-annotation protocol with human verification.} The
annotation layer is model-based; human input enters as verification
of a stratified sample (below), not as from-scratch labels.
Silver reference lists come from a strong annotator LLM (judge A,
Qwen3.6-35B) enumerating every domain term in each test abstract; arm
quality is then judged blind on stratified samples (head/medium/
tail/short-symbol entities) by judge A and a second Qwen-series model
(judge B), with disagreements and missing judgments arbitrated by a
third \emph{cross-family} judge (DeepSeek; 8--29\% of rows per cell,
Wilson intervals below). Two instrument limits are disclosed rather
than hidden. First,
enumeration is the weak link: on 30-document subsets re-annotated by
the cross-family model, term-list Jaccard with judge A is 0.41 (CS),
0.55 (biomedicine), 0.52 (materials) --- so silver P/R are indicative
levels, not ground truth, and we read contrasts between arms (which
share the instrument) rather than absolute magnitudes. Second, judge B
cannot enumerate at all (the gateway forces chain-of-thought past read
timeouts) and is used only for short blind judgments; and the LLM-direct
arm's silver-P is same-family inflated, since judge A's family also
built that arm.

\emph{Instrument sensitivity, tested on the shared subset.} Because a
shared instrument does not guarantee arm-wise bias cancellation, we
recomputed all five arms' occurrence-level P/R on the exact 30-document
A$\cap$C subset under \emph{both} references (matching rule:
lower-cased surface with naive plural stripping, applied symmetrically).
The recall ordering of the five arms is \textbf{identical under both
judges in all three domains}; the F1 ordering is identical in
biomedicine and materials, while in CS only the middle band permutes
(frequency/C-value/three-source swap ranks 2--4; first and last are
unchanged). Three-source's precision \emph{rises} under the
cross-family reference in all domains (CS 0.218$\to$0.312, biomedicine
0.195$\to$0.287, materials 0.246$\to$0.260) while LLM-direct's falls
(CS 0.653$\to$0.544), consistent with the disclosed family bias and
mildly favoring three-source under the harder reference. We therefore
rest cross-domain conclusions on (i)~structural metrics (no
instrument), (ii)~recall ordering and (iii)~arbitrated blind validity,
and treat mid-band F1 orderings and absolute P/R levels as
instrument-conditional. Judged-valid rates carry Wilson 95\% CIs ---
e.g.\ biomedicine batch-schema 0.906 [0.810, 0.956] vs.\ three-source
0.850 [0.756, 0.912]: overlapping --- and the arbitration fraction per
cell is 0.08--0.29.
\emph{A dual-annotated reference, and what survives it.} To strengthen
the instrument we finally cross-annotated 200 complete units --- 80
frozen KB1 chunks plus 40 test documents per public domain --- with
two independent model annotators reading full text (not approving
candidates): span-level entries with termness, specificity, type, and
identity-ambiguity flags. P1 = deepseek-flash, P2 = deepseek-v4-pro
(same vendor, different models --- overlap disclosed; a gateway
thinking-mode model was attempted first and is unusable for
full-document annotation: forced deliberation exceeds any practical
token budget), disagreements arbitrated by the cross-family Qwen
judge. Agreement is materially stronger than single-model enumeration:
domain-set Jaccard \textbf{0.59--0.66} across the four scopes,
termness agreement on overlap \textbf{94.5--98.8\%} (vs.\ 0.41--0.55
for enumeration pairs). Under this reference: the batch-schema arms
recall 0--1\% (the collapse signature under the strongest instrument
yet); the production lexicons' first recall estimate on KB1 reads
v2.0 1.0\% $\to$ v2.1 4.3\% of adjudicated distinct concepts
(4.3$\times$; the reference is deliberately exhaustive, so absolute
levels are floors, not ceilings); the selection arms' recall deficit
replicates (learned/cover 0.7--3.2\%); and the frequency arm's
apparent recall rides on mass its own blind validity (0.29--0.40)
already disqualifies. \emph{Human verification}: one author
independently confirmed a seeded, stratified sample of 197 adjudicated
terms over 20 units (5 per scope; sheet, protocol, and per-row
verdicts in the artifact) --- confirm rate \textbf{0.934} [0.890,
0.961]; by scope 0.833 (KB1) / 0.967 / 0.925 / 0.950; two-model
consensus items verify at 0.949 vs.\ 0.853--0.960 for arbitrated
additions. This bounds the reference's precision from the model side;
it does not measure omissions (verification, not from-scratch
re-annotation) and is single-author --- an independent two-annotator
gold remains future work. The protocol, adjudications, and per-unit
records ship with the artifact. Entity-cluster bootstrap CIs for the three-source
validity rates (10{,}000 resamples) are [0.747, 0.921] (CS),
[0.772, 0.924] (biomedicine), [0.846, 0.974] (materials) --- wider
than link-level Wilson intervals, conclusions unchanged.

\emph{From-scratch enumeration (recall side)}: the same author
enumerated terms from zero on 30 further held-out abstracts (10 per
domain, frozen test splits; 418 unique terms, exact case-folded
surface match). The batch-schema arm covers \textbf{0/418} --- 0 even
among the 83 terms recurring in $>$10 construction-corpus documents;
size-aligned statistical arms cover 16.9--22.9\% of that core stratum
(C-value 19 $>$ frequency $=$ three-source 14), LLM-direct 47.0\%
with a $4.6\times$ larger unvalidated lexicon. Half the terms (209)
never occur in the 3{,}000-doc construction corpus --- paper coinages
that no bounded static lexicon can carry (mention-layer scope),
bounding lexicon-layer recall itself. Per 1{,}000 entries,
recall efficiency: C-value 16.6 $>$ three-source 14.8 $>$ frequency
12.2 $>$ LLM-direct 10.5. Three-source is thus a high-validity,
bounded-budget \emph{repair} for the entity layer, not a high-recall
term extractor; coinages and rare terms are mention-layer scope (the
strata table ships with the artifact).

floods (30.7 concepts/doc, 4.5\% generic head). The recipe's value is
in the assembly and the gate, not in any single source. One component is demoted on this evidence: \emph{expansion}.
Removing it costs nothing structurally (entities 211.7 vs.\ 202.1,

valid under double judging with only 0.50 judge agreement ($n$=60) ---
contested quality. We reframe expansion as an optional
diversity module, not a necessary source; the load-bearing pair is
corpus-attested classification plus the presence gate.

\subsection{Where Recall Is Lost, and What Selection Can Buy}
\label{sec:funnel}

The silver recalls above are occurrence-level against an enumeration
instrument; before optimizing selection we decompose where the loss
sits. For every silver occurrence we test four nested events:
expressible by \emph{some} candidate ($C$: a wide pool --- all
construct n-grams at frequency ${\ge}$2 plus definition-evidenced
abbreviations plus all LLM candidate lists; 12{,}149/15{,}032/13{,}121
terms per domain), kept in the arm's dictionary ($K$), detected by the
actual scan at that occurrence ($D$), and linked under normalization
($L$). The funnel is unambiguous: $P(C)$ is 0.44/0.45/0.41
(CS/biomed/materials) --- \emph{the candidate pool, not the ranking,
is the binding constraint}; three-source keeps $P(K|C)$=0.15/0.21/0.14;
scan and link are nearly lossless ($P(D|K{\cdot}C)$ 0.89--0.98,
$P(L)$ $\approx$1). The pool gaps are two-character element symbols
(excluded by the tokenizer's 3-character minimum), terms attested only
in test abstracts (construct-side presence cannot see them), and
neologisms; the selection gaps include, e.g., \emph{LLM} and
\emph{large language model} themselves in CS.

\emph{Risk-constrained selection, tested and not adopted.} The
natural next question is whether a learned or set-selection objective
beats per-term ranking at equal budget. On the same pools, with
construct-side labels from the cross-family judge (test documents
never touch pool, training, or tuning) and proxy link-correctness
scores (explicitly proxies), neither learned ranking nor
coverage-linked selection dominates the three-source pipeline at any
tested budget ($K/2$ to $2K$): silver-P rises while arbitrated blind
validity collapses in CS and biomedicine (learned-rank 0.29 [0.19,
0.41] and 0.46 [0.32, 0.55] vs.\ 0.838/0.850) and trails in
materials (0.86--0.89 vs.\ 0.912 at one-fifth the recall); recall is
flat in $K$ (the pool ceiling again); and the selected lexicons
\emph{concentrate} ($C_{10}$ 0.69--0.73 in CS vs.\ 0.404) --- a
proxy-validity objective re-creates the head-heavy distribution our
monitors flag, and the silver-P gain was an instrument artifact
(enumeration under-cover flatters arms whose extra mass is invalid).
We keep the three-source pipeline as the repair baseline and report
this as a bounded negative result; construction details, budget
curves, and the beam-search reference (within 1.5\% of local search)
are in the archival long version and the artifact.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

\textbf{When does a structurally valid lexicon produce semantic
collapse?} When its entries are category words: the scan executes
faithfully, and a dictionary-only swap reproduces the signature.
\textbf{What does corpus-presence gating repair, and what remains?}
Terminology and coverage (annotated documents ${\sim}$51.5\%$\to$98.5\%)
--- but a generic tail persists; presence is necessary, not sufficient.
\textbf{What can gold-free diagnostics see?} Distribution and names;
per-link truth they cannot see --- blind judging shows old-dictionary
links are locally as ``valid'' as new ones (0.94 vs 0.93), so the
collapse hides from per-link audits, and the semantic gain runs through
vocabulary and mass (equal-size frequency control: 0.45 vs 0.93). The
incident motivated and validated; the controlled swap, the stratified
audit, the graded-degradation alarms, and a three-domain public-corpus
replay carry the evidence. \textbf{Does it generalize?} On three public
domains the batch-schema form reproduces the collapse signature
($C_{10}$ up to 0.899 with recall ${\le}$0.009), frequency floods,
and the three-source recipe is the size-matched design point with the
best arbitrated validity at bounded annotation volume --- a trade-off
under the tested metrics and AI instrument, not a claimed unique
optimum; the failure mode and the repair are properties of the construction
method, not of one corpus.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

(1)~The generic tail survives repair (9\% of links on ``be'').
(2)~Retrieval attribution is bounded, not eliminated: the full
lexicon$\times$configuration decomposition is runnable on frozen slices
or isolated stacks via graded degradation --- not yet run, rather than
impossible. (3)~Coverage: two libraries rebuilt, two smaller
treated; bilingual anomalies documented, not explained. (4)~Six
secondary incidents extended the repair; reported as findings.
(5)~Audit and cross-judge are model-assisted (same vendor family), not
human-adjudicated; the risk-stratified sample mixes quota-defined strata
and clusters on 187 entities, and unparseable cross-judge responses may
be non-random. (6)~The isolated experiment uses a semantic-replica
scanner (not instrumented against the service path) and
committed-snapshot dictionaries over ten resamples of one corpus --- a
stability check; its metrics are structural,
with semantic reading confined to sampled audits (the blind
round included: same judge family, term-mention rubric not
comparable to the link audit); governance verdicts (removed 0.033 vs
retained 0.700) inherit that exposure. (7)~The cross-domain study
(\S\ref{sec:xdomain}) is model-annotated with a
single-author human verification of a 197-term sample (precision
bound; recall is referenced by a single-author 30-abstract
from-scratch enumeration; \S\ref{sec:xdomain}): silver enumeration
agreement across families is 0.41--0.55; an instrument-sensitivity
rerun on the shared 30-document subset shows the arms' recall ordering
stable under both references (F1 mid-band orderings in CS are
instrument-conditional); one
gateway judge is unusable for enumeration, and the LLM-direct arm's
silver-P is same-family inflated --- the cross-domain conclusions rest
on structural metrics, recall orderings, and arbitrated blind validity,
with the
source-substitution adaptation (LLM knowledge seeds in place of seed
transplant) disclosed. The recall funnel and the selection study
(\S\ref{sec:funnel}) inherit the silver instrument for their loss
attribution and training labels; the selection arms' dev labels come
from the cross-family judge, and the negative result is stated under
those proxies.
