\title{Where Do Semantic Assets Help in Bilingual Scholarly Retrieval?%
\\A Nuclear-Literature Study on the KnowledgeNexus Platform}
\author{Zhiqian Chen}
\email{chenzhiqian@cdut.edu.cn}
\affiliation{\institution{Chengdu University of Technology}\city{Chengdu}\country{China}}

\begin{abstract}
Retrieving evidence from bilingual scientific articles often requires
recovering several relevant documents whose support is distributed
across passages. Multiple retrieval channels can increase candidate
availability, yet their signals may be aggregated at a different
granularity from the evidence the query requires. We investigate this
mismatch in nuclear-science literature using KnowledgeNexus, a
retrieval platform with bilingual terminology assets and
passage-level source identifiers, as an experimental setting
(80{,}852 documents, 7.2M chunks). Our analysis separates candidate
availability, document-level support aggregation, and evidence
emission, and distinguishes direct entity-dependent retrieval from
lexical and contextual uses of semantic assets. Archived paired
ablations show conditional effects: removing the direct
entity-dependent channels leaves any-hit@10 and document
coverage@10 of two scoped question sets unchanged (largest movement: $-$1.0pt cov@20, NS), while
enabling the co-occurrence path improves any-hit@10 by 10.1 percentage
points on 99 naturally phrased association questions (95\% CI,
5.1--16.2). These comparisons use different retrieval profiles and do
not establish a universal channel effect. We also examine truncated
document-level aggregation ($\lambda{=}2$ scores each document from its
two best chunks) and explain why its emission policy (pair vs.\ stride)
must be evaluated separately from its scoring rule. A provenance audit
distinguishes source addressability from semantic support: mention
spans match their stored surfaces in 500/500 sampled cases, while a
separate automated audit judges 59.6\% of 433 citation instances
faithful to their cited passages. Together these findings motivate
evaluating semantic assets by query condition and stage of use, rather
than by the presence of an entity or graph layer alone. Production
scale provides implementation context; comparative claims remain
bounded by the controlled evaluation settings.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A scholarly query may ask how a material choice affects detector timing,
or which studies report evidence for a particular association. Answering
such queries requires recovering a set of relevant articles and
identifying the passages that support the requested relation. In
nuclear-science literature, terminology and evidence span Chinese and
English sources, and the required support may occur in different
sections of a long article. The retrieval objective therefore includes
both coverage of relevant documents and access to their supporting
passages; finding one plausible passage is insufficient when the query
requires several sources.

Hybrid retrieval provides several ways to locate this evidence. Lexical
matching captures explicit terminology, dense retrieval captures
semantic similarity, and document-level or synthetic-query indexes add
alternative representations. These channels can nevertheless assign
their strongest signals to \emph{different passages of the same
article}: a fusion rule operating only on passage identities may then
leave that article below the returned cutoff even when several channels
retrieve parts of it. Aggregating support at the document level is a
plausible response, but the scoring rule and the output order must be
distinguished---returning several passages consecutively from each
document changes how many \emph{different} documents are visible within
a fixed passage budget. Whether document aggregation helps depends on
both decisions and on the query's evidence requirements.

Entity and relation assets introduce a second distinction. They can
supply candidates or alter the returned list through co-occurrence
retrieval and graph-based expansion, but they can also provide
bilingual aliases to lexical matching, entity context to reranking, or
source-linked relations to answer construction. Disabling an entity
retrieval channel does not remove these other uses. Moreover, an
adaptive channel may contribute little when the base retriever already
satisfies its triggering condition. A null channel ablation consequently
establishes a marginal effect under a specific retrieval profile; it
does not determine the value of the asset layer as a whole. Evaluating
semantic assets requires identifying \emph{where} they are consumed and
\emph{when} the relevant path is active.

We study these issues using KnowledgeNexus, a literature-retrieval
platform with bilingual terminology assets and passage-level source
identifiers, deployed at production scale (80{,}852 documents, 7.2M
chunks across 13 knowledge bases); the platform is the experimental
setting, not the contribution. Our investigation addresses two
questions. \textbf{RQ1:} when relevant documents appear in channel
candidates but are absent from the final list, how do document-level
aggregation and passage emission affect evidence coverage and
first-hit ranking? \textbf{RQ2:} under which query and gating
conditions do direct entity-dependent retrieval and other uses of
semantic assets have measurable marginal value? We organize the
evaluation around these questions, distinguishing paired interventions
from configuration bundles and deployment telemetry. Nuclear-domain
question sets provide the main setting; public multi-hop comparisons
require a matched retrieval unit, metric, and model configuration
before they can support architectural conclusions
(Section~\ref{sec:results}).

The archived results show that the value of an additional channel is
conditional on the retrieval profile and question distribution. Direct
entity-dependent channels leave the @10 metrics of the scoped question
sets unchanged, while a co-occurrence comparison on naturally phrased
association questions shows a positive any-hit effect (+10.1pt,
CI~[5.1,~16.2]). These observations motivate an analysis of gate
activity and asset use rather than an overall claim that graph
enrichment either helps or fails. We also distinguish the integrity of
the source-lookup path (mention spans match stored surfaces in 500/500
sampled cases) from the faithfulness of generated citations (59.6\% of
433 instances under automated judgment): programmatic source checks and
citation audits measure different properties and yield different levels
of assurance. The production deployment establishes that the evaluated
representations can be maintained at scale; it does not replace
controlled comparisons or resolve the remaining attribution questions.

Our contributions are threefold, each tied to evidence reported in this
paper:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A retrieval diagnosis that separates candidate
availability, support aggregation, and output truncation}
(Section~\ref{sec:autopsy}): a channel-isolated autopsy of every missed
gold document locates the dominant loss layer, and states which
observations support an aggregation-based explanation and which remain
unresolved.
\item \textbf{An empirical analysis of fusion granularity and
semantic-asset use} (Sections~\ref{sec:channels}
and~\ref{sec:results}): we examine truncated document aggregation,
distinguish score aggregation from evidence emission, and report direct
channel effects separately from indirect asset consumption and
configuration bundles.
\item \textbf{An evaluation protocol with explicit evidence units and
provenance boundaries} (Sections~\ref{sec:bench}
and~\ref{sec:construction}): we document candidate scope, gold
construction, retrieval budgets, and paired comparisons, and separate
programmatic addressability checks from automated judgments of
citation support.
\end{enumerate}
Negative results are reported with the same rigour as positive ones;
they are a reporting discipline, not a contribution in themselves.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Nuclear-domain NLP falls into three strands. \textbf{Regulatory-side
systems}: RADIANT-LLM~\cite{radiantllm} adapts an agentic RAG stack to
nuclear regulatory corpora \emph{with} page- and figure-level retrieval,
provenance tracking, citation constraints, and human verification of
answers---evidence anchoring in this domain is not our novelty; what we
add relative to it are bilingual dictionary assets, fusion-granularity
interventions with paired controls, and evaluation at two additional
orders of library scale. \textbf{Domain pretraining}
(NukeLM~\cite{nukelm}) and ontology-grounded small-scale systems
(CurieLM~\cite{curielm}) adapt encoders or curate ontologies for narrow
collections; neither addresses retrieval over tens of thousands of
full-text documents. Fayyaz et al.'s survey~\cite{fayyaz} names three
challenges---terminology density, bilingual sources, traceability---which
organize our intervention targets (dictionary-driven entity enrichment;
bilingual alias expansion in the lexical channel; auditable source
lookups). HydroLLM-Benchmark~\cite{hydrollm} is precedent for
domain-generated benchmarks; ours extends the practice with
paired-bootstrap significance and corpus-scoped arms. On the
infrastructure side, S2ORC~\cite{s2orc} (81.1M papers overall, 8.1M with
structured full text, linked citations and figure/table mentions)
demonstrates corpus-scale scholarly processing with its own evidence
layers; it does not provide domain terminology assets or
retrieval-side fusion controls, which is the layer we study. Bilingual
retrieval in the cross-language IR tradition~\cite{clir} remains largely
translation-driven, whereas our bilingual handling runs through
dictionary alias assets inside the lexical channel. For evaluation,
component-level RAG diagnosis~\cite{ragchecker} and citation-grounded
generation evaluation~\cite{alce} define the lines our failure
attribution and citation-faithfulness rounds extend. In the
\textbf{graph-RAG lineage}, HippoRAG~\cite{hipporag},
LightRAG~\cite{lightrag} and GraphRAG~\cite{graphrag} demonstrate
multi-hop or global-sensemaking gains at small-to-moderate scale, and
RAPTOR~\cite{raptor} represents the parallel
hierarchical-summarization line; our single-implementation measurements
(Section~\ref{sec:results}) show one configuration of this lineage
hitting a serial-merge construction wall (${\sim}$1 document/hour after
the first 47 of an 82-document subset) and its LLM-extracted entities
fragile on domain full text (10.6\% chunk-NER failure versus 0.25\% on
Wikipedia-grade text)---implementation- and configuration-scoped
observations, not a complexity law for the family. In the
\textbf{query/document-expansion lineage}, doc2query~\cite{doc2query}
and HyDE~\cite{hyde} transform one side of the match into the other's
space; our synq channel is a doc2query asset built at ingestion, and our
docvec HyDE slot is a trained-rewriter position that the training-free
variant could not fill (Section~\ref{sec:negative}). The
differentiation, stated as comparison axes rather than absence claims:
prior systems vary in bilingual dictionary assets, in where entity
evidence enters retrieval (candidates, fusion votes, reranker context,
or generation), and in evaluation units (rule-constructed corpus-scoped
coverage versus open-domain QA); we do not claim prior work lacks
provenance or domain adaptation---we measure \emph{where} such assets
pay off in a bilingual scholarly retrieval stack, which prior
evaluations have not isolated.

\section{Task, Evidence Units, and System}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Task and evidence units}

Let $D$ denote the candidate document collection, $C(d)$ the passages
(chunks) of document $d$, and $G_q \subseteq D$ the annotated relevant
documents for query $q$. A retrieval system returns an ordered passage
list $H_q$, each passage carrying a document identifier and a source
lookup key. Two properties are separate and must not be conflated:
\emph{document relevance} (retrieving a document in $G_q$) and
\emph{passage support} (a specific passage evidencing the query)---a
retrieved relevant document does not guarantee that every passage
returned from it supports the query. Gold sets for association questions
are \emph{rule-defined} (documents covering both entities of a mined
pair; Section~\ref{sec:bench}), not exhaustive semantic-relevance
annotations. The interventions studied in this paper act at three
stages of this pipeline: which documents enter channel candidates
(\emph{candidate availability}), how passage-level support is
aggregated into document scores (\emph{fusion granularity}), and how
many passages per document occupy a fixed output budget (\emph{evidence
emission}). KnowledgeNexus, described next, is the platform in which
these stages are implemented, deployed, and measured.

\subsection{Architecture}

KnowledgeNexus organises the platform as four cooperating services over a
single authoritative data plane: an ingestion worker, an enrichment worker,
a retrieval API, and an administration/observer pair. Persisted state
splits into \textbf{two authoritative stores}---MySQL (bibliographic and
entity registries, chunk spans, provenance, governance ledgers) and MinIO
(content-addressed raw artefacts)---and \textbf{rebuildable serving
projections} derived from them: OpenSearch (chunk/document/query/entity
projections with kNN indexes; chunk text lives only here and is
rebuildable from the MySQL spans over the MinIO raw store) and a
NebulaGraph store (entity--relation traversal, re-projected from the
registry tables---the post-repair production rebuild did exactly this,
from the registry, at zero LLM cost). All
stores are accessed exclusively through service interfaces
(Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). Three properties matter for the rest of this paper:

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../A线-figures/latex_redraw/p1_system_overview.pdf}
\caption{Traceable construction with independently ready base assets and
asynchronous enrichment. MinIO preserves original files and MySQL governs
identity, visibility and knowledge registries; OpenSearch and NebulaGraph
are rebuildable serving projections. Dashed arrows denote asynchronous
handoff. The four application services operate over this data plane.}
\label{fig:arch}
\end{figure*}

\textbf{(a) Data authority.} Chunk text exists only in the search projection;
MySQL \texttt{kb\_chunks} stores spans, and \texttt{chunk\_id} is a
deterministic hash of (kb, doc, generation, position), so every retrieved
passage is traceable to its source span, parse generation and chunker
version. Documents ingested through the batch endpoint are fingerprinted
(content-addressed raw store + \texttt{stable\_fingerprint} uniqueness),
making ingestion idempotent and auditable.

\textbf{(b) Discipline schemas.} Each knowledge base binds a layered subject
schema (\texttt{\_base} academic layer + 18 subject layers + custom layers).
The schema is not documentation but an operative asset: its term dictionary
drives mention extraction (K1), its entity/relation types drive linking and
typed-relation adjudication (K2/K3). We show in Section~\ref{sec:results}
that schema binding is the root of the entity channel: a test corpus bound to
the generic schema yields 14 canonical entities where the
nuclear-electronics schema yields 1{,}166.

\textbf{(c) Capability bits.} Every enriched channel is guarded by a
per-partition capability bit consulted at query time; a channel whose data is
absent or stale degrades silently. This is what allows the honest-ablation
methodology of Sections~\ref{sec:channels}--\ref{sec:results}: channels can
be switched on/off per query without reindexing.

\subsection{Retrieval planner}

Retrieval is a planner over channels (strategy A single-KB direct, B
bibliography drill-down, C federation; topic routing D/E experimental).
Channels contribute ranked chunk lists fused by weighted reciprocal-rank
fusion (RRF) with per-channel weights; the intent gate (template words +
$\geq$2 dictionary entity surfaces in the query) modulates weights for
association-intent queries. Latency-critical paths are cached with an ACL- and
channel-set-aware key; configuration changes (weights, caps) deliberately
invalidate nothing and require an explicit cache purge---an operational
discipline documented after it bit us twice.

\section{Traceable Construction}
\label{sec:construction}

\subsection{Corpus and ingestion}

\textbf{Corpus status.} The full-window ingestion completed: 80{,}855
documents parsed from 75{,}476 source PDFs (80{,}852 ACTIVE; the 3-document
difference is content-duplicate soft-deletes from idempotent ingestion) across
nuclear-domain journals---the English library led by Sensors (21.4k), JHEP
(13.1k), Phys.\ Rev.\ D (6.2k) and Phys.\ Lett.\ B, the Chinese library by
原子能科学技术, 强激光与粒子束, 核动力工程 and 核电子学与探测技术---across
13 active knowledge bases and 7{,}229{,}225 text chunks, with figure-media
backfill closing at 100\% \texttt{media\_uri} coverage. Ingestion accepts
parsed Markdown (MinerU route) or pre-chunked text (RADAI migration route)
and produces chunks at ${\sim}1{,}200$ characters respecting heading
structure, tables as standalone blocks, with title-context prefixes. Chunk
vectors (Qwen3-Embedding-8B; native 4{,}096-d output, indexed at the
deployed 1{,}024-d Matryoshka truncation, Section~4.2)~\cite{qwen3emb} are
written synchronously with ingestion; lexical and dense channels are therefore
immediately queryable while enrichment proceeds asynchronously.

\subsection{Enrichment pipeline (K1--K3)}

\textbf{K1 mentions} are dictionary-and-rule scans of chunk text against the
bound schema's term dictionary (plus bilingual aliases), persisted in
\texttt{raw\_mention} with stable mention ids---no LLM calls. \textbf{K2
linking} resolves mentions to canonical entities via alias tables and, only
for ambiguous surfaces, a context disambiguation LLM call. \textbf{K3 typed
relations} mines co-occurrence candidate pairs and adjudicates relation types
with the LLM, persisting typed edges with evidence. Doc2query synthesis (R3)
generates synthetic queries per chunk (${\sim}3$ per chunk,
doc2query-style document expansion~\cite{doc2query}) into a query
index consumed by the synq channel; doc/topic summaries feed the bibliography
and abstract vectors. Evidence granularity, stated precisely: relation rows
anchor at (document, chunk) level, while K1 mentions carry verbatim surfaces
with character spans. Fig.~\ref{fig:trace} traces one document end to end
with recorded values.

\textbf{Traceability audit (programmatic, sampled).} Because a designed
tracing path is not the same as a measured one, we audit the deployed
production library read-only (n=500 per check, KB1): recomputing
$\mathrm{sha256}(\mathrm{kb}{:}\mathrm{doc}{:}1{:}\mathrm{gen}{:}\mathrm{pos})$
reproduces the stored \texttt{chunk\_id} for 500/500 sampled chunks; the
OpenSearch projection's text hashes match the registry
\texttt{content\_hash} for 499/500 (99.8\%); the registry's mention spans,
sliced out of the projected chunk text, match the recorded surface
\emph{exactly} for 500/500; and 496/500 (99.2\%) sampled relation-evidence
rows cite a chunk whose text contains both endpoint entity names---an
\emph{endpoint co-presence} check on the cited passage, not an
adjudication of relation-type correctness and not evidence that the
passage entails the relation; relation semantics remain unmeasured at
scale. The
honest boundary: relation evidence is anchored at (document, chunk)
granularity---its quote field is a typed template and character spans are
unpopulated (0 of 1.18M rows)---so span-level verifiability currently ends
at the mention layer; extending it to relations is future work.

\textbf{Citation faithfulness (automated round).} Following the citation-evaluation
line~\cite{alce}, we measure the output side of traceability: we sample
50 questions (24 association / 15 real
queries / 11 hard-probe), generate answers over the deployed normal-lane
retrieval with mandatory inline citations, and judge every citation
instance (433 after excluding ``context does not provide'' disclaimer
sentences) against the full text of the chunk it cites: 59.6\% of
citations are judged faithful, 75.1\% faithful-or-partial (real queries:
68.7\%/91.2\%; adversarial hard set: 45.0\%/53.3\%); 57.3\% of all 433
citation instances carry a supportive verdict with a quote
programmatically verified to appear verbatim in the cited chunk. The
433 instances nest within 50 answers (mean 8.7 judged citations per
answer), so
instance-level percentages overstate the effective sample size; any
interval estimate would need answer-level clustering. \emph{Disclosure:}
this round is
LLM-as-judge (the pipeline model judges its own generations, temperature
0) with programmatic quote verification---an automated lower-rigour
substitute for the human judgment the question really calls for, which
remains open; the moderate per-citation rates also reflect a generator
that pads citations (8.7 mean judged citations per answer over a 10-chunk
context).

\textbf{Model configuration, for reproducibility.} All pipeline LLM calls
(doc2query synthesis, relation adjudication, context disambiguation,
question phrasing) use Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (AWQ 4-bit quantization) served
with vLLM behind a round-robin router over seven GPUs. The endpoint
exposes it under the served-model alias \texttt{qwen3.5-35b}, which
predates the current weights---the version numbers genuinely differ
between alias and weights; the deployed model is the 3.6 weights.
Embeddings are Qwen3-Embedding-8B---native 4{,}096-d,
Matryoshka-truncated to 1{,}024 \textbf{on both the index and the query
side} (the deployed kNN indexes store 1{,}024-d vectors; the truncation
was adopted after an ablation measured zero fusion-level change). The
deep-lane cross-encoder reranker is Qwen3-Reranker-4B for every
experiment dated through 2026-10-04---including the hard-set component
separation (Section~\ref{sec:autopsy}) and the deep-lane adoption
bundle---and OneJev-4B (OmniJev's public vLLM release on Hugging Face,
\url{https://huggingface.co/OmniJev/OneJev-4B})
for the 2026-10-05 production validation whose numbers appear alongside
that date. The closed-book control of Section~\ref{sec:bench} uses the
same 35B pipeline LLM (temperature 0); a stronger closed-book model would
make that control stricter, and we did not run one.

\textbf{Cost profile.} For a 300-document nuclear slice (29.6M characters),
full enrichment ${\approx}$1\,h dominated by 18{,}454 doc2query LLM calls at
concurrency 70; dictionary stages are negligible. This contrasts with
graph-RAG construction over the same corpus: HippoRAG~2 required 4.1\,h, and
LightRAG needed 13\,h to complete an 82-document subset (serial entity
merge at ${\sim}$1 document/hour after the first 47; the 300-document tier
extrapolates to 1--2 days and was not attempted)---a scale wall we report
as a finding.

\subsection{Governance}

Entity assets mutate only through a four-stage governance loop---\emph{scan}
(vector-neighbour and name-containment candidate generation, with a type
guard), \emph{adjudicate} (conservative LLM rulings; a FLIP is accepted only
when the self-reported direction and the label agree, otherwise the pair is
downgraded to KEEP), \emph{merge} (reversible batches recorded in
\texttt{merge\_log} with score, rationale, and before/after snapshots; link
endpoints are redirected so \texttt{link\_id} survives), and \emph{replay}
(reverse-order rollback restoring MySQL and the graph store consistently).
Every mutation is thus auditable and reversible by construction.

Three measured outcomes anchor the governance claim (detail in the
released evaluation log): (i)~the LLM adjudicator \emph{failed its own
pre-registered gate}---merge precision 90.0\% on a 50-pair audit against
a $\geq$96\% gate, so automatic merging remains disabled; (ii)~the actual
duplicate population was cleared by deterministic rules alone---92
accent-variant pairs merged in 19 reversible batches with exact ledger
reconciliation and zero retrieval regression (R@10 0.9722 unchanged);
(iii)~dictionary governance is batch-reviewed, not silent---one batch
deleted 383 over-generic terms and re-typed 3{,}589 mentions, raising
entity typing precision from 58.3\% to 81.7\%.

The governance position: LLM adjudication is treated as a \textbf{candidate
generator, not an authority}---it proposes, pre-registered gates dispose,
and deterministic rules are preferred wherever they cover the case. The 90\%
gate failure is reported as a finding, not hidden, because the system's audit
trail is what makes the failure measurable in the first place.

\section{Retrieval Channels and Ablations}
\label{sec:channels}

\subsection{Channel definitions}

The retrieval planner (Fig.~\ref{fig:planner}) fuses ranked chunk lists from
capability-gated channels via weighted reciprocal rank
fusion~\cite{rrf}:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{score}(c) = \sum_{i} \frac{w_i}{60 + \mathrm{rank}_i(c)},
\end{equation}
where $w_i$ is the per-channel weight (default 1.0). Five channels
participate in the default profile:

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../A线-figures/latex_redraw/p1_retrieval_planner.pdf}
\caption{Capability- and intent-gated retrieval with document-aware RRF
fusion. The normal lane returns fused evidence directly; the deep lane
adds PPR, reranking and document-entity context. Graph bridge chains enrich
the evidence output rather than casting fusion votes. Text vectors use
the deployed 1024-dimensional client-side prefix. Lane-specific settings
are isolated.}
\label{fig:planner}
\end{figure*}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Lexical}: OpenSearch BM25 over chunk text with schema term
boosts.
\item \textbf{Dense}: kNN over chunk vectors (Qwen3-Embedding-8B;
1{,}024-d Matryoshka-truncated on both index and query sides, native
4{,}096-d; HNSW).
\item \textbf{Synq}: kNN over synthetic queries (doc2query, ${\sim}3$ per
chunk, 18{,}454 for the test corpus) matched against the user query vector.
\item \textbf{Entity/Cooc}: dictionary-driven entity mentions $\to$
document-level entity co-coverage vote (intent-gated: template words +
$\geq$2 entity surfaces). This path is \emph{adaptive}, not a fixed
extra vote list: after initial fusion it checks whether the visible
result intersects the co-occurrence document set, conditionally re-runs
fusion with a restricted lexical candidate list, and may insert up to
three passages at the tail if the leading co-occurrence document
remains absent. A cooc on/off comparison therefore measures this whole
path (restricted re-fusion plus insertion), not the marginal value of
one additional RRF vote source.
\item \textbf{Docvec}: kNN over title/abstract vectors $\to$
first-chunk proxy vote.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Intent-gated channel weighting}

Static equal weights are sub-optimal in a domain-dependent manner: a
global synq $\times$1.4 boost gains +4.2pt association R@10 but costs
$-$7.7pt on real queries (rejected); the same boost intent-gated keeps an
association gain at zero real-query cost (adopted); the docvec channel
adds +5pt R@10 and +6pt MRR on real queries with its association weight
down-weighted to 0.6 (adopted); and a lexical down-weight for English
non-association queries lifts the public benchmarks by 5--17pt (adopted).
Variant-level numbers are in the released evaluation log; the English-side
fusion effect these gates respond to is quantified next.

The intent gate (template-word regex + $\geq$2 dictionary entity surfaces) is
deliberately lightweight (zero LLM calls) and reproducible across domains:
Chinese association templates (同时涉及/关联检索) and English equivalents
(research both / intersection of) trigger the same reweighting path.

\textbf{Gate validity (confusion analysis, two domains).} Because routing
heuristics that key on named entities are known to over-trigger on multi-hop
queries, we quantify the gate directly against question-set labels
(association sets = positive, real-query sets = negative;
\texttt{gate\_eval.py}, predicates byte-identical to the service). On the
nuclear-electronics set (48 positive / 39 negative), the double gate
(template $\wedge$ $\geq$2 surfaces) attains precision 1.000 / recall 0.854 /
F1 0.921; the entity-surface gate alone attains precision 0.562 (32/39
over-triggered). On a site-contamination set (34/31) the double gate is
perfect (P/R/F1 = 1.000). The template-word stage is what prevents the
documented over-routing failure mode: the entity gate alone fires on
82--97\% of natural queries in both domains. All 7 residual false negatives
are dictionary-coverage gaps (non-entity concepts), not gate-logic errors.
The English non-assoc gate triggers on 98.5\% of 600 benchmark queries
with a 2.3\% false-trigger rate on Chinese sets. Sample sizes are small (48/39 and 34/31 labelled
questions): the perfect cells are point estimates on tens of
questions---the Wilson 95\% CI for csh recall (34/34) is [0.90, 1.00]---and
we read 1.000 as ``no error observed in-sample'', not as an error bound.

\subsection{The fusion-versus-dense finding}

Across five independent experiments (three English benchmarks at n=1{,}000
each + two Chinese question sets), equal-weight multi-channel fusion
changes \emph{where} evidence appears in the English multi-hop lists
rather than \emph{whether} it appears by depth 10. At the top of the
list fusion trails the dense channel (any-hit@2
$-$0.7/$-$0.7/$-$5.1pt on HotpotQA/2Wiki/MuSiQue) and loses MRR@10
($-$1.1/$-$1.4/$-$3.6pt); at depth 10 the any-hit gap vanishes
(+0.6/0.0/$-$0.1pt) and support coverage is marginally \emph{higher}
for fusion (cov@10 +0.9/+0.1/+0.8pt). On the Chinese question sets the
same comparison reverses at the top of the list (nea R@10 +2pt, MRR
+14pt, CI excludes 0; real queries $-$3pt R@10/+3pt MRR, NS). The
English panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:forest} plots the any-hit@2
differences.

These are \textbf{distribution-dependent} observations, not a language
effect: the English and Chinese sets differ simultaneously in language,
task type, gold construction, document length and lexicon conditions,
and isolating language would require paired bilingual questions over
the same literature. The mechanism reading: the lexical vote drags the
top of English multi-hop rankings (our lexical channel alone reaches
any-hit@2 84.9/90.8/60.2 against dense's 94.6/99.7/85.7), while on
Chinese domain queries the channels provide complementary signal where
each one lives: synq on the scoped benchmark sets (synq-only hits 4
questions dense misses on nea) and the co-occurrence path on
naturally-phrased association queries (contribution-matrix row 2---the
scoped sets are its gate-closed regime). The per-domain gate is the
design response, not the removal of channels.

\subsection{Evaluation methodology}

All ablations use: identical corpus (isolated test stack), identical embedder
(Qwen3-Embedding-8B), identical question sets, and paired bootstrap 95\%
CIs. Two metric families are reported, and they consume
\emph{different output budgets}---we state the definitions because the
choice moves numbers: \textbf{any-hit@k} and \textbf{MRR@10} are
computed over the first $k$ \emph{emitted passages} (passage budget),
while \textbf{cov@k} $=$ $|\mathrm{gold} \cap \mathrm{top}\text{-}k| /
|\mathrm{gold}|$ deduplicates the full returned list first and then
takes the top-$k$ \emph{documents} (document budget): if the first ten
passages come from five documents, the document budget still consumes
passages beyond position ten, and the passage budget does not. On one
archived baseline run (LlamaIndex, nea, n=48) the two coverage
definitions disagree on 14/48 questions---the budget is a reporting
obligation, not a formality. MRR ranks are passage ranks of the first
gold-document hit under the passage budget. For external comparisons we
do not rename any-hit as passage recall: the reference multi-hop
protocol evaluates supporting-passage-set recall under its own corpus,
deduplication and truncation conventions
(Section~\ref{sec:results}). RRF configuration changes require explicit
cache invalidation (documented after it invalidated results twice).

\textbf{Status of the question sets.} The scoped nea48/ner39 sets have
been reused across development rounds---channel weights, gates and the
emission policy were selected against them---so we treat them as
\emph{development and regression evidence}, not untouched confirmatory
tests, and frame their results as effects under stated profiles. The
natural-phrasing 99-question set and the English public benchmarks have
seen less selection; a genuinely untouched confirmatory set remains
future work.

\textbf{Cluster-robust significance.} Queries generated from the same corpus
are not independent, so per-query bootstrap CIs understate variance when sets
are pooled. We therefore report every pooled comparison under two concurrent
standards: per-query bootstrap CI, and a cluster-level interval over query
sets (cluster bootstrap for $k\geq5$ clusters; cluster-mean t interval for
$k<5$, since the bootstrap degenerates to a $k^k$-point discrete distribution
there and would inflate significance). Cluster-level significance requires
all cluster means to share sign \emph{and} the interval to exclude zero.
Under this discipline the synq+cooc channel gain survives: pooled over four
question sets (two domains, n=152), R@10 +0.250---per-query CI [0.184,
0.322], cluster t CI [0.059, 0.437], all four cluster means positive.
Effects measured on two domains only (e.g.\ the docvec channel,
+2.1/+5.1pt, sign-consistent across both sets) are reported with the explicit
caveat that cluster-robust confirmation needs more domains.

\textbf{Multiple comparisons.} The campaign reports on the order of forty
paired comparisons. We do not apply a family-wise or Benjamini--Hochberg
correction across that family; at the 95\% level roughly two nominally
significant results would be expected by chance alone, and we ask the
reader to weight accordingly: single-CI findings are treated as
suggestive, while corroborated findings---cluster-robust, sign-consistent
across independent question families, or mechanistically attributed
(e.g.\ the failure autopsies below)---carry the paper's claims. Stated as
a split rather than a caveat: the better-supported results of this paper
are retrospective paired evidence---the cluster-robust doc-fusion gain,
the sign-consistent docvec and alias effects, the co-occurrence
channel marginal with its win/loss record, and the
mechanistically-attributed failure autopsies; everything
else---single-CI deltas, two-cluster intervals, bundle-level
comparisons---is \emph{exploratory} and read as directional. Two
boundaries on the stronger tier: cluster-level intervals and
sign-consistency do not remove the selection bias introduced by
choosing configurations on these same sets (Section~5.4, question-set
status), and the four question sets span two domains, not four
independent domains---cross-domain confirmation would require domains
unused during development. We therefore label all archived results
\emph{development and regression evidence}; a confirmatory tier would
require freezing a small set of primary comparisons, metrics and
thresholds, then evaluating on questions that played no role in
configuration selection---not yet done.

\subsection{Failure attribution: where missed golds are lost}
\label{sec:autopsy}

A channel-isolated autopsy of every missed gold document (97 on nea, 71 on
ner) locates the failing layer: each of the four channels is probed directly
at the engine level (top-100, bypassing the service's channel mixing) and
cross-referenced against the fused ranking (Table~\ref{tab:loss};
Fig.~\ref{fig:failambda}, panel a).

\textbf{93--97\% of misses survive candidate generation.} The channels
find the documents somewhere in their top-100; the loss occurs
\emph{after} candidate generation, in fusion and
consolidation---chunk-level RRF cannot consolidate document-level
support. Smoking-gun case: one missed gold
document is dense-rank-5 (its chunk \#47), synq-rank-31 (chunk \#4), and
docvec-rank-1 (first chunk \#0)---three channels vote for three
\emph{different} chunks of the same document, each vote individually too weak
to surface any chunk into the top-20. The docvec channel is a
FirstP-style document signal in the MaxP/FirstP family~\cite{maxp}, but its single
first-chunk vote does not merge with votes landing on other chunks.

This attribution extends component-level RAG
diagnosis~\cite{ragchecker} to the \textbf{fusion layer} and motivated
doc-aware fusion (post-RRF document-level consolidation of chunk
scores), which targets the loss stage \emph{after candidate generation}.
The autopsy establishes where the loss occurs (candidates exist; the
final list does not contain them); it does not by itself prove that
granularity mismatch causes every such loss---in-pool ranking noise and
truncation are alternatives---and how many missed golds the truncated
aggregation actually recovers is answered by the frozen-score replay
below. The measured gains of the adopted $\lambda{=}2$
configuration are the empirical support for the aggregation component.
Dictionary/enrichment coverage gaps are real but secondary.

\textbf{Constructive validation on a hard-target set.} We instrument the
dilution layer as a question-set constraint: 37 questions drafted from
non-abstract passages with lexical detachment ($\leq$15\% 8-gram copy rate)
and accepted only if the gold is visible in some channel's top-100 yet
absent from the fused top-10. \textbf{This is a mechanism probe on a
purpose-built adverse distribution, not a representative benchmark}:
questions were selected precisely because the deployed base endpoint fails
them (acceptance rate 6.2\% over 600 drafts on the full production corpus),
so absolute numbers on this set quantify the recovery mechanism
(displacement and its repair), not general retrieval quality.
On this distribution the dilution phenomenon reproduces constructively:
single-dense retrieval already ranks 37.8\% of golds into top-10 (MRR 0.167)
while the fused base endpoint returns 0.000---multi-channel voting displaces
strong single-channel signals on lexically-detached phrasing. BM25 collapses
to zero on all metrics (the detachment gate seals the lexical path by
design). The deep endpoint recovers 62.2\% into top-10 (binomial 95\% CI
[0.45, 0.77], median recovered rank 1). Component separation on the same
set: removing the cross-encoder rerank drops hit@10 from 0.57 to 0.16
(rerank dominates recovery); the PPR graph expansion adds a positive but
modest +2.7pt (37/37 queries fire, p50 +1.9\,s). Together with the
90\%-edge-deletion result (Section~\ref{sec:eng}), this yields the paper's
positioning of graph structure: \textbf{graph assets empower the vector
hybrid} (dictionary $\to$ intent gating and seeding; bilingual aliases $\to$
+7.6pt real-query cov@20; entity-link co-occurrence $\to$ the
association-channel gains), while structural traversal contributes only at
the margin and only inside the budget-constrained deep lane.

\textbf{Doc-aware fusion: score aggregation and evidence emission are two
separate decisions.} Doc-aware fusion is a \emph{post-fusion} re-ranking
of the chunk-level RRF ranking---not a per-channel pre-aggregation; the
deployed rule, stated precisely. (1)~Chunk-level weighted RRF runs
first, $\mathrm{score}(c)=\sum_i w_i/(60+\mathrm{rank}_i(c))$ over the
raw channel lists, so a chunk receiving votes from several channels
already carries their sum. (2)~The top $\max(3k,20)$ chunks of that
fused ranking form the candidate pool, grouped by document; a document
whose passages fall outside the pool cannot be recovered by the
aggregation. (3)~A document's score is the sum of its top-$\lambda$
\emph{chunk-level RRF scores within that pool}---with $\lambda{=}2$, at
most \emph{two} passage scores contribute, by construction. In the
smoking-gun case the three scattered votes (dense on chunk~\#47, synq
on chunk~\#4, docvec on chunk~\#0) enter the pool as three
separately-scored chunks, and the document score aggregates the two
highest of them---the third adds nothing under $\lambda{=}2$. The
example must be read as truncated aggregation, not a three-chunk sum;
it resolves because two aggregated votes already outrank any
single-chunk competitor. (4)~Documents are sorted by this sum and
emitted under a separate \emph{emission policy}: \emph{pair} emits up
to $\lambda$ passages of the first document before the next document;
\emph{stride} emits the first passage of every ranked document before
any second passage. Pair and stride share identical document scores and
differ only in how the output budget is spent---pair buys evidence
adjacency at the cost of document diversity within the first $k$
passages. The aggregation rule itself is a truncated sum of
passage-level RRF scores---the RRF analogue of the established
MaxP/SumP document-aggregation family~\cite{maxp}; document aggregation
is not new, and we claim only the truncated-pool variant, the separated
emission policy, and the failure-layer diagnosis that motivates both.

\textbf{Measured arms, labeled by configuration.} The $\lambda$ sweep
of Fig.~\ref{fig:failambda}(b) was measured under \emph{pair} emission
on the scoped sets, as configuration exploration: $\lambda$=1 lifts
cov@20 (+5.0pt, per-query CI [0.023, 0.078] significant, cluster
interval wide) with any-hit@10 unchanged and cov@10 $-$1.3pt (NS);
\textbf{$\lambda$=2 under pair emission} lifts cov@10 +6.28pt under
both CI standards (cluster-t interval [0.029, 0.097], both clusters
positive---the campaign's first cluster-robust gain; k=2 clusters, so
the interval is fragile and the claim campaign-scoped, not field-level)
at $-$4.6pt any-hit@10; $\lambda$=3 overshoots (cov@10 +7.4pt
per-query-significant but not cluster-robust; cov@20 $-$2.8pt, any-hit
$-$5.8pt). Pair-$\lambda$2 was first adopted gated on association
intent (association cov@10 0.569$\to$0.633, real queries unchanged).
The production adoption then changed the \emph{emission policy}:
\textbf{$\lambda{=}2$ with stride emission, applied globally}.
Relative to the pair configuration, stride returns the top-of-list
concession (association R@10 +4.2pt, MRR +3.6pt, cov@20 +4.7pt) while
giving back 2.7pt of association cov@10; relative to the pre-adoption
chunk-level baseline it shows no regression on any reported metric on
either question family, with single-intent coverage gains (cov@10
+4.1pt, cov@20 +7.1pt) carried through. A structural caution on that
$-$2.7pt: with a fixed candidate pool, fixed document scores, an output
of at least twenty passages and no downstream insertion or filtering,
pair and stride expose the same top-ranked documents, so
document-budget cov@10 should not differ between them. \textbf{The
frozen-score replay confirms exactly this.} We re-captured the channel
rankings behind the final-configuration scoped runs (tee on the fusion
entry point) and re-ran the production aggregation/emission code itself
over seven arms: F0 (chunk-RRF, $\lambda{=}0$) and
$\lambda\in\{1,2,3\}\times$\{pair, stride\}. Validity check: the
$\lambda{=}2$+stride arm reproduces the archived live run
\emph{bitwise} (nea 0.5993/0.9375/0.7346 cov@10/R@10/MRR). Results:
(i)~\emph{aggregation} at frozen scores contributes cov@10 $+$0.9pt
(nea, ns) / $+$4.9pt (ner, CI [$+$1.1, $+$9.2]) at $\lambda{=}2$,
with $\lambda{=}1$ significant on both sets ($+$1.9/$+$4.4) and
cov@20 $+$14.0/$+$11.1pt; (ii)~\emph{emission order} moves passage
budgets only: stride over pair buys any-hit@10 $+$10.3pt (ner, CI
[$+$2.6, $+$20.5]) and MRR $+$4.8/$+$3.0pt (both significant) while
the cov@10 difference is \emph{exactly 0.000 on both sets}---the
budget identity holds, so the adoption-window $-$2.7pt was
configuration co-variation, not an emission-order effect; (iii)~the
$\lambda{=}3$ overshoot reproduces (any-hit $-$8.3/$-$10.3pt under
pair, repaired by stride). Because the historical $\lambda$ sweep was
measured against a pre-adoption baseline, its larger pooled $+$6.28pt
carries that window's configuration context; under the final
configuration, aggregation alone accounts for the smaller frozen-score
deltas above. On corpora with one chunk per
document---the public multi-hop benchmarks---the rule is structurally
inert: toggling it reproduces the baseline \emph{bitwise} on
HotpotQA/2Wiki and within $\pm$0.002 (NS) on MuSiQue (600 paired
questions), so its effects are a long-document-corpus phenomenon.

\subsection{Design}

canonical-entity $\times$ entity-link co-occurrence (gold $=$ documents

questions from actual usage logs); \textbf{Title lookups} (net, exact title
retrieval; saturating control).

\subsection{Benchmark validity controls}

Three controls and one robustness round establish that the question sets
measure retrieval rather than artifact (\textbf{closed-book}), that the
top-$k$ under-count component of cov@k is bounded (\textbf{unjudged
audit}), that construction circularity is bounded rather than denied
(\textbf{circularity bounds}), and that the system ordering survives
independently-judged gold extension (\textbf{pooled round}):

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Closed-book control} (HybridRAG-Bench protocol): asking the LLM
directly which papers answer each question---without retrieval---yields a
gold-document naming rate of \textbf{0/48 (nea) and 0/39 (ner)} (86/87
answers are ``don't know''). The gold documents are domain journal papers; no correct gold naming
was observed for this model under this prompt protocol. Whether the
papers appeared in the model's training data is unknown---the control
bounds memorization-based solving \emph{as tested}, not for all models
and protocols.

\item \textbf{Unjudged audit} (topic-specific re-judging): we re-judge a
sample of top-10 retrieved but unjudged (non-gold) documents under a strict
evidence-quotation protocol---the judge receives each entity's full
bilingual name set (canonical + aliases) and must quote one passage of
original text per entity side; a ``relevant'' verdict additionally requires
programmatic verification that both quotes are present in the document.
Result: 18/203 sampled unjudged documents on nea carry verified both-entity
evidence---admitting them as gold would raise measured coverage by
\textbf{$\leq$+8.1pt (nea) and $\leq$+2.4pt (ner)}. We phrase this
deliberately: cov@k is coverage of the \emph{constructed gold set}, not an
estimate of full-collection recall---relevant documents absent from the gold
set altogether would raise the denominator too, and without complete
annotation the direction of the true-recall difference cannot be signed.
The audit bounds only the under-count of already-retrieved relevant
documents inside top-$k$. A naive one-word relevance
protocol over-estimates the correction 3$\times$ (+24.7pt) and is falsified
by a surface cross-check (0/76 of its ``relevant'' verdicts have both entity
surfaces in the document)---we report this as a measurement methodology
finding: unjudged re-judging without entity name sets and verifiable quotes
is not trustworthy.

\item \textbf{Generation circularity (acknowledged and bounded).} The
association question sets are generated from the same entity-pair
co-occurrence assets that the co-occurrence channel consumes---the gold
criterion (documents covering both entities) coincides with the channel's
mechanism, so association-set numbers partially measure the asset they
were built from. We bound rather than deny this: (a)~the closed-book
control above rules out memorization, not circularity; (b)~the
natural-phrasing variants (template words banned) still show the
co-occurrence path's +10.1pt hit@10 gain (CI [+5.1, +16.2]; the whole
adaptive path, historical deployment profile)---the
channel works through
dictionary-surface hits in the query, not template-word leakage; (c)~single-intent real queries define
gold by information need with no entity-pair construction; and (d)~the
English public benchmarks involve none of our assets. Additionally, a
single-variable channel ablation on the scoped sets under the final
configuration shows the entity-dependent channels leave the @10 metrics
unchanged there (the only movements are a +0.0006 MRR shift and a
$-$1.0pt cov@20 change, both NS; next section)---the benchmark is not
being carried by entity fusion votes under that profile. The bound is
profile-specific: a channel whose gate never opens cannot demonstrate
its value through a removal test, so this null does not extend to the
distributions where the gates fire. Corpus-construction-level claims
(bilingual aliases, dictionary coverage) remain partially circular, and
we label them as such where they appear.

\item \textbf{Pooled independent-judgment round (automated).} A
TREC-style pooling check on the least-circular set (39 production real
queries): the union of five systems' top-10 predictions (437 documents
outside the constructed gold) was judged under the strict
quote-verification protocol; 9 documents on 6 questions were judged
relevant (3 with programmatically verified quotes). Under the resulting
pooled gold, \emph{every} system's any-hit@10 is unchanged (KNC 0.949,
HippoRAG~2 0.923, LlamaIndex 0.897, BM25 0.872; genrag $+$1 question to
0.897)---the system ordering on real queries is robust to
independently-judged gold extension. \emph{Disclosure:} judged by the
pipeline LLM (temperature 0), not human annotators; a small human round
remains the outstanding item.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Controlled ablation on the nuclear-electronics domain}

Post-rebuild refresh (2026-10-09): after the schema-lexicon repair
(Section~\ref{sec:incident}) the scoped arms were re-run under the

0.533$\to$\textbf{0.599}, single-intent 0.603$\to$\textbf{0.675}, MRR

lexicon repair); we bound it with a paired channel ablation under the

\textbf{On the @10 metrics the removal is a null}: R@10 and cov@10 are

(0.7695$\to$0.7701, CI [0, 0.0018]) and by zero on association; the
largest movement anywhere is associative cov@20 $-$1.0pt (95\% CI
[$-$3.1, 0.0]); deeper or secondary metrics are not claimed identical.
(Two runs of the nominally identical full configuration differ
slightly: the ablation's own all-arm reads single-intent cov@10 0.670
against the refresh run's 0.675, so we quote the ablation's paired arms
throughout.) p50 latency drops from 1.46\,s to 0.52\,s (association)
and 1.54\,s to 0.51\,s (single-intent)---a two-thirds reduction, not a
halving. What this intervention establishes is narrow: under the final
retrieval profile, the direct entity-dependent channels are not a
necessary contributor to the @10 levels of these sets. It does not
isolate the effects of the repaired lexicon through aliases, reranker
context, or answer construction, and therefore cannot attribute the
repair-window trajectory to configuration changes alone. On these
benchmark families the entity layer's retrieval-facing value does not
run through fusion votes under this profile; the remaining candidate
paths---lexical alias expansion (entity names as BM25 terms), deep-lane
reranker context, and generation-side knowledge chains---are consistent
with the graph-governance nulls reported later in this section, but
their individual contributions are not separately quantified by this
ablation.

\textbf{Why they ship enabled.} The operations answer, stated as such:
(1)~the benchmark families under-represent the traffic the channels
insure against---naturally-phrased association queries are real usage
(the 99-question set exists because of them) and hard queries recover via
deep-lane PPR (+2.7pt); (2)~the graph channel serves the evidence surface
(knowledge chains in output) regardless of ranking; (3)~the deep lane
consumes entity assets (reranker context, chain evidence for
generation); (4)~p50 1.46\,s sits inside the deployed SLO with the
channels on. A latency-first deployment could gate the normal lane's
entity channels off at zero measured benchmark cost---a configuration
decision the ablation makes available, not a necessity claim we retreat
from.

Fig.~\ref{fig:scalefig} plots the scale trajectory; the data boundary is

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Production-scale schema-quality incident and repair}
\label{sec:incident}

Enriching 61{,}137 documents with a \emph{category-word} schema lexicon
collapsed the entity layer to 575 type-level vertices carrying ${\sim}$19.2M
links (``method'', ``energy'' as entities) while every job-level monitor
stayed green. A three-source, corpus-attested term lexicon (3{,}702 entries)
and a full-chain rebuild restored it to 1{,}871 canonical entities with
25.1M links and a functioning relation layer (44{,}043 typed relations, 1.56M
evidence rows); scoped retrieval over the repair window moved +6.6/+7.3
cov@10 (system-level attribution; the repair-window trajectory cannot
be attributed to configuration changes alone, Section~\ref{sec:results}). The top-linked entities after
repair remain generic short symbols (admission without ranking). The
repair also surfaced secondary engineering failures whose forensics
belong to the companion lexicon manuscript; the finding that matters
here is the monitoring lesson. During a link-writer bug, jobs,
throughput and entity creation all read healthy while the asset was
being written into a per-query-invisible state, and the original
dashboard-derived duration narrative was later corrected by the
surviving rows' own timestamps---job-level heartbeats measure work, not
asset state, and ``DONE'' is indistinguishable from ``DONE with correct
rows'' at that layer. The checks that catch this class are row-level:
registry growth alarms keyed to ingested-document counts, dictionary
hit-rate sampling, and affected-row accounting beside every bulk
\texttt{INSERT IGNORE}---adopted as hard rules after the incident.
Full incident forensics and lexicon methodology: companion lexicon
manuscript.

Two distribution-conditional findings connect directly to RQ2 and are

\paragraph{LLM query understanding on the deep lane.} A single LLM call
per deep request classifies intent and extracts entity surfaces, overriding
the template-word intent gate (natural-phrasing coverage 4/70) and feeding
co-occurrence candidates. Verdict quality is high (48/48 template-assoc
correctly labelled), yet on the target distribution of naturally-phrased
association questions the hit count is unchanged (0 wins, 0 losses; MRR
$-$0.04) and hard factual questions lose 2.2pt from assoc-mechanism false
triggers: the bottleneck for natural association queries is not intent
detection. Note what this ablation does \emph{not} show: at run time the
co-occurrence machinery could not fire on the queried surfaces at all
(trigger counts identical across arms---the surfaces were absent from the
dictionary), so ``better intent detection buys no hits'' and ``the
association channel has no value'' are two different claims, only the
first of which is measured here. The dictionary backfill and channel-side
ablation that separate the two causes follow below
(Table~\ref{tab:naturalbridge}). Rejected;
the understanding slot stays available for trained rewriters.

\paragraph{Graph-side mechanisms and asset completions---one
positioning, consolidated.} Graph-asset audits (87\% of entity--relation
edges are bibliographic authorship stars; the doc--entity bipartite is the
densest asset) motivated three governance mechanisms, a deep-lane retune
and dictionary backfill; their distribution-conditional effects are
tabulated in Table~\ref{tab:channmatrix} and Fig.~\ref{fig:forest}, so we
state only the verdicts here. Structural traversal does not pay in
ranking: typed-edge PPR governance and dense seed fallback are
\emph{bitwise identical} to baseline (PPR's total contribution sits below
the deep lane's sensitivity floor), and the doc-level bipartite PPR
channel is significantly negative (nea hit@10 $-$4.2pt---the bipartite
asset's value is already captured by the co-occurrence channel's exact
entity-pair intersection). The deep-lane retune (\textbf{adopted}) drops
the docvec channel, whose votes push co-occurrence winners out of the
fusion pool (nea hit@20 $-$4.2pt), and feeds each reranker candidate its
top linked document entities, letting graph assets reach the ranking
surface directly (nea hit@10 +6.2pt, 0.896$\to$0.958; 4 of 6 metrics
significant, none negative). Dictionary backfill (246 corpus-verified

\subsection{Engineering findings}
\label{sec:eng}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Filtered HNSW robustness}: 16\% foreign-corpus vectors in a
shared index cause zero steady-state degradation of the dense channel
(measured over 3 days, 98 prediction files). Write-window rank jitter
($\pm$6pt arm-level on borderline questions) is a transient, not a permanent
loss.

\item \textbf{LightRAG serial-merge wall}: in this implementation and configuration
(LightRAG 1.4.13, single-process merge), entity merging degraded to
${\sim}$1 doc/hour after the first 47 docs of an 82-document subset
(11.5k chunks, 13k+ entities). LLM extraction concurrency (16$\to$70)
helps; merge does not. A single implementation/version/configuration
observation, not a complexity characterization of the family.

\item \textbf{Link-edge mass is redundant}: randomly deleting 30/60/90\% of
the 158{,}057 entity links of the test KB causes no statistically
significant retrieval change (nea at 90\% deletion: R@10 $-$2.1pt, cov@10
$-$2.7pt, all CI include zero; ner bitwise unchanged). No link-mass effect was detected at this configuration and sample
size; edge \emph{quality} was not manipulated, so a
quality-over-quantity law is not established. Link value in this system
anchors in the structural surfaces (governance, navigation, evidence
panels), not in the fusion vote.

\item \textbf{Schema binding is the root of the entity channel}: binding a
corpus to the \texttt{generic} schema yields 14 canonical entities;
rebinding to \texttt{nuclear\_electronics} yields 1{,}166---an 83$\times$
difference with zero code changes, purely from the dictionary.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Safety-relevant failure modes}

In nuclear-domain analysis a missed or wrong passage can propagate into
safety-relevant conclusions, so we state the failure model explicitly.
In \emph{offline} evaluation, misses are measured against the
constructed gold (cov@k, any-hit); \emph{online} there is no gold, so
unmeasured misses are not knowable from the system's own output---an
absence of reported gaps is not evidence of absence. Wrong-passage risk
is not eliminated by infrastructure: mandatory provenance makes every
returned claim \emph{checkable} at read time (a verification
affordance for analysts, not a quantified bound on wrong-answer risk);
the audits bound different things---Section~4.2's programmatic audit
measures lookup integrity, and the citation-faithfulness round measures
how often generated citations carry supportive evidence (75.1\%
faithful-or-partial, an automated judgment with disclosed limits). The
documented production failure was
asset-side---a collapsed dictionary---and capability bits do degrade
quietly by design, a deliberate trade now monitored at row level
(Section~8.1). The system is an evidence-delivery layer for analysts,
not an authoritative source; traceability is the safety mechanism, and
uncited claims should be treated as unsupported.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Single-corpus domain (nuclear electronics) for the primary ablation;
English benchmarks provide external validity but not domain depth.
\item Synquestion generation requires LLM access---doc2query dominates the
bill (${\sim}$3 calls per chunk, ${\sim}$62 per document at our
${\sim}$1{,}200-character chunking; 18{,}454 calls for the 300-document
slice), plus relation adjudication; offline corpora need a pre-processing
pass.
\item The intent gate is regex-based; LLM-based query classification could
improve coverage but adds latency (SelRoute: 83\% accuracy with regex).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Conclusion}

This study examined fusion granularity and semantic-asset use in
bilingual nuclear-literature retrieval. The archived evidence shows
conditional channel effects---direct entity-dependent retrieval adds
nothing measurable on the scoped development sets, while the adaptive
co-occurrence path gains +10.1pt any-hit@10 on naturally phrased
association questions---and motivates separating candidate
availability, document-level score aggregation, and passage emission:
the frozen-score F0--F3 replay now separates them (aggregation
$+$4.9pt cov@10 on single-intent; emission order $+$10.3pt any-hit@10
at exactly zero document-budget cost). Source audits
distinguish addressability (500/500 spans, 499/500 hashes) from the
semantic support of generated citations (59.6\% faithful under
automated judgment); both are needed and neither substitutes for the
other. The production deployment provides a large-scale setting for
these questions; the comparative conclusions remain limited to the
evaluated profiles, question distributions, and---for public
benchmarks---to within-system metric decompositions until a
protocol-aligned external rerun exists. Fixed-profile component
comparisons and independently assessed relevance are the outstanding
work.
