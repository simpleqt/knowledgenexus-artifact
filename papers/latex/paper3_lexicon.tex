\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background.} In dictionary-driven entity enrichment the schema
lexicon is effectively the extraction model. A category-word lexicon
silently collapsed the entity layer of a 61{,}137-document scholarly
knowledge base: 575 type-level entities (``method'', ``energy'')
carrying ${\sim}$19.2M links while every job-level monitor stayed
green. \textbf{Method.} We diagnose the failure across extraction
mechanism, lexicon provenance, and monitoring blindness; repair it with
a corpus-attested lexicon built by LLM classification of corpus
candidates under presence gating, deployed as a three-source recipe
(3{,}702 terms);
rebuild the entity layer under production load; and derive gold-free
monitors led by a link-concentration diagnostic invariant to uniform
rescaling of link counts. \textbf{Results.} A fixed-code, fixed-corpus
experiment that varies only the lexicon reproduces the collapse
signature alone (109$\pm$5 entities, top-10 concentration
0.513$\pm$0.016) and isolates the repair (692$\pm$27, 0.300$\pm$0.008;
ten resamples). The rebuilt layer carries 1{,}871 entities and 25.1M
links; distinct concepts per document rise 15.7$\to$37.8 with
annotated-document coverage 51.5\%$\to$98.5\%; a risk-stratified
300-link audit reads 0.84 overall. Matched spans are equally
``valid'' under old and new dictionaries (0.94 vs.\ 0.93)---the
collapse is a vocabulary-and-mass failure invisible to per-link
audits---and no single gold-free monitor suffices (a composite
four-leg rule reaches zero misses and zero false alarms on the
degradation archive). A three-domain replay on public corpora shows
the failure form and the repair travel: batch-schema lexicons
reproduce the collapse signature out of domain ($C_{10}$ up to 0.899,
silver recall ${\le}$0.009). \textbf{Limitations.} The generic tail
survives repair (9\% of links sit on the symbol ``be'');
retrieval-facing gains are not attributable to the lexicon alone;
annotation is model-based, bounded by single-author human verification
of a 197-term stratified sample (confirm 0.934); alarm bands are
post-hoc candidate thresholds.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Large scholarly knowledge platforms increasingly generate their subject
schemas with LLM assistance: a batch tool drafts entity types with a handful
of aliases each, a validator checks structural well-formedness, and the
schema is bound to knowledge bases as configuration (our own batch generator
is the primary failure source in this account; LLM-for-ontology generation is surveyed
in~\cite{llmonto}). This is attractive at scale---our deployment covers
80{,}855 parsed journal articles (80{,}852 active, 7{,}229{,}225 text
chunks) across two large knowledge bases and several smaller ones---and it
keeps humans out of a loop that would otherwise require domain
lexicographers per discipline.

This paper is about what that design decision does to the entity layer when
the enrichment pipeline extracts mentions by dictionary scan. In our system,
stage-K1 mention extraction is deliberately LLM-free: a schema's
\texttt{entity\_types} main names and aliases constitute the scan
dictionary. The choice is defensible---dictionary scan is cheap enough to
run over 7.2 million chunks, deterministic, idempotent under re-ingest---and
it performed adequately on the knowledge base whose schema had been
hand-evolved over years. It failed catastrophically, and silently, on the
two knowledge bases whose schemas came out of the batch generator.

The failure signature: a 61{,}137-document nuclear-electronics knowledge
base (104112E6) accumulated \textbf{19.2 million entity links onto 575
canonical entities}; the entities were not domain concepts but type
words---``method'', ``parameter'', ``energy''. A 13{,}408-document physics
knowledge base (693372D5) showed the same failure pattern at 2{,}018 entities.
Meanwhile every operational signal was green: enrichment jobs completed
normally, link volume read as growth, and retrieval endpoints answered. The
entity graph had degenerated into a bipartite ``type-to-document'' index
while presenting, to every surface-level check, as a healthy knowledge
graph.

We contribute:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A failure mode and its causal confirmation.} A forensic
record of entity collapse induced by schema-lexicon quality
(Section~\ref{sec:collapse}), elevated from correlation to cause by a
controlled lexicon-only experiment (Section~\ref{sec:after}): with
pipeline code and corpus both fixed, swapping the dictionary alone
reproduces the collapse signature and its repair across ten random
resamples of one corpus. The observables that expose the collapse are the
scale-invariant concentration of links on the top entities and the
top-entity name distribution, with entity cardinality as a
corroborating (and manipulable-in-both-directions) secondary signal.
\item \textbf{A corpus-attested lexicon lifecycle whose decisive
mechanism is LLM classification of corpus-frequency candidates under
corpus-presence gating, deployed through a full-chain rebuild at
production scale} (Sections~\ref{sec:lexicon}--\ref{sec:rebuild}):
the production recipe combines 2{,}284 transplanted seed terms from a
healthy near-domain library, 7{,}391 corpus-frequency candidates
classified by an LLM, and bounded per-type expansion---yielding
3{,}702 real terms for nuclear electronics (v2.1) and 1{,}279 for
physics (v1.1), 223 legacy conflicts repaired in passing; the
cross-domain ablation (Section~\ref{sec:xdomain}) attributes the
load-bearing share to candidate classification and presence gating,
with expansion retained as an optional diversity module. The rebuild
itself---vertex-and-table-scoped teardown and 766{,}501 re-enqueued
jobs---surfaced pipeline pathologies, each with a measured fix (most
dramatically a 137$\times$ link-throughput recovery once an $O(N^2)$
alias check, amplified by the richer lexicon itself, was cached).
\item \textbf{Gold-free entity-quality observability}
(Sections~\ref{sec:after}--\ref{sec:discussion}): before/after evidence
(cardinality 575$\to$1{,}871 with a functioning relation layer; scoped
retrieval trajectory +6.6/+7.3 cov@10 with a channel-level attribution
bound; a 300-link stratified audit), the four monitors---led by top-10
link concentration---calibrated on 13 production libraries, and a
corrected incident log showing that dashboard-derived incident
narratives themselves require row-level verification.
\end{enumerate}

The larger claim we defend: in dictionary-driven pipelines, \textbf{the
schema lexicon is not descriptive metadata but operative extraction
surface}. Treating it as configuration-file decoration---easily
batch-generated, rarely audited---is a structural mistake that this incident
renders measurable.

\section{Background: dictionary-driven enrichment}
\label{sec:background}

Our enrichment pipeline (four stages per document: mentions $\to$ links
$\to$ relations $\to$ graph) extracts mentions in stage K1 by normalized
dictionary scan: every schema alias match over chunk text yields a
\texttt{raw\_mention} with an entity-type hint; stage K2 links mentions to
canonical entities by normalized form + type; K3 lifts selected mention
pairs into typed relations with an LLM pass; K4 projects the per-document
entity subgraph into the graph store and search-side entity index.
Retrieval consumes all four: an entity channel seeds personalized PageRank,
a co-occurrence channel, and entity-vector similarity all trace back to
linked mentions.

Why no LLM in K1? Cost and determinism. At 7.2M chunks, a per-chunk LLM
extraction pass is a material recurring expense and a re-ingest hazard
(non-determinism breaks idempotent rebuild); a scan is $O(\mathrm{corpus})$
with zero marginal model cost. The design bets everything on one input: the
dictionary. That bet is invisible in the architecture diagram and total in
its consequences---a point Section~\ref{sec:collapse} makes empirical.

The healthy control in our own deployment: a hydrogeology knowledge base
whose schema was hand-evolved to 3{,}742 genuine domain terms (78 documents
$\to$ 1{,}608 canonical entities, well-distributed). The contrast cases:
schemas produced by our batch tool, whose output per entity type is a small
set of \emph{category} aliases (4--8 per type)---a taxonomy of the subject,
not a terminology of it (the distinction between terminological and
taxonomic vocabulary is classical~\cite{iso1087}, and the adjacent question
of how to measure knowledge-graph quality is itself an established
literature~\cite{paulheim}).

Neither half of our pipeline is novel in isolation. Dictionary- or
gazetteer-driven entity recognition is one of the oldest NER
traditions~\cite{nerSurvey}, and corpus-statistical term recognition with
linguistic evidence goes back to C-value/NC-value~\cite{cvalue}---our
corpus-candidate classification source (Section~\ref{sec:lexicon}) is that
automatic-term-recognition program reborn with an LLM in the classifier
seat, and recent work extracts terminology directly with
LLM-plus-retrieval pipelines~\cite{llmterm}. Automatic schema and KG
construction has meanwhile advanced to dynamic schema induction over
corpora (AutoSchemaKG~\cite{autoschemakg}), incremental schema evolution
(DIAL-KG~\cite{dialkg}), and joint evaluation of extraction quality,
schema adherence, and stability~\cite{schemakgeval}. Our subject is
complementary and upstream of a different risk: in \emph{dictionary-scan
deployment}, where the schema's alias list is the extraction surface,
we show that schema legality and even per-link mention validity can
coexist with asset-level semantic collapse---a failure locus those
methods do not measure, and one our diagnostics target directly; we
claim no better schema-induction or term-extraction algorithm.
On the term side, evaluation traditions for automatic term extraction
are annotation-guided and domain-stratified (TermEval/ACTER across
domains and languages~\cite{termeval}); our silver/dual-model
references follow that spirit at zero marginal human cost, with the
same caveats about instrument dependence. Diachronic analyses show
domain vocabularies drift measurably even without
failure~\cite{hamilton16}; our monitors treat lexical drift as one
more reason lexicon content, not just schema shape, needs standing
statistics. Likewise, detecting and repairing degraded
knowledge-graph content is an established concern~\cite{paulheim},
and production data-validation systems routinely gate model inputs
with schema- and statistics-level checks~\cite{tdd,deequ}. What this incident supplies is a specific failure locus: when the
dictionary \emph{is} the extraction model, a structurally valid but
terminologically hollow schema passed every gate \emph{this pipeline had
in place}---schema-shape validation, ingestion checks, job-level
monitoring. That is a statement about our gates, not about the prior
art: distribution-statistics monitoring is the right tradition, but the
statistic must be aimed at the lexicon's terminological content---none
of our gates asked whether a lexicon's entries are domain-specific
terms, the question term-recognition research takes as its starting
assumption. The monitors we propose (Section~\ref{sec:discussion})
target that acceptance gap.

\textbf{Layer 2 --- provenance.} The dictionaries were the schemas. The
batch generator, prompted to ``draft entity types with aliases for subject
X'', produced exactly that: type-level category vocabulary. Nothing in its
validator checks whether aliases are \emph{domain-specific}---structural
validity is the gate, and category words are structurally valid.

\textbf{Layer 3 --- the reference case.} Hydrogeology's 3{,}742
hand-evolved terms over 78 documents yield 1{,}608 entities. We call it
a reference case, not a controlled comparison: domain, scale, register,
and lexicon provenance all differ, so it establishes plausibility, not
causation --- which is what the isolated experiment of
Section~\ref{sec:after} (6.5) is for.

\textbf{Why nothing fired.} Job-level observability was all green (jobs
DONE, throughput nominal, error rate zero). Link volume---if anything---read
as \emph{success} (19.2M links!). The observables that expose the collapse
are (a)~the concentration of links on the top entities---the collapsed
state measures 0.419 with its top-10 entities being pure type
vocabulary, versus 0.18 for the healthy control
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}'s monitor~(a) gives the measurement and
its provenance)---and (b)~the name distribution of top-linked entities, which is
pure type vocabulary. Entity cardinality per document---a factor of 2{,}192
below the reference case (0.0094 vs 20.6), over three orders of
magnitude---corroborates, but cardinality is a coarse
signal gameable in both directions (the small-library treatment of
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} shows category-word aliases inflating it),
which is why it is the secondary, not the primary, monitor. Neither
observable is provided by standard pipeline dashboards by default. This
blindness generalizes to any dictionary-driven system whose dashboards
count jobs and rows.

\section{Corpus-Attested Lexicon Construction and Validation}
\label{sec:lexicon}

Figure~\ref{fig:lexicon-method} shows the three-source admission process
and its quality boundary.
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../A线-figures/latex_redraw/p3_lexicon_method.pdf}
\caption{Three-source terminology generation and deterministic enrichment.
Healthy seeds, classified corpus candidates and per-type expansion
proposals pass corpus-attestation, vocabulary, conflict-resolution and
structural gates before entering a versioned schema. The admitted
vocabulary drives mention scanning, canonical linking, typed-relation
adjudication and graph projection. Attestation verifies occurrence;
contextual sense and domain specificity still require an independent
link-quality audit.}
\label{fig:lexicon-method}
\end{figure}

The repair requirement: produce genuine domain terminology at scale, without
per-subject lexicographers, without hallucinated terms---and make it a
repeatable tool, not a one-off patch. Our generator implements three
sources, all gated:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Seed transplant (2{,}284 terms).} A healthy near-domain
library (the RADAI corpus knowledge base, same academic register) donates
its terms with types. Transplant is the highest-precision source; corpus
attestation below still applies.
\item \textbf{Corpus-candidate classification (7{,}391 candidates).}
High-frequency n-grams from the target corpus's own text are
proposed---concretely, the top 8{,}000 n-grams (unigrams and bigrams) by
chunk frequency over a 3{,}000-chunk-per-KB sample, keeping candidates with
frequency $\geq$5 after a boilerplate and stop-word filter (section
vocabulary such as \emph{acknowledgements/approach/using}; closed-class
English words). An LLM classifies each surviving candidate into a schema
entity type or rejects it. The corpus
floor is a corpus-presence gate---attestation means the term occurs in
the corpus, which is necessary but not sufficient for termhood (the
``be'' case of the name-quality section is exactly this gap); it
prevents hallucinated entries, not generic-word admissions.
\item \textbf{Per-type expansion ($\leq$60 per type).} For each entity type
the LLM proposes additional domain terms, given the type description and
the transplanted seeds as in-context anchors; the same corpus-attestation
gate applies. The bound keeps any single type from flooding.
\end{enumerate}

Generation parameters, for reproducibility: the classifier/expander is
an AWQ 4-bit build of the Qwen3.6-35B-A3B checkpoint (community build
\texttt{cyankiwi/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-AWQ-4bit}; we retain the deployment
string verbatim since the endpoint serves it under a historical alias),
served via vLLM behind a round-robin router, temperature 0.2,
$\leq$4{,}000 output tokens per call, three retries on empty or non-JSON
responses; the entire three-source run for both schemas is a single
idempotent invocation of the generator.

Quality gates beyond attestation: \emph{catch-all types}
(\texttt{term}, \texttt{term\_cn}, \texttt{model\_or\_code},
\texttt{concept}, \texttt{question}) accept no classified noise---they are
the natural sink of classification uncertainty and the main entry route for
taxonomy garbage; a boilerplate filter removes layout/section vocabulary;
cross-type alias conflicts resolve by a specificity ordering (the more
specific type wins); and the schema validator runs as a final structural
gate before any version bump. The same pass repaired 223 legacy cross-type
conflicts in existing schemas.

Output: \textbf{nuclear\_electronics v2.1 = 3{,}702 terms; physics v1.1 =
1{,}279 terms}. 104112E6 rebinds to nuclear\_electronics (its corpus is
nuclear-domain journals). Both versions are idempotent re-runnable---the
tool is now part of the schema lifecycle, not an incident artifact.

An honest note on the asymmetry (physics 1{,}279 versus nuclear 3{,}702):
the physics corpus is smaller and its seed transplant thinner; v1.1 is a
floor, not a ceiling, and the runbook treats small-physics libraries as
follow-up work (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\section{Rebuild at scale}
\label{sec:rebuild}

Repairing live knowledge bases requires tearing down the collapsed entity
layer without stopping ingestion or retrieval. The rebuild driver executes
per knowledge base: delete graph-store vertices with edges; delete the four
relational tables (mentions, links, relations, graph-projection queue rows)
in bounded chunks; delete the search-side entity projection by query; then
re-enqueue all documents through the four stages---\textbf{766{,}501 enqueue events}
across the two affected libraries. The count is events, not distinct
doc-stages (74{,}545 documents $\times$ 4 stages $=$ 298{,}180): the
surplus is the repair window's re-enqueue waves---the silent-empty
links reset (75{,}831), the post-fix relations/graph zero-effect resets
(${\approx}$149k), the watchdog-deletion graph recovery
(${\approx}$122k), the small-library treatment (5{,}772), and
re-dispatch of partially processed waves during the gated window; the
job table holds one row per doc-stage with an attempts counter, so
every wave re-uses rows. The rebuild ran against the production
fleet (five multi-worker enrich instances, one links-dedicated consumer per
KB) while normal retrieval served.

The rebuild doubled as the system's first full-corpus stress test of the
\emph{rich-lexicon} configuration; four secondary engineering incidents
surfaced and were fixed with measured effect (an unbounded projection join,
two link-insertion deadlock classes, and an $O(N^2)$ alias check the
richer lexicon itself amplified---fleet link throughput 8/min
$\to$ 1{,}100/min, a window-level aggregate). Their per-pathology
accounting is engineering, not evidence, and moves to
\ref{app:incidents}; one reading matters here: the collapsed
lexicon (575 de-facto terms) masked the $O(N^2)$ hot path because $N$
was tiny---correcting the lexicon changed the pipeline's computational
regime, so lexicon quality is load-bearing in the most literal sense.

The rebuild also supplied its own fifth pathology, and revisiting it
with the registry's own timestamps forced a correction to our incident
log that we report in full. The original record: a single-statement
link writer shipped with a parameter-order bug (the SQL's first
placeholder expected the knowledge-base id; the call bound a term form
there), jobs completed green for hours, and per-KB link counts read
zero growth until a row-count reconciliation caught it. What a minimal
reproduction on the production engine (MySQL 8.4, \texttt{STRICT\_TRANS\_TABLES},
faithful DDL) now establishes is sharper than ``rows were swallowed'':
under \texttt{INSERT IGNORE}, the NULL produced by
\texttt{UNHEX('alloy')} is \textbf{replaced by the implicit default
0x00$\times$16 and the row is inserted}---into a ghost bucket invisible
to every per-KB query and monitor (warnings 1411 and 1048 fire; nothing
fails)---and a later correct re-insert for the same (mention, entity)
pair is \textbf{silently skipped by the unique key}, so the orphan
actively poisons the repair unless deleted first. Production carries
zero 0x00 rows today, so the recovery path included that cleanup. A
second correction concerns duration: the operations log recorded
``seven hours of zero growth'', but the surviving rows' own
\texttt{created\_at} histogram (Figure~\ref{fig:telemetry}) shows KB1
inserting at 1.0--2.3M rows/hour continuously through that nominal
window and KB2's entire link layer landing in a single hour---the
zero-growth reading cannot be reconciled with the timestamps and most
plausibly reflects a monitoring view scoped to the buggy writer's
partition rather than the whole table. We keep both the original log
entry and its correction in the record: incident narratives written
from job dashboards are themselves unverified claims; row-level
forensics is the only authoritative account.

Rebuild final state. All four stages drained across both knowledge bases:
mentions, links, relations, graph projection. The repaired entity layer,
measured: \textbf{KB1 1{,}871 entities carrying 25{,}097{,}704 links (from
575 / ${\sim}$19.2M collapsed), 44{,}043 relations with 1.56M evidence rows
(from effectively zero); KB2 2{,}730 / 5{,}009{,}755 / 12{,}774.}
Spot-checked canonical entities (germanium detector, hpge detector,
neutron, preamplifier, scintillation detector) all project correctly into
the graph store. Retrieval on KB1 answers in 0.6--1.3\,s warm with all
channels active.

cardinality-family metric: its numerator scales with documents, it is
readable by domain experts, and the coverage column exposes the
collapse's most user-visible symptom---half the library had
\emph{no} entity annotations at all. The concentration drop
0.419$\to$0.345 is honest about its own limits: the pre-repair figure
is a mid-stream snapshot (4.81M links; the terminal state was 19.2M on
the same entities, which can only push concentration toward the
type-word head), and part of the drop comes from the entity population
growing---the name audit below remains the sharper discriminator.
Absolute link counts \emph{rise} after repair in both libraries (KB1
${\sim}$19.2M$\to$25.1M; KB2 2.23M$\to$5.0M, same-snapshot backup on
both sides): the repaired lexicons carry thousands of real terms that
match more often. Count was never the quality signal---distribution
(concentration) and names (top-entity audit) are.

\textbf{Name quality, honestly.} The top-linked entities after repair are
\texttt{be} (2.26M links), \texttt{alpha}, \texttt{gamma}, \texttt{beta},
\texttt{method}---generic words and short ambiguous symbols still dominate
the link head. The corpus-attestation gate can only guarantee that a term
\emph{occurs}; it cannot distinguish a high-frequency common word from a
domain term. The \texttt{be} case is instructive because it is not even a
ranking problem but a normalization one: the element symbol \emph{Be} and
the English auxiliary verb \emph{be} are distinct in the source text, but
K1's scan normalizes case (ASCII terms match case-insensitively with word
boundaries), so every auxiliary ``be'' in 7.2M chunks links to the
beryllium entity. A case-sensitive allowlist for short chemical symbols
plus an English stop-word exclusion at admission would remove the bulk of
this mass; \texttt{alpha}/\texttt{gamma}/\texttt{beta} are harder---genuine
nuclear-spectrum symbols that also serve as generic Greek-letter
variables---and need context-type constraints, not stop-word lists.
Genuine terminology (hpge detector, neutron, preamplifier, \ldots) is
present and correctly linked and projected---but the repair relocated the
lexicon-quality problem from ``no terminology at all'' to ``terminology plus
a generic tail.'' We consider this the honest headline of the incident:
repairing a collapsed lexicon is one battle; lexicon hygiene is a permanent
operational discipline (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}'s monitors exist
precisely for this).

\textbf{Retrieval trajectory, with a channel-level attribution bound.} On
the scoped evaluation slices (same runner, same corpus scope) the system
moved from the collapsed-era run (2026-09-24) to the rebuilt state
(2026-10-09): associative queries cov@10 0.533$\to$\textbf{0.599} (+6.6pt,
n=48), single-intent queries 0.603$\to$\textbf{0.675} (+7.3pt, n=39) with
MRR $-$2.0/+5.0pt respectively. The two-week window also carried
retrieval-configuration evolution (document-fusion re-ranking, bilingual
alias expansion, kNN search-depth tuning), so the trajectory measures
system evolution \emph{including} the lexicon repair. To bound the
attribution we ran a single-variable channel ablation under the identical
post-rebuild configuration (in-process planner, same corpus scope, same
questions): a \emph{no-entity} arm with every entity-dependent channel
disabled (co-occurrence, graph, entity-vector, PPR) against the full
configuration. Result: \textbf{@10 any-hit and document coverage are
unchanged on both families} (nea 0.599/0.938, ner 0.670/0.923); the
only movement anywhere is ner MRR $+$0.0006 (CI [0, 0.0018], ns) and
nea cov@20 $-$1.0pt, 95\% CI [$-$3.1, 0.0]---while p50 latency falls
from 1.46\,s to 0.52\,s. The ablation bounds these channels' marginal
contribution in this configuration, not the semantic assets' value at
large. The
co-occurrence channel fired on only 6/48 association queries (its adaptive
gate suppresses it when base retrieval already serves co-mention
documents) and changed no outcome.

\textbf{Channel-liveness check (the ablation's alternative explanation,
closed).} A null @10 result admits a defensible alternative
reading---that the entity channels are not wired, or that their gates
never open---so we ran a forced-trigger check on the 99-question
natural-phrasing set (template words banned), the distribution the
co-occurrence gate was designed for: there the channel fired on
\textbf{54/99 queries} (versus 6/48 on the scoped template set), killing
it changed the top-10 on 26 queries, and the paired gold-hit comparison
reads \textbf{20 losses / 0 wins / 34 ties} for the off-arm---removing
the channel never helps and hurts on a fifth of the set. The channel is
alive, the gate opens exactly where designed, and the scoped zero-effect
is the gate closing because base retrieval already serves those
co-mention documents---not a dead wire.

\textbf{Run-to-run noise floor.} Two same-configuration scoped runs
hours apart (the refresh run and the ablation baseline) differ on
exactly one of 39 single-intent queries (cov@10 0.675 vs 0.670)---a
measured 0.5pt noise floor from nondeterministic channel timing that
bounds every within-window comparison in this paper.

\textbf{Alias-expansion ablation.} The one entity-adjacent mechanism
that plausibly carried the trajectory---bilingual alias expansion
feeding BM25---was ablated on/off under the final configuration:
association cov@10 $+$1.5pt (paired bootstrap CI [$-$0.6, $+$3.9], ns) and single-intent
$+$0.1pt, at a p50 latency cost of 1.6$\to$3.5\,s. On the benchmark
sets the retrieval-window trajectory is therefore not attributable to
the lexicon through this mechanism either; where alias expansion pays
is query distributions rich in dictionary surfaces (the
natural-phrasing set), which the benchmarks under-represent.

The attribution this forces is precise and we state it against interest:
the +6.6/+7.3pt trajectory is \textbf{not} carried by entity channels
casting fusion votes. The entity layer's retrieval-facing value on these
benchmark families runs through the lexical channel's bilingual alias
expansion (entity names as BM25 terms---a mechanism that needs a real
terminology to expand, and which the collapsed lexicon could not feed) and
through structural surfaces (reranker context, knowledge-chain evidence
for generation) documented in the companion system
manuscript~\cite{companion}; at the fusion
layer itself, entity channels are currently a latency cost with zero
measured recall benefit on these sets. The lexicon repair was necessary
for every entity-consuming surface; the scoped trajectory number alone
cannot be credited to it.

\textbf{Link precision audit (LLM-assisted).} Distributional monitors say
nothing about whether individual links are \emph{correct}. We audited a
seeded, reproducible uniform sample of 100 links from the rebuilt KB1
registry (\texttt{ORDER BY RAND(20261009)}): for each link the mention
span was programmatically verified against the chunk text (span integrity
100/100---offsets are exact), and an LLM judge (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B,
temperature 0) received the $\pm$250-character context with the span
marked and answered a strict two-part rubric: (i)~does the span denote the
same concept as the entity's canonical name (different sense, different
concept, or non-term mention $\Rightarrow$ invalid; debatable $\Rightarrow$
invalid + borderline); (ii)~is the canonical name a domain-specific
technical concept or a generic academic word. Results: \textbf{overall
precision 0.85} (Wilson 95\% CI [0.77, 0.91]); the domain stratum is
\textbf{25/25 $=$ 1.00} (CI [0.87, 1.00]) and the generic stratum
60/75 $=$ \textbf{0.80} (CI [0.70, 0.88]). Every adjudication, span, and
reason string is released. The reading is the name-quality headline
quantified: in this 25-link domain subsample no erroneous link was
observed, and the residual error mass sits almost entirely in the generic
tail---but ``no errors observed at $n{=}25$'' is not ``error-free'': the
larger stratified audit's domain stratum reads 142/149, not 149/149. The audit is LLM-assisted rather than
human-adjudicated---a limitation we keep visible
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations})---but its stratum split is exactly what
the monitor design predicts, and the programmatic span check removes the
cheapest class of audit noise.

\textbf{Cross-model check (the judge-independence concern, addressed).}
The audit judge and the lexicon classifier are the same model family---a
self-grading risk we take seriously. We re-adjudicated the identical 100
links with a second judge from a different model, checkpoint, and provider
(Qwen3.8-Flash via an external gateway; temperature 0, identical
rubric). Both judges are Qwen-series---vendor-level correlation is not
excluded, and we label this a cross-model check, not an independent
family confirmation.
The second judge failed to emit parseable verdicts on 45/100---a
flash-tier instruction-following failure we report as-is, not hide.
The agreement figure therefore applies only to the 55-link parseable
subset and cannot be read as confirmation of the full 100; on that
subset, verdict agreement with the primary judge is
\textbf{53/55 $=$ 96.4\%} (50 both-valid, 3 both-invalid, 2
discordant---both discordances on the borderline generic word
``term''), and the domain stratum agrees \textbf{14/14}. Where an
independent model opined, it confirmed the primary adjudication; but
vendor-level correlation is \emph{not} confined to the unparseable
subset---the 55 parsed links, including the domain stratum (n=25
primary, 14 cross-parsed), carry the same family risk. A
human-adjudicated stratified sample remains the decisive control and is
listed as such in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\textbf{Stratified 300-link audit (third review round).} A uniform
sample reads the head; the failure modes live in the tail. We
re-audited with a stratified design: entities drawn uniformly within
four strata (top-10 by link frequency; medium, ranks 11--100; tail,
ranks 101+; short symbols, canonical names of $\leq$4 letters), then one
link uniformly per entity---seeded, replayable, 50/100/100/50 links,
same rubric and judge. Span integrity 300/300. The judged-valid rate
for this sample is \textbf{0.837} (Wilson 95\% CI [0.791, 0.874];
entity-cluster bootstrap over the 187 clusters, 10{,}000 resamples:
[0.782, 0.887]);
top-10
\textbf{0.920} [0.812, 0.968]; medium \textbf{0.850} [0.767, 0.907];
tail \textbf{0.910} [0.838, 0.952]; short-symbol \textbf{0.580}
[0.442, 0.706]; across the specificity axis, domain 142/149 $=$ 0.953
and generic 109/151 $=$ 0.722. The error mass is localized, not
diffuse: the stratified design confirms the uniform reading at the head
and pins the failure precisely on the short-ambiguous-symbol
class---the more informative finding of the audit, and the motivation
for the governance pricing below (not yet a demonstration of its
post-treatment validity). Two scope notes: the 50/100/100/50 quotas
target risk rather than production link frequencies, so the 0.837
figure is a stratum-mixed rate, not an unweighted production-wide link
precision; and the 300 links come from 187 distinct entities (the head
stratum's 50 links from just six; the short and head strata share
``term''), so the intervals treat links, not concepts, as exchangeable.

\textbf{The lexicon variable, isolated.} The repair bundled lexicon,
teardown, and fleet changes; this experiment isolates the lexicon
alone. Design: a semantic replica of the K1 scan-and-link semantics
(CJK substring match; ASCII word-boundary $+$ optional plural,
case-folded) and the K2 fold (normalized form $+$ type), with
\textbf{only the dictionary swapped}: v2.0 as committed at library
creation (182 scan terms), v2.5 (mid-evolution, 2{,}932), v2.1 (the
repair, 3{,}733 scan terms; three v2.1 counts appear in this paper
and refer to different objects: 3{,}702 canonical schema terms (the
committed lexicon), 3{,}733 scan terms after alias dedup (the replica
frame), and 3{,}824 entries in the production composite dictionary
(schema lexicon $\cup$ operational seed overlay)), plus two v2.1
governance variants (case-sensitive
matching for short ASCII symbols; an English stop-word block at
admission). Corpus frame: \textbf{ten seeded, uniformly
random resamples of 2{,}000 chunks each} from the library's 5.83M
visible chunks---independent draws from a \emph{single} corpus, i.e.\ a
sampling-stability check within one library; the sampling unit is
the chunk, and in this corpus chunks map to documents almost
one-to-one (sample 0: 2{,}000 chunks $\to$ 1{,}965 distinct
documents, 1.02 chunks/doc), so ``matched documents'' below means
matched chunks and no document is sampled twice
evidence---cross-sample disjoint, deduplicated on redraw. The replica
copies the scanner's matching and folding rules from the same source
reading (CJK substring; ASCII word-boundary with optional plural,
case-folded; fold key = normalized form $+$ type); it is not
instrumented against the live service path, so absolute levels may
drift---the experiment's force is the within-frame arm contrast. A
frozen-sample parity check bounds that drift: on 200 frozen KB1 chunks
scanned with the \emph{live} production dictionary (3{,}824 entries),
every one of the 913 production mention spans is reproduced by the
replica (span-level \emph{superset}, not output equivalence: the
per-term replica additionally reports 263 overlapping or nested
occurrences that the production merged longest-first alternation
suppresses by design, and a normalized-form bookkeeping rule differs
for aliases that themselves end in ``s''). Boundary, case, plural, and
CJK semantics are identical; the replica's link counts are an
inclusive upper bound within that frame. Because nesting prevalence
can differ per lexicon, we re-ran all cross-domain arms under
production-style overlap suppression (longest-match, non-overlapping):
links fall 10--40\% per arm but \emph{every ordering conclusion is
unchanged} (schema CS $C_{10}$ 0.859 with identical entities; the
size-matched arm $C_{10}$ 0.323 vs.\ three-source 0.366; frequency
still floods at 23+ concepts/doc). An
earlier single run had sampled an ordered prefix---a bias we replace
rather than defend; all metrics below are deterministic, reported as
mean$\pm$sd across the ten resamples. Concepts-per-document is averaged
over \emph{matched} documents only (those with $\geq$1 scan hit under
the arm's dictionary: mean 977 matched documents per sample under v2.0,
1{,}579 under v2.1), so it is not an all-sampled-document mean, and the
two arms' denominators are different sets.

Three findings. \textbf{(1)~The collapse signature is reproduced by the
lexicon alone}: 109$\pm$5 entities at concentration 0.513$\pm$0.016 and
1.85$\pm$0.05 concepts per \emph{matched} document from the v2.0 dictionary---same
code, same corpus, no other variable. \textbf{(2)~The repair effect is
isolated}: v2.0$\to$v2.1 multiplies link-bearing entities $\times$6.3 and
concepts per matched document $\times$2.1 (the denominator grows too:
mean link-bearing documents per 2{,}000-chunk sample rise
977$\to$1{,}579), while concentration decreases 0.513$\to$0.300 and
generic-head mass falls 0.146$\to$0.058; the production collapsed
concentration ($C_{10}$=0.419 at the 10-06 backup) sits between the
v2.0 (0.513) and v2.5 (0.322) counterfactuals,
consistent with the operational dictionary having evolved
mid-enrichment after the bulk of extraction had run.
\textbf{(3)~The ``be'' class is treatable at known cost}: case-sensitive
short-symbol matching removes the single largest entity and takes
concentration to 0.265$\pm$0.012, at the price of a wider case-fold
recall loss (links $-$12.8\%); a stop-word block is
gentler ($-$8.1\% links, $C_{10}$ 0.252$\pm$0.011). Judged
precision is near-tautological for dictionary self-matches (the entity
name \emph{is} the surface form), so the structural metrics lead and
precision is a bounded secondary---the same caveat the link audit
carries. These deltas measure matching \emph{output}, not true
recall: the removed mass mixes errors with correct terms, and the
0.580 pre-intervention judged-valid rate on the short-ambiguous-symbol
class motivates the treatment---the removed-vs-retained verification
below prices exactly this cut.

\emph{Budget sweep, closing the size confound.} A natural
concern is whether the isolated comparison (182-term collapsed
vs.\ 3{,}733-term repair dictionary) confounds size with selection. We re-ran the frozen
KB1 frame at fixed budgets $K \in \{200, 500, 1{,}000, 2{,}000,
3{,}733\}$ with three same-$K$ sources: the v2.1 pipeline in
construction order (prefix), frequency-top-$K$, and C-value-top-$K$
(candidates ranked on sample-0 text; frame overlap disclosed).
Coverage under the dual-annotated reference grows monotonically with
v2.1's own budget (R 0.004$\to$0.043 from $K$=200 to 3{,}733) while
concentration falls 0.73$\to$0.30 --- the repair's value accrues with
budget but its structure at every budget is distinct: frequency-top
recruits 4--5$\times$ the entities (2{,}036 at $K$=2{,}000) for
nominally higher R that rides on mass its own blind validity
disqualifies (0.45 at the equal-size control), and C-value-top
saturates at 554 entities with R stuck at 0.012 --- statistical
salience without attestation stalls on this corpus. Re-running the
whole sweep with the two baselines re-typed by the unified classifier
leaves R identical at every budget (0.001/0.016/0.037/0.073/0.098;
entity counts within 1.5\%)---the budget-sweep conclusions are
typing-invariant. Size explains the
counts; it does not explain the validity or the distribution.

\textbf{Verification: semantics, governance, monitors.}
Three follow-ups on the same frozen samples
close the gaps declared above.
\emph{(i)~Blind term-mention judging.} The replica folds matched
surfaces onto themselves, so the production audit's
does-the-span-denote-the-canonical question is tautological here; we
ask instead whether the matched span is, in context, a genuine
terminological mention of the term (ordinary-language use of the same
letters fails). Entity-uniform, 150 links per arm, judge Qwen3.6-35B at
temperature 0 (same family as the lexicon classifier---disclosed, and
the same exposure the cross-model check bounds). Result: \textbf{old
and new dictionaries are locally indistinguishable}---0.940 [0.890,
0.968] of v2.0's matched spans are genuine term mentions vs.\ 0.927
[0.873, 0.959] for v2.1 (domain share among valid: 89/141 vs 89/139).
The collapse is therefore not per-link falsehood: category-word links
are individually ``valid'' mentions of generic concepts. What fails is
the vocabulary and its mass---109 entities at $C_{10}$=0.513 vs 692 at
0.300---and a per-link precision audit, however large, would not have
caught the incident. \emph{(ii)~Equal-size frequency control.} A
3{,}733-term dictionary drawn purely by corpus frequency (same
$_{\!}$filters, no seed transplant, no LLM classification, no
expansion; types randomly assigned from v2.1's inventory) reads
\textbf{0.450 [0.356, 0.548]} on the same frozen text, and only 15 of
its 45 valid links name domain concepts (v2.1: 89 of 139): the
dictionary-size confound is answered directly---size buys nothing,
selection carries the validity. Its structural profile is itself
diagnostic: 1.70M links on the same 20k chunks ($17\times$ v2.1's),
with a head of markup fragments and function words (\texttt{td},
\texttt{of}, \texttt{sup}, \texttt{mathrm})---the frequency pipeline
without the classification gate does not merely underperform, it
re-collapses the link layer through a different door. \emph{(iii)~Governance verified.}
Short-symbol hits that case-sensitivity removes (case-mismatched
surfaces of $\leq$3-letter ASCII terms) are \textbf{0.033 [0.009,
0.114]} valid; exact-case retained ones are 0.700 [0.575, 0.801]: the
$-$12.8\% link cut removes almost purely error mass, and the retained
class still carries 30\%---the treatment is demonstrated post-hoc, not
merely motivated. \emph{(iv)~Graded-degradation monitor validation.}
Two axes on the ten frozen samples, no gold labels: a
\emph{replacement} axis mimicking the incident (drop $m$\% of v2.1,
keep all category words) and a \emph{shrinkage} axis (random deletion).
The $C_{10}$ band fires at $\geq$75\% replacement (0.371) and at 100\%
reproduces the collapse arm bitwise (0.513/109 entities---the replica's
cross-script determinism check); count monitors fire earlier under
shrinkage (links $-$50\% at 50\% deletion) where $C_{10}$ stays flat
to 50\%; and moderate replacement (10--50\%) moves $C_{10}$ by less
than one sd while counts drop up to 41\%---a blind zone for both single-monitor
families. Conclusion unchanged from the design argument, now with
evidence: gold-free monitors deploy as a set, and the name audit
remains the sharpest early discriminator of the vocabulary failure that
per-link and count signals both miss.

\textbf{Short-symbol ambiguity: four linking policies.} On 150
short-symbol mentions sampled from the frozen replay chunks (60 on the
\texttt{be} class; each mention judged once, blind, by judge A and
the cross-family judge, 12 disagreements), four policies on the same
dictionary: linking everything (current behavior) reads precision
\textbf{0.24}; exact-case gating (the deployed treatment) 0.725 at
true-symbol recall 0.806, rejecting 73\% (rejection precision 0.936);
adding context evidence (long-term neighbors in a $\pm$150-character
window) recalls slightly more (0.833) at lower precision (0.652);
context plus reverse-NIL trades recall (0.583) for precision (0.724).
On the \texttt{be} class specifically, \emph{zero} of 60 sampled
mentions are genuine element references---the exact-case gate removes
pure error there. Context gating and case gating exchange precision
for recall rather than dominating each other; the recoverable mass
lives in the non-\texttt{be} short symbols, where identity (not
term-ness) is the open problem.

\textbf{Why 692 entities here vs 1{,}871 in production?} The isolated
frame counts only entities induced by scanning 2{,}000 chunks (0.034\%
of the library) under replica folding: rare terms need corpus mass to
be attested, and the production layer additionally carries entities
from the full corpus and historical merging. The causal claim rests on
within-frame comparisons, never on this cross-frame count.
Methodological caveats: semantic-replica scanner, 2{,}000-chunk samples
(ten resamples bound the variance but not the coverage of a 5.83M-chunk
library), and committed-snapshot arms rather than independently
regenerated component ablations (the provenance decomposition in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} approximates that axis lexically).

\textbf{Provenance decomposition (component contribution, approximated
lexically).} The v2.1 file carries no per-term source tags, so we
decompose its isolated link mass by which earlier committed lexicon
first contained each matched term: v2.0-inherited terms contribute
22.0\% of links (70 entities), mid-evolution terms 16.0\% (232), and
terms first introduced by the three-source repair \textbf{61.9\% of
links (384 of 686 entities)}---the repair's additions carry
three-fifths of the rebuilt link mass (shares stable against the
retired ordered-prefix frame, which read 21.4/15.3/63.2). A true component ablation (independently
regenerating single-source dictionaries) remains future work; this
set-algebra attribution is its lexical lower bound. A scope note on
dictionary size: the arms vary the dictionary \emph{as a whole} (v2.0
and v2.1 differ by ${\sim}$20$\times$ in scan terms), so the contrast
identifies the dictionary change, not its decomposition into term
count, term-selection quality, and per-source method; the equal-size
frequency control and the blind old/new matched-context judging in the
verification below supply both controls on the same frozen samples
(the public-domain cached-component ablation of
Section~\ref{sec:xdomain} supplies the single-source analogue;
independently regenerating single-source dictionaries for the
\emph{production} library remains future work).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Observability for lexicon quality.} Four cheap monitors would have
caught both incidents at first symptom, in the spirit of production

\item[(a)] \emph{Top-10 link concentration (primary).}
The fraction of all entity links held by the ten most-linked entities---a
concentration diagnostic invariant to uniform rescaling of link counts.
That specific invariance is what raw cardinality lacks; it is not a
license to read the value as a corpus-size- or discipline-independent
quality scale (topic concentration moves it too). Measured across the thirteen libraries of
our deployment, the metric spans 0.16--0.77 among libraries with
$\geq$100 entities (a five-entity library reads 1.000---the
$\min(10,N)/N$ floor bound, not degradation; $N\leq$10 forces
$C_{10}{=}1$, and the metric also responds to topic concentration as
well as to degradation): the healthy and repaired
large libraries run at 0.16--0.19 (rebuilt physics KB2 0.162, hydrogeology
control 0.184); the rebuilt nuclear library sits at 0.345 with its generic
tail; and the two small treated libraries \textbf{currently} read 0.398
(1{,}324 docs, 629 entities) and 0.768 (119 docs, 153
entities)---values we report as they are, not smoothed: the smallest
library also illustrates the metric's floor effect (with O(10$^2$)
entities, ten of them can hold most links under an ordinary Zipfian
distribution alone), so the band should be read jointly with library
size. The collapsed state itself is now measured, not gestured at:
recovering the pre-incident cold database backup (2026-10-06, two days
before discovery, with the collapsed enrichment still writing---4.8M
links on KB1 at that moment versus 19.2M at discovery) gives
\textbf{0.419} for collapsed KB1 (top-10 entities: \emph{time, model,
method, energy, temperature, field, term, tau, phase, material}---431k
links on ``time'' alone) and \textbf{0.290} for collapsed KB2. The
repair moves KB1 0.419$\to$0.345 and KB2 0.290$\to$0.162---direction
correct, and the residual gap is the generic tail. Note also that
collapsed KB2's 0.290 sits \emph{below} the 0.35 investigation band: a
concentration threshold alone does not catch every collapse---KB2's is
exposed by the name-audit and coverage legs, which is why the monitors
deploy as a set. One honesty note:
the metric partly improves simply because the entity population grows
(503$\to$1{,}871 link-bearing entities); the name audit (c) remains the
sharper discriminator of the collapse itself. We propose a band alarm
($>$0.35 investigation, $>$0.5 escalation) with an explicit caveat:
these are \emph{empirical, post-hoc} candidate thresholds read on this
deployment's thirteen libraries, not on an external
population---triage triggers for human inspection, not classifiers.
Two detection targets must be kept distinct. \emph{Collapse
detection} asks whether the entity layer has degenerated;
\emph{lexicon-quality warning} asks whether the link distribution is
contaminated with generic vocabulary. For collapse detection, a
healthy library whose terminology is genuinely concentrated is a
$C_{10}$ false positive; for a lexicon-quality warning the same
trigger is correct when the tail is in fact generic (the small-library
case below). The composite rule's zero-false-alarm claim below is
scoped to collapse detection on the graded-degradation archive.
Their known blind spots, stated as such (per target). \emph{False
positives} (collapse target): healthy libraries whose terminology is
genuinely concentrated (the two small treated libraries at
0.398/0.768---real terms, small corpora; the public-domain CS
three-source arm at 0.404, Section~\ref{sec:xdomain}). \emph{False
negatives}: replacement-type
degradation below ${\sim}$75\% substitution, where $C_{10}$ moves less
than one sd (offline graded degradation on the frozen resamples,
Section~\ref{sec:after}; the experiment has no time axis, so we make no
detection-latency claim), and collapses whose head happens to be
diversified (the collapsed physics backup reads 0.290, below the band
---count monitors and the name audit carry those cases). Moderate
replacement is a blind zone for both signal families---monitors deploy
as a set;
libraries below a few hundred entities warrant size-stratified bands.
A leave-one-library-out check on the 14 rows (thresholds re-frozen at
the q90/q97 of the remaining healthy libraries each fold) yields 12
correct quiets, the same known false-positive class (the genuinely
concentrated small library), and the same known false-negative class
(the collapsed backup reads below the LOO band---count monitors and
the name audit carry it): the bands are self-consistent across
libraries but remain deployment-derived; unseen-deployment validation
stays future work.

\emph{Composite rule, evaluated.} Because single-leg thresholds
misfire in both directions, the deployable unit is a four-leg
disjunction (fire on any leg): concentration ($C_{10}{>}0.35$, small
libraries exempt), link-count drop ($>$50\% vs.\ healthy baseline),
generic-head share ($>$0.10), and entity-cardinality drop ($>$50\%).
On the frozen graded-degradation archive this composite fires on
every should-alarm state (collapse, ${\ge}$75\% replacement,
${\ge}$50\% shrinkage) with \emph{zero} misses and zero false alarms
on the healthy baseline and mild degradations (collapse target; the
naturally concentrated small libraries are exempt from this leg, where
the same reading remains informative as a lexicon-quality warning). We report this as
\emph{in-sample diagnostic verification}, not external validation:
thresholds, degradation classes, and pass criteria all derive from the
same production libraries and frozen samples, so the result shows the
rule reproduces the intended behavior, not that it generalizes to
unseen knowledge bases; a frozen-threshold test on unseen libraries
remains open. The 10--50\%
replacement band remains undetected by every leg (at 50\% the
generic-head leg sits at its threshold, 0.096 vs.\ 0.10) --- the
honest structural floor of aggregate-statistics monitoring, and the
reason the name-audit leg exists.
\emph{Cold start.} The two count legs (link-count and
entity-cardinality drops) compare against a healthy history baseline
that a newly created library does not have; until a first full
enrichment pass completes and its post-pass snapshot seeds the
baseline, those legs are inert by construction, and protection rests
on the two history-free legs---top-link concentration and the
top-entity name audit---which are exactly the legs that exposed this
incident.

hand-evolved control library and is offered as illustration, not a

None of the four requires ground truth. The small-library treatment
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}) is an illustrative within-deployment
case for the monitor stack---the candidate bands were read \emph{after}
observing these thirteen libraries, so this is not out-of-sample
validation: after the same repair playbook, entity counts contracted
(870$\to$629, 209$\to$153---the cardinality signal normalizes) while the
concentration monitor still flags both libraries---one above the
investigation threshold (0.398 $>$ 0.35), one above the escalation
threshold (0.768 $>$ 0.5)---correctly \emph{as lexicon-quality
warnings}, since their tails remain
generic---demonstrating that
the two monitors separate ``cardinality repaired'' from ``distribution
still skewed'', which cardinality alone cannot.

\textbf{What the repair restores, and what it does not.} The evidence
supports two claims of different strength. At the \emph{entity-layer
structural} level the repair succeeds on every deployed observable:
entity diversity (575$\to$1{,}871/2{,}726 canonical entities),
annotated-document coverage (48.5\%$\to$98.5\%), concentration back
inside the healthy band, and link precision confirmed by stratified
audit. At the \emph{terminology-coverage} level the repair is
deliberately partial: the from-scratch enumeration
(Section~\ref{sec:xdomain}) bounds coverage of human-recognized
terms at 16.9\% of core recurring constructions under the size cap,
and half of the enumerated terms are corpus-unseen coinages that no
bounded static lexicon can carry. Three-source construction is
therefore a high-validity, bounded-lexicon-budget \emph{repair} for
the entity layer---not a high-recall general-purpose term-extraction
method; low-frequency and newly coined terminology is mention-layer
scope, where a bounded lexicon hands off to runtime extraction.

\textbf{LLM extraction versus dictionary scan.} The incident is not an
argument for LLM-everywhere. It is an argument for honoring the input
contract of cheap deterministic components: if extraction is a scan, the
dictionary is the model---and it deserves the same evaluation discipline
(held-out attestation, distributional checks) as a trained model.
Batch-generating that dictionary with an LLM and shipping it on structural
validation alone is the actual failure mode.

\textbf{Schema tooling implications.} The batch schema generator produced
category taxonomy where a terminology was needed; the repair gave the schema
a real lexicon (3{,}702 terms) and folded lexicon generation into the schema
lifecycle as an idempotent tool. What remains open---and what the rebuilt
top-entity distribution makes plain---is that \emph{admission} criteria
(corpus attestation) are necessary but not sufficient: a lexicon also needs
\emph{ranking} criteria that demote high-frequency generic words and short
ambiguous symbols (IDF-style corpus contrast, bilingual alias consistency,
length/type priors). The observation that the collapsed state and the
repaired state differ by a factor of 3.25 in entity cardinality---while both
pass structural validation---is the strongest argument we have for treating
lexicon quality as first-class schema infrastructure with its own acceptance
gates, not as a one-time repair.

\section{Does It Generalize? Three Public Domains}
\label{sec:xdomain}

The incident corpus is nuclear-science literature; a natural objection is
that the failure and the repair are local accidents. We therefore replay
the lexicon-only design on three public domains---arXiv cs.CL (10{,}000
abstracts), PubMed biomedicine (9{,}988), and arXiv cond-mat.mtrl-sci
(10{,}000)---with seeded splits per domain: 3{,}000 abstracts for lexicon
construction only, 100 frozen test abstracts for silver annotation, and
the remainder replayed as ten disjoint ${\sim}$690-document samples for
structural metrics (mean across samples; sd archived). \emph{Mass}
throughout denotes two reported quantities---lexicon size ($|$L$|$) and
per-document annotation volume (concepts/doc)---plus matched-document
coverage where stated; we report all three per arm and predefine no
scalar utility over them, so no ``best'' is asserted. Five arms share
one scanner; only the lexicon varies: \emph{batch schema} (the
production-failure form: each domain's \emph{own} current repository
schema lexicon, i.e.\ the batch tool's committed output for that
domain---category-shaped alias sets, in CS largely Chinese or
uppercase-acronym forms), \emph{frequency} top-$K$ and \emph{C-value}
top-$K$ (each size-matched to ours), \emph{LLM direct} (per-batch
extraction without corpus validation), and the \emph{three-source}
recipe. One adaptation is disclosed: source~1 transplants seeds from a
healthy near-domain library, which does not exist for these public
domains---we substitute LLM knowledge seeds under the same
corpus-presence validation; sources 2--3 (corpus-candidate
classification, per-type expansion with presence validation) are
unchanged.

\textbf{Model-annotation protocol with human verification.} The
annotation layer is model-based; human input enters as verification
of a stratified sample (below), not as from-scratch labels.
Silver reference lists come from a strong annotator LLM (judge A,
Qwen3.6-35B) enumerating every domain term in each test abstract; arm
quality is then judged blind on stratified samples
(head/medium/tail/short-symbol entities) by judge A and a second
Qwen-series model (judge B), with disagreements and missing judgments
arbitrated by a third \emph{cross-family} judge (DeepSeek; 55--71 rows
per domain; 8--29\% of rows per cell). Two instrument limits are
disclosed rather than hidden.
First, enumeration is the weak link: on 30-document subsets
re-annotated by the cross-family model, term-list Jaccard with judge A
is 0.41 (CS), 0.55 (biomedicine), 0.52 (materials)---so silver P/R are
indicative levels, not ground truth, and we read contrasts between arms
(which share the instrument) rather than absolute magnitudes. Second,
judge B cannot enumerate at all (the gateway forces chain-of-thought
past read timeouts) and is used only for short blind judgments; and the
LLM-direct arm's silver-P is same-family inflated, since judge A's
family also built that arm.

\textbf{Instrument sensitivity, tested on the shared subset.} Because a
shared instrument does not guarantee arm-wise bias cancellation, we
recomputed all five arms' occurrence-level P/R on the exact
30-document A$\cap$C subset under \emph{both} references (matching
rule: lower-cased surface with naive plural stripping, applied
symmetrically). The recall ordering of the five arms is
\textbf{identical under both judges in all three domains}; the F1
ordering is identical in biomedicine and materials, while in CS only
the middle band permutes (frequency/C-value/three-source swap ranks
2--4; first and last unchanged). Three-source's precision
\emph{rises} under the cross-family reference in all domains (CS
0.218$\to$0.312, biomedicine 0.195$\to$0.287, materials
0.246$\to$0.260) while LLM-direct's falls (CS 0.653$\to$0.544),
consistent with the disclosed family bias and mildly favoring
three-source under the harder reference. Cross-domain conclusions
therefore rest on (i)~structural metrics (no instrument),
(ii)~recall ordering, and (iii)~arbitrated blind validity; mid-band F1
orderings and absolute P/R levels are instrument-conditional.
Judged-valid rates carry Wilson 95\% CIs---e.g.\ biomedicine
batch-schema 0.906 [0.810, 0.956] vs.\ three-source 0.850 [0.756,
0.912]: overlapping.
\emph{A dual-annotated reference, and what survives it.} To strengthen
the instrument we finally cross-annotated 200 complete units --- 80
frozen KB1 chunks plus 40 test documents per public domain --- with
two independent model annotators reading full text (not approving
candidates): span-level entries with termness, specificity, type, and
identity-ambiguity flags. P1 = deepseek-flash, P2 = deepseek-v4-pro
(same vendor, different models --- overlap disclosed; a gateway
thinking-mode model was attempted first and is unusable for
full-document annotation: forced deliberation exceeds any practical
token budget), disagreements arbitrated by the cross-family Qwen
judge. Agreement is materially stronger than single-model enumeration:
domain-set Jaccard \textbf{0.59--0.66} across the four scopes,
termness agreement on overlap \textbf{94.5--98.8\%} (vs.\ 0.41--0.55
for enumeration pairs). Under this reference: the batch-schema arms
recall 0--1\% (the collapse signature under the strongest instrument
yet); the production lexicons' first recall estimate on KB1 reads
v2.0 1.0\% $\to$ v2.1 4.3\% of adjudicated distinct concepts
(4.3$\times$; the reference is deliberately exhaustive, so absolute
levels are floors, not ceilings); the selection arms' recall deficit
replicates (learned/cover 0.7--3.2\%); and the frequency arm's
apparent recall rides on mass its own blind validity (0.29--0.40)
already disqualifies. \emph{Human verification}: one author
independently confirmed a seeded, stratified sample of 197 adjudicated
terms over 20 units (5 per scope; sheet, protocol, and per-row
verdicts in the artifact) --- confirm rate \textbf{0.934} [0.890,
0.961]; by scope 0.833 (KB1) / 0.967 / 0.925 / 0.950; two-model
consensus items verify at 0.949 vs.\ 0.853--0.960 for arbitrated
additions. This bounds the reference's precision from the model side;
it does not measure omissions (verification, not from-scratch
re-annotation) and is single-author --- an independent two-annotator
gold remains future work. A third gateway model (Qwen family, short-task capable) independently confirms the shared domain-term decisions at 0.75 (KB1) and 0.875/0.875/0.95 (CS/biomedicine/materials) on 40-item samples per scope. Entity-cluster bootstrap CIs for the three-source
validity rates (10{,}000 resamples) are [0.747, 0.921] (CS),
[0.772, 0.924] (biomedicine), [0.846, 0.974] (materials) --- wider
than link-level Wilson intervals, conclusions unchanged.

\emph{From-scratch enumeration (recall side)}: to bound the omission
side that verification cannot see, the same author enumerated domain
terms from zero on 30 additional held-out abstracts (10 per domain,
sampled from the frozen cross-domain test splits; no candidates were
shown), yielding 418 unique terms matched against the five arms by
exact surface after case folding (Table~\ref{tab:enum}). The
batch-schema arm covers \textbf{0/418} --- including 0 of the 83 terms
attested in more than ten construction-corpus documents: the collapse
signature replicates under the strongest instrument yet, now from the
recall side. Half of the enumerated terms (209/418) never occur in
the 3{,}000-document construction corpus --- paper-specific coinages
(new method, benchmark, and model names) that no bounded static
lexicon can carry and that belong to the mention layer; this bounds
what lexicon-layer recall can mean at all. Within the core stratum
the size-aligned arms cover 16.9--22.9\% (frequency 14/83, C-value
19/83, three-source 14/83) and the unvalidated LLM-direct arm 47.0\%
with a $4.6\times$ larger lexicon; per 1{,}000 lexicon entries the
recall-efficiency ordering is C-value 16.6 $>$ three-source 14.8 $>$
frequency 12.2 $>$ LLM-direct 10.5. The probe prices the validity
-first selection honestly: under the shared size cap, three-source
trades a modest recall deficit to C-value for its arbitrated blind
validity (0.838--0.912 vs.\ 0.27--0.40). The enumeration sheet
(git-ignored: public-corpus text), per-term document-frequency counts,
and the strata table ship with the artifact.

0.5\% (CS), 3.4\% (biomedicine), 7.0\% (materials)---reported per
domain because the head list is the incident corpus's category
vocabulary: its biomedicine/materials drivers (``disease'', ``drug'',
``phase'', ``cell'') are legitimate domain terms in those domains, so
the cross-domain residual is substantially list--domain mismatch
rather than pure genericity (the frequency arm reads 4.7\% in CS,
where the same words are generic). No single arm dominates all
reported metrics; we present three-source as the design point that
pairs size-matched validity with bounded annotation volume under this
protocol, not as a uniquely optimal balance.

\emph{Language confound, resolved by control.} Because the CS
batch-schema lexicon carries Chinese and uppercase-acronym aliases
against English abstracts, its failure could be read as language
mismatch rather than semantic shape. A language-aligned control
(English-normalized aliases only: CJK dropped, ASCII lower-cased, 80
terms) reads $C_{10}$ 0.859 with the same 18.4 entities and the same
5.7\% matched documents as the raw arm --- the Chinese aliases never
matched English text, and the English ones that do are category
words. Normalization does move one observable: raw 94.1 links fall to
65.9 ($-$30\%), because case-variant aliases (``Algorithm'' /
``algorithm'') compile to overlapping case-insensitive patterns that
each counted the same span; folding duplicates deflates top-head
counts unevenly, which is the whole of the $C_{10}$ 0.899$\to$0.859
shift. A translation control closes the remaining path: the schema's
non-ASCII aliases are exactly sixteen type-name translations (the
Chinese for ``algorithm'', ``data structure'', etc.), not domain-term
aliases; translating them to English (LLM) adds two net-new surfaces
that match the corpus zero times, and every metric is identical to the
deletion-based alignment. Deleting versus translating the Chinese
content is a distinction without a difference---there is no Chinese
terminology to lose. The CS collapse is semantic, not orthographic.


\textbf{What carries the recipe} (CS ablation, same frozen samples;
Table~\ref{tab:xdablate}). Removing corpus-candidate classification
collapses coverage (concepts/doc 3.60$\to$1.63): classification is the
coverage workhorse. Removing presence validation keeps 977 raw entries
for almost the same coverage (3.92 vs 3.60)---the gate removes 74\% of
raw LLM mass at ${\sim}$8\% coverage cost, which is precisely the trade
the incident demanded. Seed and expansion alone are thin (1.3--1.5
concepts/doc); unvalidated knowledge seeds carry 13$\times$ the entries
of validated ones (768 vs 58) for barely more matched entities (80.8
vs 50.9)---mass without coverage. The size-matched frequency control
floods (30.7 concepts/doc, 4.5\% generic head). The recipe's value is
in the assembly and the gate, not in any single source. One component is demoted on this evidence: \emph{expansion}.
Removing it costs nothing structurally (entities 211.7 vs.\ 202.1,
concepts/doc 3.73 vs.\ 3.60), and its standalone hits read 0.433
valid under double judging with only 0.50 judge agreement ($n$=60) ---
contested quality. We reframe expansion as an optional
diversity module, not a necessary source; the load-bearing pair is
corpus-attested classification plus the presence gate.

\subsection{Where Recall Is Lost, and What Selection Can Buy}
\label{sec:funnel}

The silver recalls above are occurrence-level against an enumeration
instrument; before optimizing selection we decompose where the loss
sits. For every silver occurrence we test four nested events:
expressible by \emph{some} candidate ($C$: a wide pool---all
construct n-grams at frequency $\geq$2 plus definition-evidenced
abbreviations plus all LLM candidate lists; 12{,}149/15{,}032/13{,}121
terms per domain), kept in the arm's dictionary ($K$), detected by the
actual scan at that occurrence ($D$), and linked under normalization
($L$). The funnel is unambiguous: $P(C)$ is 0.44/0.45/0.41
(CS/biomed/materials)---\emph{the candidate pool, not the ranking, is
the binding constraint}; three-source keeps $P(K|C)$=0.15/0.21/0.14;
scan and link are nearly lossless ($P(D|K\cdot C)$ 0.89--0.98,
$P(L)\approx 1$). The pool gaps are two-character element symbols
(excluded by the tokenizer's 3-character minimum), terms attested only
in test abstracts (construct-side presence cannot see them), and
neologisms; the selection gaps include, e.g., \emph{LLM} and
\emph{large language model} themselves in CS.

\textbf{Risk-constrained selection, tested and not adopted.} A
learned-rank/set-selection alternative at equal budget (linear
feature scorer on corpus contrast, C-value, phrase structure,
abbreviation evidence, and ambiguity flags, trained on construct-side
silver; set-selection over construct mention structure;
greedy-plus-swap within 1.5\% of a beam-search reference) raises
silver-P while recall falls outside CS, and arbitrated blind validity
collapses (learned-rank 0.29/0.46 in CS/biomedicine vs.\
0.838/0.850 for three-source)---the silver-P gain was an instrument
artifact, and construct-side learned selection without attestation
gates reproduces the invalid-mass regime the incident taught. The
full account, including the budget-sweep and concentration evidence,
is archived in Section~\ref{app:selection}; the headline: within an
attested pool and construct-side proxies, neither learned ranking nor
coverage-linked selection dominates the deployed pipeline, and
selection pressure alone manufactures concentration.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Three questions framed this study. \emph{When does a structurally valid
lexicon produce semantic collapse?} When its entries are category words:
the scan-and-link machinery executes faithfully, and a single-variable
dictionary swap reproduces the collapse signature---no pipeline bug
required. \emph{What does corpus-presence gating repair, and what does it
leave?} It restores terminology and coverage (annotated documents
${\sim}$51.5\%$\to$98.5\%; link-bearing entities $\times$6.3 in the
isolated frame) but admits a generic tail---presence is necessary, not
sufficient, and ranking criteria are the open problem. \emph{What can
gold-free diagnostics see?} Distribution and names---the collapse
signature---but not per-link truth: blind judging shows old-dictionary
links are locally as ``valid'' as new ones (0.94 vs 0.93), so the
collapse hides from per-link audits; the semantic gain runs through
vocabulary and mass (equal-size frequency control 0.45 vs 0.93), graded
degradation bounds when the alarms fire (band at $\geq$75\%
replacement; counts earlier under shrinkage), and the judged rates
themselves (0.84 overall, 0.58 short symbols, removed 0.03 vs retained
0.70) remain model-assisted readings with disclosed family exposure. The production
incident supplied the motivation and the validation environment; the
controlled lexicon swap, the stratified audit, and a three-domain
public-corpus replay carry the evidence. A fourth question closes the
loop: \emph{does it generalize?} On three public domains the
batch-schema form reproduces the collapse signature ($C_{10}$ up to
0.899 with recall ${\le}$0.009), frequency floods, and the three-source
recipe is the size-matched design point with the best arbitrated
validity at bounded annotation volume---a trade-off under the tested
metrics and AI instrument, not a claimed unique optimum; the failure mode and the
repair are properties of the construction method, not of one corpus.

\section{Limitations and honesty}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The generic tail survives the repair}: top-linked entities
remain short generic symbols; admission gates without ranking gates move
the problem, they do not remove it.
\item \textbf{Retrieval attribution is bounded, not eliminated}: the
channel ablation of Section~\ref{sec:after} shows the trajectory does not
run through entity-channel fusion votes, but the full decomposition
(lexicon old/new $\times$ retrieval configuration on/off) has not been
run---the graded-degradation verification leg (frozen
slices, Section~\ref{sec:after}) is a first step; the full
lexicon$\times$configuration grid remains unrun.
\item \textbf{Coverage}: two knowledge bases rebuilt (61{,}137 $+$
13{,}408 documents) and, after the two main libraries drained (2026-10-09), the two smaller
affected libraries (1{,}324 $+$ 119 documents) with the same playbook---where
entity cardinality \emph{contracted} (870$\to$629, 209$\to$153, with
916k/26k links and 4.6k new typed relations): the category-word aliases had
inflated the count, so cardinality is not the quality metric, name
distribution is. The bilingual alias layer showed isolated anomalies (an
entity whose mentions vanished between extraction generations) that we
document but do not fully explain.
\item \textbf{Incident-response cost}: six secondary incidents across the
repair window (one INSERT-IGNORE ghost-bucket mis-write whose logged duration was
corrected by row-level timestamps, three half-open-connection wedges, a watchdog that mistook completion for
failure and deleted the freshly rebuilt graph, one missing-index class)
each extended the repair; we report them as findings, not hidden costs.
\item \textbf{No human-adjudicated ground truth} for entity correctness:
the link-precision audit is LLM-assisted (with programmatic span
verification); the stratified 300-link sample replaces the uniform 100
but entity-uniform sampling intentionally over-weights the tail
relative to link frequency, and the cross-judge is same-vendor-family;
the blind term-mention judging (term-mention rubric), the equal-size
control verdict, and the removed-vs-retained governance verdicts
(0.033/0.700) inherit the same family exposure and a rubric not
directly comparable to the production link audit.
A human-adjudicated stratified sample is the
natural next evaluation step.
\item \textbf{The cross-domain study is model-annotated, with a
single-author human verification of a 197-term sample that bounds
precision, plus a single-author from-scratch enumeration of 30
held-out abstracts that references recall}
(Section~\ref{sec:xdomain}): silver enumeration agreement across
families is 0.41--0.55; an instrument-sensitivity rerun on the shared
30-document subset shows the arms' recall ordering stable under both
references (F1 mid-band orderings in CS are instrument-conditional);
one gateway judge is
unusable for enumeration, and the LLM-direct arm's silver-P is
same-family inflated; cross-domain conclusions therefore rest on
structural metrics, recall orderings, and arbitrated blind validity,
and the
source-substitution adaptation (LLM knowledge seeds in place of seed
transplant) is disclosed rather than hidden. The recall funnel and the
selection study (Section~\ref{sec:funnel}) inherit the silver
instrument for their loss attribution and training labels; the
selection arms' dev labels come from the cross-family judge, and the
negative result is stated under those proxies.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The selection study: tested and not adopted}
\label{app:selection}

Moved from the main text (third-review slimming); content unchanged.

\textbf{Risk-constrained selection, tested and not adopted.} The
natural next question is whether a learned or set-selection objective
beats per-term ranking at equal budget. We built the wide pool above,
a linear feature scorer (corpus contrast, C-value, phrase structure,
abbreviation evidence, short-symbol and generic-word ambiguity flags)
trained on \emph{construct-side} silver enumerated by the cross-family
judge (60 documents per domain; test documents never touch pool,
training, or tuning), and the set-selection objective over construct
mention structure with proxy link-correctness and specificity scores
(explicitly proxies: no calibration claims). Greedy-plus-swap local
search reaches within 1.5\% of a beam-search reference on the CS
top-300 slice. At the same $K$ as three-source the selection arms
fail, and the manner of failure is itself a finding. Silver-P
\emph{rises} (CS 0.274$\to$0.362, biomedicine 0.273$\to$0.352,
materials 0.223$\to$0.526) while recall falls outside CS
(biomedicine 0.060$\to$0.015, materials 0.046$\to$0.009; CS
flat-to-up 0.050$\to$0.053); doubling or halving the budget moves
recall by less than half a point while precision decays --- the pool
ceiling of the funnel again; and the selected lexicons
\emph{concentrate} (top-10 concentration 0.69--0.73 in CS vs.\
0.404 for three-source). The arbitrated blind judging then reverses
the apparent precision gain: validity collapses in CS and biomedicine
(learned-rank 0.29 [0.19, 0.41] and 0.46 [0.32, 0.55], vs.\
0.838/0.850 for three-source) and stays below three-source in
materials (0.86--0.89 vs.\ 0.912, at one-fifth the recall). The
silver-P gain was an instrument artifact --- enumeration
under-covers, which flatters arms whose extra mass is invalid --- and
construct-side learned selection without attestation gates reproduces
precisely the invalid-mass regime the incident taught. We therefore
keep the three-source pipeline as the repair baseline and report this
as a bounded negative result: within an attested pool and
construct-side proxies, neither learned ranking nor coverage-linked
selection dominates it, and selection pressure alone manufactures
concentration.

\section{Secondary engineering incidents during the rebuild}
\label{app:incidents}

Reported as findings, not evidence for the paper's claims; each is
fixed and committed.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{B90 --- unbounded projection join.} Graph projection
originally joined each document against \emph{whole-corpus} entities and the
\emph{whole-corpus} link table; at 80k documents and millions of links a
single projection took 25+ minutes, and 80 concurrent consumers saturated a
112-core MySQL (107 cores, load 500). Scoping the join to the document's own
mentions (an index-ordered path) is semantically equivalent---an entity
projects through documents that mention it---and took projection to
seconds, database to 29 cores.
\item \textbf{B91/B93 --- link-insertion deadlocks.} Two deadlock classes in
link insertion: unordered INSERT-SELECT acquiring gap locks in interleaved
order (fixed by pinning insertion order to \texttt{mention\_id}), and
per-action round-trip inserts (fixed by folding all actions into a single
UNION-ALL derived-table INSERT-SELECT). Deadlock error 1213 went to zero.
\item \textbf{B94 --- the $O(N^2)$ the new lexicon amplified.} Alias
canonicalization checked every acronym pair quadratic in alias count---95
of 101 seconds per links task, burning the GIL while 16 threads queued.
Caching canonical forms by schema signature (lexicon version + alias count)
took it to 0.05\,s. Net link throughput: \textbf{8/min $\to$ 1{,}100/min
(137$\times$)}---the collapse-era lexicon was so small it hid this bug for
months; the \emph{repair} exposed it in an afternoon. The 137$\times$ is a
window-level aggregate over concurrent fixes (the deadlock repairs land
in the same window); the cache's isolated contribution is measured at
task level---95 of 101 seconds per links task---and the cache key
(schema version $+$ alias count) can in principle alias two same-size,
same-version, different-content lexicon edits; version-pinned bumps
(v2.0$\to$v2.1) are the mitigation.
\end{itemize}

The last point deserves emphasis in prose, not just as a bullet: the
collapsed lexicon (575 de-facto terms) masked an $O(N^2)$ hot path because
$N$ was tiny. Correcting the lexicon did not merely improve extraction---it
changed the computational regime of the pipeline and surfaced latent
engineering debt. Lexicon quality is load-bearing in the most literal sense.
